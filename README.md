# Living City

A Combine-occupied city for Garry's Mod: ambient citizens and Civil Protection running real routines, plus factions and ranks, radiant quests with dice, personal story chains, twelve Union jobs, hunger and thirst, social credit, a Commissary, contraband and a black market, stop-and-search, disguises and forged papers, radios, prisons, curfews, and an in-game editor for all of it. Its own gamemode, for singleplayer and servers.

## Install and first run

1. Unzip `livingcity.zip` into `garrysmod/addons/` so you have `garrysmod/addons/livingcity/gamemodes/livingcity/...` (and `.../lua/weapons/gmod_tool/stools/`, the editor). Everything else is the gamemode's: none of it loads in any other gamemode.
   `garrysmod/addons/livingcity/` is the one official place for the gamemode: keep no other copy (an old `addons/livingcity-rp-main`, say) beside it, or both load. Your own recordings and songs go in the server's **`garrysmod/sound/livingcity/`**, the official place for them: `overwatch/`, `administrator/`, `radio/` and `endings/` (create the folders). They're outside the gamemode's folder, so updating it (deleting `addons/livingcity` and unzipping the new one) never touches them, and FastDL mirrors them to players: press the host's **FastDL sync** after adding one, then change the map.
2. In the main menu, click the gamemode button in the bottom right corner and pick **Living City**. Start a game on a city map.
3. The city starts by itself. You're a citizen. Press **F1** for your quick menu (nine things on the number keys), **C** for the full menu (the guide is the first thing in it), **F4** to pick a faction.
4. To build the city as an admin, type `!build`. Build mode gives you the spawn menu, the physgun, the toolgun and noclip. Then go to Q menu > Tools > **Living City > Living City Editor**. Type `!build` again to go back to playing.

### Permanent death, and a slower pace

**Close calls first.** Each person gets `lc_close_calls` (3) deaths that aren't the end: you wake in the infirmary, what was in your pockets is gone, you're still recovering for `lc_close_call_recover` minutes (10: 20 less health), and you don't remember the fight or who started it (play it that way: no going back for revenge). A used close call comes back after `lc_close_call_regen` hours (3) of that person's life; `!characters` shows how many are left. Some deaths are the end whatever's left: a stalker's, and stepping into the energizer. You can also **retire** the person you're playing (`!characters`, Retire them). Close calls are the person's, so another character has their own.

**Careers for life.** Once conditioned for the Overwatch, or made a stalker, that person can't take up another faction; the way to be someone else is another character.

**What passes on.** Credits, XP and everything else belong to the person, never to the player. When a person ends, the next inherits `lc_inherit_pct` (25%) of their savings, up to `lc_inherit_max` (200). Hoarding is taxed: every `lc_levy_minutes` (30) the Union takes `lc_levy_pct` (10%) of whatever anyone holds above `lc_levy_threshold` (2000; 0 turns it off). The player keeps only a record, their **legacy**: how many lives, the longest, and how the last few ended, shown at the top of `!characters`.

**When a person ends, they're gone.** You come back as someone new: a new name and citizen number (shown on name tags and in chat), no credits, no Civil Standing, no job, empty pockets and stash, no rank in any faction, no story, and a housing block of your own. Infested players come back as themselves, admins in build mode are exempt (you're told so in chat when it happens, so type `!build` to leave build mode and play for real), and dying in custody doesn't end or shorten a sentence. Conduct strikes belong to the real player and stay. `lc_permadeath 0` turns it off; `lc_newcharacter <name>` wipes someone by hand.

**The end of a life.** When you die the screen goes dark and a card says who that person was: their name and citizen number, how long they lived in the city, how many people knew their name, and what killed them, as they saw it ("Shot by a masked Civil Protection unit.", "A fall.", "Starved."). Put down first and finished off later, it's whoever put them down. The card stays up for `lc_respawn_secs` (12) before you can click to go on, and your eyes open on the city again. A death that doesn't end the person (build mode, custody, the Infested, permanent death off) gets a short card that says why. **Word gets around:** everyone who knew their name hears it a minute or two later, in passing ("[Word gets around] You hear that Ada Grey died. They say it was the Combine."), with what people would say did it: the Combine, the Resistance (only if the killer looked the part; a rebel passing as a citizen is just somebody), the infestation, a fall, hunger. Whoever did it isn't told. Someone who's away, or playing another of their characters, hears when they're next in the city as the person who knew them (for up to 30 days). Time lived is counted while you're alive and kept with each character; people from before this was counted show "at least". `lc_word_gets_around 0` keeps deaths quiet. Who knows each citizen number is kept in `garrysmod/data/livingcity/known.json`, the news still to be passed on in `word.json`.

**Movement is slower and grounded**, so it feels like a person walking through a city: walk 110, sprint 190 (`lc_walk_speed`, `lc_run_speed`), a normal Half-Life 2 jump (`lc_jump_power`, 200) with a pause before the next one (`lc_jump_delay`), no jumping from a crouch, no ducking in the air, and no gaining speed in the air. **You can climb:** jump at anything up to about chest height (a crate, a platform edge, out of a track bed) and you pull yourself up onto it. If you still end up somewhere you can't get out of, type `!stuck` (`lc_stuck_cooldown`, 5 minutes, not while wanted or in custody) to be put back on the street. `lc_slow_movement 0` brings the Half-Life 2 feel back.

**How it looks: a city too big for you.** The view is never wider than `lc_fov` (75, Half-Life 2's own; plenty of players run 90 or 100, which shrinks everything on screen), and your eyes sit at `lc_eye_height` (61 units; GMod's is 64), a little lower than everyone else's, so people and units stand a touch over you. The camera sways faintly in step with your feet (a grown-up's stride: running is about three steps a second, not a scamper) and dips when you land, harder the further you fell (`lc_view_bob`; `lc_view_bob_amount`, 100, sets how strong, in percent; any player can keep theirs still with `!bob`, or `lc_my_view_bob 0`). Every camera effect (holding a pose, flying a scanner, Breen's water) runs through one camera, so they all keep the field of view. **The quiet HUD** (`lc_quiet_hud`, on) keeps score off the screen: no XP bar or "+15 XP", no credits or standing (they're at the top of your pockets, F2; rank and progress on F4), no kill feed in the corner, and the hunger and thirst bars only once they start to bite (under half), or for a few seconds after you eat or drink. Editor panel: **How it feels**. The visual style (models, materials, lighting) is up to the map and content packs you run.

**Choosing who you are.** The first time you join, and again each time you start over after dying, a window asks for a **name** and a **face** (one of the citizen models, shown turning in a preview: Half-Life 2's own and any installed citizen pack, found by itself; a Workshop pack is sent to joining players, and a model nobody's computer can load is never offered, so no ERROR models), then, under the picture, that model's **face skins** and its **clothes and accessories** (bodygroups: glasses, caps, coats...), one at a time with < and >. What you choose stays with that person. An admin can narrow what's offered in **Job outfits > New characters choose from** (only the models, faces and pieces ticked there). It reminds you to pick a realistic name for a person in this city and era, an ordinary name, with no celebrities, characters from games, films or shows, memes or joke names. The server enforces it: one to three words, 3 to 26 characters, letters only, no names on the blocklist (public figures, fictional characters, memes, slurs and profanity), and nobody else in the city can share your name. "Keep what I was given (which ignores anything you typed or picked: only This is me applies the name in the box and the face you chose)" accepts the random ones. You can change them whenever you like with `!character`. Your face carries over to whatever you become: the same face in the Resistance's clothes, say. Staff: `lc_setname <player> <name>`, `lc_creator <player>`. In a city with Civil Intake (below) the window is your registration form instead, filled in at the desk.

### Civil Intake: arriving with nothing

**On a map with a Civil Intake desk, a new person arrives with nothing and has to ask for each thing.** That's the first time you join, every time you start over after a permanent death, and a new character slot. You step into the city at an **arrival point** (editor: "Point: arrival"; failing that, the train platform), and a line at the bottom of your screen, with a marker over the terminal, says what's next and how far.

1. **Civil Intake** (editor: "Fixture: Civil Intake (registration)"). Press E and your registration form opens: your name (typed in by you, not handed out; **Suggest a name** offers an ordinary one if you're stuck), your face, and a description for the register, how a stranger would know you (8 to 120 characters; it's what people who don't know you see). There's no "keep what I was given". Submit it to the terminal and it processes you: `lc_intake_secs` (8) seconds with a bar on screen. **Stay beside it**: walk away and it stops (come back and it carries on, without the form again). Then you're on the register, with a citizen number, and your **identity papers** and **ration card** are in your pockets (F2). The screen marks the way to Housing Allocation next.
2. **Housing Allocation** (editor: "Fixture: Housing Allocation"). Press E and the terminal's **housing register** opens: every block, how many people live there, and how far it is. Present your papers (you need them on you) and apply for a block, or "any block". It searches the register for as long again, then gives you a room and marks the way there. **Any block** is approved at once (the least crowded). A **crowded block** (two or more above the least crowded) is only granted to someone with Civil Standing 50 or more; otherwise you're put in the least crowded one and told why. On a map without this desk, a room comes with your papers.

**Housed already?** The same screen shows where you live and **how you got it** (allocated here, transferred, assigned when you first arrived, came with your papers, or set by staff), marks the way home, and takes **transfer requests** to another block: Civil Standing 25 or more, once every half hour, and a crowded block needs 50 again.

**Your papers are things you carry.** Everyone on the register has identity papers and a ration card in their pockets (people who were here before are handed them the next time they spawn). They weigh nothing, take no room, and can't be dropped or handed over (you can stash them at home, at your own risk). **Show your papers** to whoever you're looking at (quick menu: Show them my papers, or F2 > Identity papers > Show): a card pops up on their screen with your name, number and block, and they learn your name. A unit who stops you asks for them: carrying none costs a little standing. The ration dispensers want the card (a forged one still works). Lost them? **Civil Intake replaces them**: E at the desk shows your record and what's missing, `lc_papers_fee` (10) credits for papers and `lc_card_fee` (15) for a card.

**One person at a desk at a time:** anyone else gets "Processing another citizen. Wait your turn." New arrivals who come in on the train queue at each desk too, in a line straight out from the screen, and each is processed in turn before they head home (a unit pressing E at a desk sees how many are waiting).

**Until you're registered:** the ration dispensers have no card for you (a forged ration card from the black market still works), the Union won't employ you, and the Commissary wants a number you haven't got. A unit who stops you sends you to Civil Intake; after `lc_intake_grace` (10) minutes it's **Failure to register** and you're wanted. Someone arrested before they register is processed in custody. **Until you have a room,** a curfew finds you waiting it out near the desks or where you arrived: units leave new arrivals there alone. The quick menu's **My papers** says where you stand.

Everyone who was in the city before Civil Intake counts as registered and housed. **A map with no Civil Intake desk works as before** (people are registered and handed a room as they arrive), take the desks away and anyone still waiting is registered, and `lc_intake 0` turns it all off. Editor panel: **Civil Intake**. Staff: `lc_register <name>` (on the register now), `lc_unregister <name>` (back through all of it: no papers, no room, register again). Put a checkpoint point beside the desks and a unit will stand there, checking papers.

### What now? Never lost

**Clothes that show the job.** The editor's panel has **Job outfits** (or `!outfits`, or `lc_outfits_edit`, admin; the button used to do nothing, its command having the same name as the on/off setting): pick a job, then a model from whatever's installed (the stock citizens and medics, the hostages, a Workshop pack such as the Black Mesa citizens), tick the skins and bodygroup pieces that fit in the live preview (shirts, overalls, caps, glasses; leave out anything that doesn't), add the look, save. A pack's battle gear (a soldier's helmet, a welding mask: any piece named for a helmet or welding) is only worn by the Resistance in the open: a rebel fighter, or a rebel player not passing as a citizen; on anyone else it's left off. A job can have several looks. The city's people with that job wear one, and so does a citizen player who holds it; each person gets their own pick and keeps it, and a look made from Half-Life 2's citizens can keep each person's own face. **Everyone else (no job)** gives the unemployed glasses, beanies or caps the same way. The Clinic Medic starts out in Half-Life 2's own medic clothes. Saved for every map in `garrysmod/data/livingcity/outfits.json`; `lc_outfits 0` turns it off. Cheap variety on top, nothing to download: each of the city's people is a slightly different height (`lc_people_heights`), and each citizen player's shirt has its own drab colour (`lc_citizen_tint`).

**Ways to get by** (F1 > What now? > Ways to get by, or `!ways`): everything you could do right now, one plain line each, ready ones first, the rest saying the one thing missing ("Needs Civil Standing 50, you have 12"), with a button that marks the way there or opens the screen it happens on: eating, bins, a Union job, a market stall, renting an apartment, salvage, fishing, notes and posters, joining Civil Protection or the Resistance; a unit's list is a unit's, a rebel's a rebel's.

**With a rank** (the Resistance, Civil Protection, the administration: anyone not a plain citizen), the corner shows who you are instead of the nudges: faction and rank, credits, Civil Standing, your Union job, and WANTED when you are; only something urgent (hungry, thirsty, down) still takes its place. Everyone always has **one next step**, worked out from where they stand (a plain citizen sees it in the corner): register at Civil Intake, ask Housing Allocation for a room, find your block, eat, drink, get a Union job, work a shift. Each step says why, and **F1 > 1 (What now?)** shows you the way (a waypoint). A new life sees the step as a quiet line under the status box for its first hour (`lc_whatnow_minutes`, 60), and anyone sees it when it's urgent (hungry, thirsty, nowhere to live). After that it's on F1 whenever you want it, or always on screen (C > Settings > Next step on screen; `lc_whatnow_show` 0 never, 1 new life and urgent, 2 always).

**Hungry or thirsty?** The step names the nearest way to eat or drink right now, in order: what's in your pockets, your ration if it's due, food you can afford at the Commissary, bins to search (and how long until your next ration). Running low puts a card on screen once, with the way. **A new life's first ration allocation is never turned away by a supply shortfall.**

**Places:** F1 > What now? > Places (and C > Places in the city) lists everywhere worth knowing, each with a waypoint: the ration dispenser, water, bins, your block, your workplace, the Commissary, Civil Intake and Housing Allocation (units: their Commissary; the Resistance: the lab). `lc_whatnow 0` turns all of it off.

### The quick menu (F1)

**F1 opens the quick menu**, like a voice menu in a team shooter: a short list at the left of the screen, picked with the number keys (1 to 9; 0 goes back or closes), and you keep moving while it's up. Nine things, for who you are:

| You are | 1 to 5 | 6 to 8 (nothing in front of you) |
|---|---|---|
| Civil Protection (the Overwatch too) | Call for backup, Send my 10-20, Issue a BOLO, **Voice lines >** (the mask's most used lines; the Overwatch ranks' own), Kick | Unit tools, My directives (F3), Sociostability > (officers) or My pockets |
| Citizen | **What now? >** (your next step, Places), Hands up, My papers, **Talk >** (and Tell everyone nearby my name), My pockets | **Pose >**, **Gesture >**, My board (F3) |
| Resistance | **What now? >**, Hands up, Gear up or Plain clothes, **Talk >**, Black market | **Resistance >** (find the lab, donate food, decode a chip), **Pose >**, My board (F3) |

**6 to 8 change with what's in front of you and your own trouble**, shown in green: a person (units: search, cuff or uncuff and drag, escort, take a microphone; everyone else: tell them your name, show them your papers, inform, a work order), someone who's down, a body, a door, a forcefield, a transformer, a power bank; and you being downed, tied, wanted, interrogated or hauling. **9 is everything else**: the full menu. A ">" opens a page of its own. Bind a key to `lc_quick` to open it from something else.

### Everything is in the full menu (C)

**You never need a chat command.** C (outside build mode), or 9 on the quick menu, opens a menu of everything you can do right now, for who you are and what you're looking at. At the top: the guide, your pockets (F2), your board (F3), factions (F4), **Tips I've seen**, and for units **Unit tools**. Then whoever or whatever is in front of you (a person by the name you know them by, or what they look like: recognize, inform, cuff, search, escort, give them a key; someone who's down; a body; a door; a machine), and:

- **Me:** hands up, holster, work at the cuffs, give yourself up, give up (when you're down), poses and gestures, your papers, your credits and standing, your characters, your name and face, how strangers see you, I'm stuck.
- **Talk:** tell everyone nearby your name, yell, whisper, describe what you do or the scene, roll the dice, out of character (nearby or everyone), your radio and the Union band. (These are the chat prefixes, typed for you.)
- **Getting by:** your stash, the Commissary, the black market, writing, fishing, salvage, a Civil Protection request, escorts, becoming a foreman.
- **Home:** your housing block, the way home (marked on your screen), the way to Civil Intake or Housing Allocation (while you need them), your apartment, locking it (looking at its door), taking a key back, giving it up.
- **The city:** the time and weather, who holds the city, the laws, and for the Administrator a broadcast.
- **Civil Protection** (units) or **Resistance** (rebels): their tools.
- **Settings:** camera sway, third person while posing, warnings and notices on screen, tips off or on or from the start.
- **Help:** ask staff for help (a ticket); for staff, the staff panel, the admin testing tools, build mode and path mapping.

Every chat command a player can type has a place in it (the client smoke test checks each one, and that every quick menu has nine working items), and the tips and the guide point to the menus, not to the command. The commands still work, as shortcuts.

### On the screen, not just in chat

Chat is busy, so what matters goes on the screen too (and stays in chat to scroll back through). **Someone talking to you** (a rebel guarding the supplies, the quartermaster, the scientist, an interrogator) shows as a subtitle low in the middle; when a window is open there (the quartermaster's, the lab's), just below it instead (or above, with no room below), never hidden behind it. **Warnings** (Civil Protection, Overwatch, conduct strikes, custody, a disguise slipping) show as a card at the top, edged in red; **notices** (Civil Intake, Housing Allocation, your papers, rations, the city) edged in blue; and **the city's announcements** in amber, with what they mean for you. The same line again bumps a counter instead of stacking up. **Waypoints:** asking the way somewhere (the Civil Protection Commissary, Housing Allocation, home, the lab) marks it on your screen with how far it is, at the edge of the screen when it's behind you, gone when you get there (home: once you're inside your block). Turn the cards off for yourself in C > Settings (`lc_notices 0`).

### The gamemode, and what's left of Sandbox

Living City is built on Sandbox only so admins keep the building tools. Regular players can't spawn props, NPCs, weapons or vehicles. They also can't noclip, use the physgun or toolgun, or open the spawn and context menus. Factions and ranks are always on. Falls hurt the way they did in Half-Life 2, and the death card stays up for a few seconds before you can go on (`lc_respawn_secs`, 12). Admins outside build mode play by the same rules as everyone else.

| Key | Opens |
|---|---|
| F1 | The quick menu: nine things on the number keys, for who you are and what's in front of you (9 is the full menu). F1 works whatever it's bound to; F1 again closes it, the full menu or the guide |
| C (outside build mode) | The full menu: everything you can do right now, plus **Guide / manual**, your pockets, your board, factions and tips at the top |
| `!help` or `lc_help` | The guide on its own: topics down the side and a search box (and the admin commands, if you're an admin) |
| F2 or `!inv` | Your pockets (and your stash, when you're home) |
| F3 or `!jobs` | Your board: jobs, story chains, and your Union employment |
| F4 or `!faction` | Factions |
| `!shop` | Union Commissary (or use a commissary terminal) |
| `!market` | Black market (at a dealer, or in rebel territory) |
| `!r <msg>` | Talk on your transmitter's frequency (the CP net for units; Freq 7 with a Freq 7 radio and a radio transmitter). Nobody talks on the Union band. |
| `!search` | Units: search the person you're looking at |
| `!stash` | Your hidden stash at home (and the weapons in your hands go in it too: "Stash it") |
| `!block`, `!credits`, `!report <msg>`, `!abandon` | Housing, money and standing, staff reports, drop a job |
| C > **Tips I've seen** | Every tip card you've had, to read again at your own pace (each card says so as it shows) |
| `!holster`, or R with your hands down | Put away what you're holding (nothing in view, nothing to swing), or raise your fists / draw it again. You spawn with your hands down. Bind a key: `bind h lc_holster` |

## Building a map with the editor

Left click places or tags, right click removes, R cycles modes, Z undoes. The list of things to place can be arranged **by type** (points, fixtures, zones, doors) or **by what it's for** (citizens' daily life, each Union job with its workplace and what goes with it, Civil Protection, the prison, the Resistance and rebel bases, infestation, exercise, power and the street, paths and doors): building a rebel base, its zone, lab, bunks, lookouts and the rest are in one folder. "Show the editor's markers" (untick to hide them all) and "See markers through walls" (untick: only what's in sight) keep a busy map readable. A ration line's front is the end nearest its dispenser, whichever end was placed first. Holding the tool shows the points, paths, zones and doors near you. Text labels appear only for what's close, a zone's name only while you're standing in it, and an NPC's state and destination only for the one you're looking at (tick "Show every nearby NPC" in the panel for the old everything-at-once view).

- **Zones** are shaded on the ground in their color. The zone you're aiming at fills in strongly with a bold edge, and the others dim. Its name and radius show under your crosshair. Aim at a zone's center marker to pick it out from overlapping zones; that's also the one a right click removes. While you're placing a zone, its size is shaded where it will go. Points and path nodes you're aiming at are highlighted the same way. Untick **Shade zones on the ground** in the panel to only shade the one you're aiming at.

### Saving

Every change saves straight away to `garrysmod/data/livingcity/<map>.json`, **settings included**: every slider and checkbox in the editor panel (population, territory, events, all of it), and any `lc_` setting changed from the console or rcon, is kept with that map's layout and put back when the map loads, so a server restart doesn't reset them (a dedicated server doesn't keep them by itself). There's no save button to forget, and the layout loads whenever that map loads, whichever gamemode you're in. Before each save, the previous version is backed up to `garrysmod/data/livingcity/backups/<map>/` (at most every 5 minutes, newest 30 kept). Backups can also be made by hand.

| Command | Does |
|---|---|
| `lc_backup` | Back up the layout now |
| `lc_backups` | List the backups, newest first |
| `lc_restore <number>` | Bring one back (what's there now is backed up first) |

If the layout file is ever damaged, it's copied aside as `<map>_unreadable_<time>.txt` before anything can overwrite it.

**Moving to a server:** install it on the server, and copy `garrysmod/data/livingcity/<map>.json` into the server's `garrysmod/data/livingcity/` folder.

### Placing things

**The editor's panel** lists everything you can place in sections that fold away (points, fixtures, street furniture, workplaces, paths and ladders, zones, doors, tools), each line with how many are on this map; the section headings show their totals. Click a line to use it. **Find** marks every one of that thing on your screen, through walls, numbered, with how far away it is (handy for "where did I put the other dealer?"); **Go** takes you to the next one (in noclip). At the top: the layout at a glance (points, fixtures, zones, tagged doors, path nodes, city NPCs about) and what the city would like that the map hasn't got yet.

**Custom announcements (Overwatch and the Administrator).** Make your own recordings and drop them in; the city plays them when things happen. No code and no list to edit: **the file's name says when.** They live in the server's own **`garrysmod/sound/livingcity/`** (the official place), outside the gamemode's folder, so updating the gamemode never touches your recordings, in three flat folders beside `endings/`: `overwatch/` (the Combine PA), `administrator/` (the city's Administrator over the loudspeakers) and `radio/`. (The older places, `sound/livingcity/announce/<voice>/`, in the old `livingcity_sound` addon or not, are still read.) Drop the files straight in: a file's name starts with its trigger (`curfew1.wav`, `ambient2.wav`; the longest trigger that fits wins, so `curfew_soon1.wav` is the warning), and for the Administrator `firm`, `stern` or `furious` anywhere in the name is that tone (`curfew_firm1.wav`; no tone word is her warm one). `livingcity_sound/sound/livingcity/announce/<voice>/README.txt` in the project lists each voice's triggers and when they play, and `livingcity_sound/ANNOUNCEMENT_SCRIPTS.md` has every draft line with a file name for it. **The format matters: the file's extension must match what it really is.** An MP3 renamed to `.wav` is silent for everyone (rename it back to `.mp3`); a `.wav` must be 16-bit at 44100, 22050 or 11025 Hz (48000 Hz, 24-bit or 32-bit float WAVs are silent), and an announcement `.mp3` should be 44100 Hz too. Ending songs are played by the game's music player, which takes an `.mp3` or `.ogg` at any rate; `lc_endings` just points out a low-quality copy (under 32000 Hz) that will sound muffled. MP3s are the easy choice, and much smaller for players to download. `lc_announce_clips` lists every file the game can't play and how to fix it, and a superadmin is told on joining when there are any. `.wav`, `.mp3` or `.ogg`, as many as you like: one is picked each time, never the same one twice running. A `.txt` with the same name as a sound (`curfew1.wav` and `curfew1.txt`) is its subtitle, printed in chat as it plays. Triggers: the events (`rations`, `sweep`, `curfew`, `lockdown`, `raid`, `supply`, `ended` for a curfew or lockdown being lifted, and the morning exercise: `jog`, `drill`, `parade`), the two-minute warnings for the routine ones (`rations_soon`, `curfew_soon`; sweeps, raids and supply convoys are surprises and lockdowns answer violence, so they have none), `socio_green`, `socio_yellow`, `socio_red`, `dawn`, `day`, `dusk`, `night`, `outbreak`, `blackout`, `power_restored`, `skirmish_raid`, `skirmish_sweep`, `response`, `breakout`, `overwatch_drop`, `block_raided`, `territory_grew`, `train_arrival`, `welcome` (only for someone who still has to register at Civil Intake, when they join or a new character arrives; never for anyone already registered. Only they hear it, whatever else is playing, the Administrator first and Overwatch straight after her; the city's other announcements skip them until it's done, and wait for nobody) and `ambient` (general announcements, and what the Administrator's periodic addresses are made of when `lc_breen_broadcasts` is 1). A custom Overwatch line replaces the built-in one for an event or a sociostability change (`lc_announce_custom_pct`, 100, is how often); for everything else it's new. The Administrator follows Overwatch once he has finished (never over him) over the loudspeakers (each player's `lc_breencast_volume`). **Her tone escalates.** She is a kindly parent with a dash of the cheerful host, and the worse things get, the more domineering she becomes. Her tone is in the file name, as above (older layouts, a folder per trigger or per tone, still work). Each trigger has a usual tone (rations and the time of day warm, a curfew firm, a lockdown or raid stern, a breakout furious); it goes up a step while the city is on red sociostability, and up again after a run of violence (three raids, skirmishes, breakouts, outbreaks or rebel gains in ten minutes is a step, six is two). If the tone she wants has no recordings she uses the nearest lower one, then the nearest higher. Overwatch has no tones: always cold. Her name is `lc_administrator_name` ("The Administrator"), her studio model `lc_administrator_model` (Dr. Mossman), and Dr. Breen's own stock Breencasts are only used with `lc_administrator_stock_lines 1`. **Every player's own computer plays these, so each one needs the files:** on a dedicated server they're sent on joining with `resource.AddFile`, through FastDL (`sv_downloadurl`), which mirrors the server's `garrysmod/` (so `garrysmod/sound/livingcity/`, never an addon's folder). A player missing a file gets a "Sound missing" notice the first time, and `lc_sound_check` (any player, in the console) counts what their computer has, opens every ending song on it to say whether the game can play it (and how big it is: 0 KB is a download that never finished), lists the stock Half-Life 2 Overwatch lines the city uses and whether this install has each one (a missing loudspeaker version, `..._spkr.wav`, falls back to the plain one if that's there), and test-plays one of the server's own announcements. A song added to the server only reaches players after a FastDL sync, a map change, and their reconnecting. A stock Half-Life 2 line an install doesn't have is skipped quietly (the banner and the words still show). **The radios and the repair bench** use `radio/`, the same way: `static1.wav`, `union1.wav`, `freq7_1.wav` (see `radio/README.txt`). `lc_announce_clips` lists what it found and points out any file that never plays (a typo in the trigger, a retired `_soon` warning, a tone word in Overwatch's folder), `lc_announce_rescan` picks up files added while it runs, `lc_announce_say <overwatch|administrator> <trigger> [warm|firm|stern|furious]` plays one now and `lc_announce_tier <trigger>` says what tone she would use right now (superadmin), `lc_announce_custom 0` turns it all off, `lc_announce_gap` (25 s) and `lc_announce_cat_gap` (90 s) space them out. **One voice for each thing:** whatever happens is announced by Overwatch or by the Administrator, picked at random (`lc_announce_admin_share`, 50: how often it's her), never both; and every voice (Half-Life 2's Overwatch, your Overwatch recordings, the Administrator) waits its turn on each player's game, so two never play at once (how long a line runs is read from the file's own header, and each player's game takes the longer of that and its own reckoning, with a moment's gap after: a length guessed short was a voice talking over the next). The minor ones (the time of day, a train in, a skirmish, the power back) are left out straight after another announcement (`lc_announce_quiet`, 60 seconds), and no "new day" is said while a curfew or lockdown holds everyone indoors.

**Path mapping** (editor panel, "Paths and doors": *Path mapping*, or `lc_pathmap` / `!pathmap`; superadmins and the listen-server host only): turn it on and **walk the places NPCs should be able to go**. A path node is laid every `lc_pathmap_spacing` units (130) of walking, linked to the one before it (only if a person could really walk between them) and to any other node within about 230 units you could walk to, so loops close by themselves; walking over an existing node joins it instead of stacking another. A green line at the top of the screen says it's on. It pauses in **noclip**, in the air, on a ladder or in a vehicle, and a jump of more than 400 units (a teleport) starts a fresh chain rather than drawing a link across it. Everything is saved to the map's layout file a few seconds after you stop (and the moment you turn it off), shows in the editor overlay, and can still be deleted or linked by hand. `lc_pathmap_undo` removes what this session added. These are the same path nodes NPC routing already uses first, so a patch you've walked is a patch pursuing units can path through.

- **Points:** loiter, sit, bench, lean, ration line slots (front first; the person at the front walks up to the line's dispenser for their ration, then steps away), patrol beats (in order), checkpoints, spawn/exit doorways (citizens), prison cells, release spot, **loading docks** (supply trucks and crates for the kitchen and factory shifts), **graffiti walls** (sanitation scrubs them), **rubble piles** (reclamation crews and citizens' work orders clear them), **leaky pipes** (citizens' work orders patch them), **broadcast studio** (where the Administrator speaks, filmed live, when no player is Director of Propaganda), **train platform** (where trains stop), **arrival** (where new people step into the city before Civil Intake; place it from the way they should face), **infestation spawns** (zombies and infested players) and **resistance spawns** (hunted players). Place the three kinds of spawn far apart so citizens don't walk into the infestation. Citizens only ever appear at their spawn doorways (or, on a map with none, at clean spots), never on Combine metal or glass, in a Combine-only zone, or near an infestation spawn.
- **Fixtures:** water vending machine, ration dispenser, commissary terminal, **bed**, **toilet** and **bathtub** (for maps without enough of their own: the map's own beds, mattresses, toilets, tubs and showers all work too; see Toilets and Sleep below), **Civil Intake** and **Housing Allocation** (new arrivals register and ask for a room; see Civil Intake above), stove, burning barrel, black market dealer, **Combine console** (put one in a cell: see Flying a scanner below), **Resistance lab** (a lab bench and a scientist who makes the Resistance's guns: one in every rebel base; see The Resistance lab below), **Civil Protection Commissary** (units are issued their supplies there; see Civil Protection's tools), **Union reclamation point** (hand in scrap for credits), **incinerator** (where the dead are hauled; see Mortician under Union jobs; nothing ever spawns one by itself), **transformer** and **Combine forcefield** (the power grid, below), **Combine power bank**, **Combine turret** and **Combine barricades** (low, medium, tall: checkpoints, below). **Bin points** and **salvage piles** are points, not props: they make a spot searchable, or salvageable with a crowbar, on maps short of bins and junk. They appear in the world and come back with the layout. Place them at the foot of a wall and they'll face you. **Move one with the physgun** (build mode) and it's saved where you drop it.
- **Persistent props:** build mode, spawn any prop, put it where you want it, and zap it with **Prop: make persistent** (left click). It's frozen, saved in the layout with everything else, and there every time the map loads: model, position, angle, skin, colour, material, scale and bodygroups. Left click it again to save it as it is now (after recolouring it, say); move it with the physgun and it's saved where you drop it; right click and it's no longer persistent (it stays until the map changes); the remover tool takes it out of the layout as well. One that gets broken or blown up comes back a minute later, out of sight. The editor labels them PERSISTENT PROP, and the layout browser lists them. City screens are the Combine's own wall monitors (the big one is the giant Breencast screen); TVs are an apartment's little set. Floor-standing ones sit on the ground. The ration dispenser is wall-mounted at chest height, and a **ration dispenser placed at the front of a ration line** serves that line. Vending machines drop the can out of their slot near the floor.
- **Workplaces:** the editor's "Workplaces" section has a line for each Union job (Sanitation depot, Ration plant, Clinic, Factory, Post office, Maintenance yard, Train station, Civil records office, Nexus laundry, City morgue, Reclamation yard, Radio repair shop). Put one or a few where that work is done and its shifts happen there. Workplaces placed the old way ("workplace" with a job number) still count.
- Any of these spots the map hasn't got, the shifts that use them find a street instead, as before.
- **Overwatch landing zones:** "Point: Overwatch landing zone" is where Overwatch drops reinforcements when the city is in unrest (otherwise a street). Put it under open sky: a dropship comes in over the rooftops and its soldiers jump down out of its container there.
- **Rebel lookout posts:** "Point: rebel lookout post" keeps an armed Resistance fighter on guard there (see Resistance lookouts below). Place it at the way into rebel ground, from the side the Combine would come.
- **Bunks:** "Point: bunk" is a bed a rebel sleeps on now and then (see Rebels at home below). Aim at the middle of the bed from its foot or its head, so it lies along the bed. You only need one for a bed the game can't tell is a bed: mattresses, beds, couches, chairs, benches, tables, workbenches and crates inside rebel territory (your persistent props, the zone's dressing, and the map's own) are found by themselves. The sit, bench and lean points work for rebels too, inside rebel territory.
- **Loading docks:** "Point: loading dock" is where supply drops come in (and supply trucks for the Union's shifts). Under open sky a dropship brings the supplies; under a roof, an APC.
- **Flying a scanner far from the console:** the city around the scanner stays as awake as around anyone standing there (no NPCs asleep and hopping about), and the camera looks where you look, not where the scanner's body tips when it bumps something.
- **Scanner launch spots:** "Point: scanner launch spot" is where scanners flown from a console go up (the one nearest the console). Until there is one, they go up out in the open air near the console, somewhere with sky above it, never inside the map's geometry. `lc_scanner_range` is measured from wherever it went up.
- **Ladders:** NPCs can't climb, so mark a ladder for them: "Path: ladder end" at its foot and again at its top, both with the same number. Routes then go up and down it, and an NPC who reaches one end on the way to the other is put at the other end (a step and they're there; it looks better than gliding up a ladder and sticking halfway). Usable ladders the map already has are found by themselves where the map allows it. **Ladders are also learned from players:** most maps' ladders are only a ladder texture on a wall, but when anyone climbs one, the spot they got on and the spot they got off are saved as a ladder (the same "Path: ladder end" points, numbered for you, so you can see them and remove one), and NPCs use it from then on (`lc_learn_ladders`). Someone you're escorting who sees you go up or down one goes to its foot (or top) and follows you, and so do the fighters following you. One who falls far behind (a door, a jump they couldn't make) catches up when nobody's looking.
- **Paths:** nodes and links for places the map's own navigation can't handle. Send a **test walker** to see if a route works. Where you haven't drawn any, NPCs route over the map's own AI nodegraph: the trip is planned node to node and walked as short legs (the engine is good at a few hundred units and bad at crossing a multi-level map in one go), places that aren't connected to where someone is standing are never picked, and a place people keep failing to reach is skipped for ten minutes. Someone stuck a step from where they're going is quietly moved on when nobody's looking; someone stuck far from it gives the trip up instead of popping across the map. When the engine can't walk a leg of a planned trip (a locked or CP-only door the nodegraph doesn't know about, a gate, a crowd), the walker tries it again in single node-to-node steps, and if one link still won't walk it's remembered for 15 minutes and the trip is re-planned around it: once, it counts as a long detour; twice, nobody is routed along it. The debug report's NPCs section says how often each happens (the "route repairs" line).
- **Rebel territory** ("Zone: rebel territory") is the Resistance's ground: their fighters live there, and street units don't patrol it or chase anyone into it (the editor's "Street units chase people into rebel territory" setting, `lc_rebel_pursuit`, lets them). It used to say "(no security)", meaning exactly that; no rebel zone has security of its own. Raids and Overwatch still come for it.
- **Zones** are boxes or circles (the "Zone shape" choice in the editor panel). A **box** takes two clicks, one corner of the room or area and then the opposite corner, and stands "Box zone height" tall (192 units) from the floor you clicked; it fits square rooms and streets without the gaps a circle leaves in the corners. A **circle** is one click at the radius on the slider. Every zone has `lc_zone_mercy` (96 units, about eight feet) of leeway at its edge: your housing block and the prison reach that far past the line, so a step into a corner the shape missed isn't breaking curfew or escaping; Combine-only zones start that far inside it, so brushing the edge isn't trespassing. **Rebel ground has no mercy**: its rooms are often small, and eight feet either side of a small room is more than the room. Instead, anyone within that eight feet of it sees REBEL TERRITORY AHEAD, and REBEL TERRITORY once they're in; a unit inside the line is shot at (rebels ignore anyone in build mode, so an admin can build there). Right click cancels a box you've started. Kinds: no-go, rebel territory (no security), housing blocks (numbered), prison area, infestation, and **Combine only** (the nexus, offices, armory, prison). Citizens, NPC or player, stay out: NPCs never wander or route through it, and a player gets `lc_trespass_grace` seconds (10) to leave before they're wanted for trespassing. Units, admins, people in custody and anyone in a convincing uniform may be inside. **Every Combine-only zone is garrisoned:** mostly Overwatch soldiers with a metrocop or two (metrocops on their beat walk the streets and don't wander in), spread through it, some standing post and some walking from one end of it to the other, on top of the city's own numbers (editor panel, Events: the garrison sliders; `lc_garrison`, most per zone, 6; a small zone gets fewer, about one per 15 x 15 metres of floor; `lc_garrison_cops`, out of every ten, 3; `lc_garrison_respawn`, seconds before a lost one is replaced out of sight, 90). **Near the prison they're Nova Prospekt's own:** any soldier posted in a Combine-only zone within `lc_prisonguard_range` (3000 units) of a prison zone or cell wears the prison guard's uniform (some carry shotguns); `lc_prisonguard_range 0` turns that off.
- **Doors:** CP only, **prison door**, always locked, always open, NPCs ignore, force-treat-as-door, plus a tester and inspector. **Doors beside a prison cell or release spot, or inside a prison zone, are prison doors by themselves; doors inside a Combine-only zone are CP only by themselves.** Both are physically locked, opened only by a unit's E (for a few seconds), a keycard, a bypass charge, an escorted prisoner being walked through, or a Union worker's Civil Protection escort. The difference: a **CP-only** door's lock goes dead when its district loses power, but a **prison door stays locked through any blackout**, and if anything else opens it (a button on the map, a trigger, the map's own logic) it shuts and locks itself again at once. A unit pressing one of the map's buttons gets ten seconds to open the cells that way. A prisoner can't open any door or press any button, admin or not. Prisoners keep their fists. A tag of your own overrides all that: if a cell door isn't locking, tag it **Door: prison or cell**. **Any tagged door counts as a door, whatever its shape** (a wide Combine shutter, a brush that slides; sliding `func_movelinear` doors can be tagged too), and the map's navigation is joined through it: a map's nodegraph is built with its doors shut, so the nexus behind a Combine door is often cut off from the rest of the city. A Civil Protection or prison door joins the two sides for **units only**, so cops and soldiers walk through it (opening it as they get there) and citizens don't try; an always-open or force-tagged door joins them for everyone. An **always open** door stays open: the door's own timer that would swing it shut is turned off, it's locked in the open position (E does nothing), and if anything shuts it anyway (the map's own logic) it opens again a moment later.

## The city

Citizens wander, sit, lean, work at workplaces, chat, queue at ration lines, and arrive and leave through doorways. They don't know who Gordon Freeman is. Cops walk beats, hold checkpoints, walk right up to citizens to inspect them (with the trainstation harassment animations, where the model has them), chatter on the radio, and occasionally ask you to pick up a can. Crowd a cop or keep pestering him and you get "move along", then a warning, a shove, a crack with the stick, and then you're wanted for obstructing Civil Protection. Hostile units actually come after you. Walking up to a unit to report to him (a loyalty check, say) is never treated as crowding: he acknowledges you instead of warning or shoving you. Wandering NPCs stay on their own level and within a few streets of where they are, rather than setting off across the map.

**Ration lines:** people nearby join the line and take their places in the order they arrive. The person at the front is served from the ration dispenser if one is placed at the front of the line (it plays its dispensing animation), or by an unseen clerk if not. Players can stand at the front of the line too. After a few seconds you're handed a ration, and the NPCs behind you wait their turn. The usual allocation rules apply: one per allocation, and none for anticitizens. Scanners follow citizens, change altitude now and then, and work themselves free when they snag on walkways. They never photograph units (or anyone in a convincing CP uniform). Birds that wander into a doorway are shooed out of it, and anyone opening a door scares off the birds in its way. Overwatch soldiers are optional. Zombies live in infestation zones; birds and the odd headcrab roam the streets.

**The Resistance** lives in rebel territory: a few armed fighters (`lc_rebels`, 4, spread across the rebel zones) mill about and keep watch. Resistance players are among friends there and ordinary citizens are left alone, but anyone who looks like the Combine (a unit, Overwatch, the Administrator, or someone in a convincing CP uniform) who walks in gets a shout ("Combine!") and then, after `lc_rebel_warning` seconds (2), gunfire. Their shots do `lc_rebel_damage` (0.5) of normal damage, because dying here is permanent. Shoot at one and the fighters nearby shoot back, whoever you are; the Combine doesn't care who shoots rebels, and units earn a little XP for it. Quests that send you into rebel territory (the Missing Unit's Retaliation, the Overwatch incursion) make sure there's someone home.

**Territory spreads, and can be pushed back.** The rebel and infestation zones you place are where it starts. Left alone, each slowly spreads: every `lc_rebel_spread_minutes` (20) or `lc_infest_spread_minutes` (10) minutes on average, a new patch grows out from its edge in a random direction, onto ground people walk on, away from housing blocks, the prison, Combine areas, checkpoints and ration lines. Someone moves in, and each patch brings one more rebel or zombie to the population. It stops at `lc_territory_max` patches (6) for the Resistance and `lc_infest_max` (30) for an infestation, and at `lc_territory_city_max` (45) of each kind across the whole city. Each patch of infestation brings one more of the infested, up to `lc_zombies_max` (50) alive at once. Zones of one kind that you drew touching or overlapping count as one territory: they grow, and are reported, as one place, and growth close together comes over the radio as one message. Fighting back pushes it in: every rebel or zombie killed there (by a player or a unit, not by their own side) counts, every `lc_territory_hits` kills (3) take away the outermost patch, and clearing a patch out completely takes it away at once. After a fight it doesn't grow for `lc_territory_calm` minutes (10). The zones you placed are never taken. The CP net and the Resistance radio hear about ground gained and lost. Grown patches are kept in `<map>_territory.json`, not your layout. `lc_territory` shows the state, `lc_territory_grow rebel|infest` grows one now, `lc_territory_reset` takes it all back.

**Containment pay.** A unit who puts down the infested is paid for it once the shooting stops (or the patch is contained): `lc_containment_pay` credits a kill (3, at most 60 at a time), and rounds for the weapon they used (a few per kill; `lc_containment_ammo 0` turns that off). When a patch is contained or ground retaken, the whole city hears which unit did it.

**Containment** (the CP infestation job) sends you to where the infestation has spread to, if it has. A few extra infected crawl out for it on top of the usual number; nothing replaces them once they're down. From the moment you reach it, **nothing new spawns in the area** while the job is on, so whatever is there when you arrive is all there is to clear. The job isn't done until **every infested thing in the area is down** (the tracker counts how many are left). Then that patch is contained, and nothing infested spawns or spreads near it for `lc_infest_quiet_minutes` (10). Poison zombies are rare: about one in a hundred.

**You can see it, and fight over it.** Crossing into rebel territory or the infestation puts a line on everyone's screen. Taken ground gets dressed to match (`lc_dressing`): barrels, crates, pallets, old furniture, a table with chairs round it and a burning barrel for the rebels; rubbish, bones and blood for the infestation. Pieces are spaced out (never inside each other, a wall or a doorway), small junk lies where it falls, and zones drawn overlapping get a piece or two each rather than a full set (60 pieces a kind at most, city-wide). It appears out of sight as ground is taken, and is cleared away once ground is lost. Everything stands on the zone's own floor with solid ground all round it: a rebel camp on a rooftop is dressed on the roof, never hanging off its edge, and territory only spreads where someone could walk to it: across the same floor, or up or down a stairwell, ramp or ladder an NPC can use (by the map's nodegraph), never out over the street into thin air. A patch that spreads is one floor deep, so a patch on a roof isn't also the street under it, and it's shrunk to fit the floor it's on. Patches an older version grew over thin air are dropped, and ones reaching past an edge shrunk, on the next load. **The infestation looks and sounds wrong:** thick putrid yellow stains and scorch marks all over its ground, a heavy, dark, sickly yellow haze (fog, a yellow cast over everything and motes drifting in the air) that starts faintly a few metres before its edge and thickens steadily the deeper in you go, thickest in its middle, and a geiger counter clicking faster and faster (`lc_infest_ambience 0` turns it off for you; the stains are painted by each player's own game, once, as ground is taken). CP and Overwatch get a **Retake the block** job (clear a grown rebel patch, then hold it 30 seconds and it's gone); the Resistance gets **Take ground** (hold a spot by its territory for a minute and it becomes theirs; dispatch sends units to look).

**Who holds the city.** The share of the city's walkable ground that's Combine, Resistance and infested is shown at the top of the F4 screen, and by `!city`. It changes the city (`lc_city_effects`): **unrest** (the Resistance holds `lc_unrest_share` of it, 12%, or 4+ grown patches) means more inspections and raids, rations every 1.5 times as long, a harsher PA, and a black market 15% cheaper; **full compliance** (nothing has spread) means skirmishes half as often and a black market 25% dearer. An infestation that's grown as far as it can **breaks out** (`lc_outbreaks`, at most every 30 minutes): six infected loose in the nearby streets and a lockdown. `lc_outbreak` (admin) starts one.

**One camp, however many zones.** Rebel zones you placed that touch, or nearly (`lc_rebel_merge`, 128 units apart or less), are one territory: a room that isn't a perfect box takes several zones to cover, and it's still one camp, with one quartermaster, one supply crate, one fire and one table, the scavenging runs leaving from it one at a time, and the zones after the first just a few more beds and crates. A quartermaster left over from a zone that was deleted, or a second one in the same territory, goes. **The quartermaster.** Each rebel territory you placed has one (named on your crosshair). He sells the black market's stock to the Resistance, and nothing to anyone else; if he's killed, someone takes his place five minutes later. **Fighters follow you:** a Resistance Member or above can press E on a rebel to bring them along (three at most), each with his own spot just behind you. A quartermaster or a post guard never joins a squad, and nobody stays in a squad from an earlier session: squads end when you leave or rejoin. **Following you comes first:** they never stop to chase anyone, take cover somewhere else or wander off after a unit. They run when you run, climb ladders after you (they wait at the foot while you climb, then come up the ladder after you), and one who falls far behind catches up out of sight. **They hold their fire until it's on,** so you can slip past a patrol with them: then they shoot at anything Combine that's shooting at you or at them, whatever you shoot at, units hunting the Resistance, and (on rebel ground) the usual. They shoot as they go, and stand and fire whenever they're already at your side. E again sends them home, and they go home if you die. To his own he charges what the lab does (`lc_lab_price` of the dealer's price), for everything he sells. A forged ration card costs 10. He hands out food free from the camp's supply crate, so he buys food and drink back for next to nothing (`lc_qm_food_price`, at most 2 credits; 0: he won't buy it).

**A Resistance squad** (E on fighters to bring them along) takes orders from the C menu (Resistance) or chat: **act casual** (`!casual`: guns away, plain clothes, and units take them for citizens; fired on, or the leader in a fight, they pull their guns at once), **guns out** (`!gunsout`), and **meet me there** (`!meet`: they go on ahead, acting casual, to your job's marker and wait for you). **Raids:** the Resistance's fighters plan a raid on the Combine a minute and a half ahead (`lc_raid_warning`, 90): the Resistance hear where, and "Back up the raid" goes up on their board (get there, take down two of the units they're fighting). **The other way round:** Civil Protection's sweeps into rebel territory are planned a minute and a half ahead too (`lc_sweep_warning`, 90): Civil Protection and Overwatch players hear where on their net and in a notice, and "Join the sweep" goes up on their board (get to the edge of rebel territory, take down two armed anticitizens); and the moment the Resistance hit the Combine, "Repel the attack" goes up for them (get there, take down two of the attackers). The Resistance hear nothing of a sweep until it moves in.

**A camp of your own.** A rebel zone gets as many fighters as it has room for (`lc_rebel_room`, 350: one for every 350 by 350 units of it). Nothing the city puts down there blocks a passage (it keeps `lc_walkway_clear`, 100 units, to walk by) or sits in the water; fighters and their things go on dry ground first. Sleepers lie on their mattresses, eyes shut. **Hold E** (half a second) on anything the city put in a camp (mattresses, couches, crates, the table and chairs, the fire, the supply crate, the pots) to pick it up: it goes where you look, along the floor; let go of E to set it down, R turns it. Looking down at your feet keeps it a step in front of you, and it's always set down on the floor, never in it, so it can be picked up again (a mattress, a model on its side, is lifted by how it lies). Where everything was left is kept for the map (`data/livingcity/<map>_camp.json`). **Spawning on top of someone** (put back where you left off, and an NPC standing there): you're moved to the nearest clear spot beside them, and anyone stuck inside a person for two seconds is moved out the same way, so nobody needs noclip.

**Units helping units.** `!backup` sends the three nearest NPC units running to you, armed; they go after anyone wanted, or any rebel, they find there (45-second cooldown). It also puts out a 10-20. `!10-20` (or `!mark`) marks where you are on every unit player's screen for 90 seconds; units who get there earn XP, 20 if they're quick, at least 3. Bind keys with `lc_backup_call` and `lc_1020`.

**Talk on the street.** What's happened lately (ground taken or lost, raids, skirmishes, outbreaks, arrests, deaths) gets around. Press E on a citizen and now and then they'll tell you something you haven't heard. Units mostly get nervous silence.

**Getting by.** Press E on any bin or dumpster (props, or the map's own) to go through it: scrap, rags, cleaning chemicals, empty cans and bottles, tins of food, seed packets, and now and then gun parts or a spray can. A searched bin is empty for `lc_scavenge_refill` seconds (600), a fridge for `lc_fridge_refill` (240), and it's only ever the one you're aiming at (not the fridge beside the sink); rebel territory's bins are better. Units don't go through bins. **Fishing:** a fishing line from the Commissary; stand in the canal or look at the water, Fish (F2, or `!fish`), and keep still. Raw fish wants cooking.

**Feeding the Resistance.** Every rebel zone you placed has a **supply crate** and two **pots**. The crate gains a meal every `lc_cache_trickle` minutes (5), up to `lc_cache_max` (12); a Resistance member takes one tin every four minutes, anyone can `!donate` food into it, and a unit who finds it confiscates the lot. A **Supply run** (Resistance job) steals a crate of Union food from the ration dispenser, a checkpoint or the ration line and brings six meals home. Pots take a seed packet (black market, or bins) and ripen in `lc_garden_minutes` (8) into three lots of vegetables; anyone can harvest a ripe pot, yours or not, and units uproot them.

**Graffiti.** A spray can (black market, three for 25) puts the game's own lambda-in-a-circle spray decal on a wall (each player's game paints it; `lc_tag_scale` on the client sets its size, 1 by default; if an install has no lambda spray decals, a drawn lambda stands in), for everyone to see, for `lc_tag_minutes` (60). A unit seeing you do it makes you wanted for vandalism; tags near rebel ground make it spread sooner. Units scrub them off with E for XP, loyal citizens for standing.

**Making things.** By hand, from F2, anywhere: **bandages** (2 rags) and **lockpicks** (4 scrap). That's all. Everything with more to it (ammunition, pistols, shotguns, SMGs, the revolver and its rounds, armour vests, crowbars, smoke bombs, bypass charges, jammers, a radio tuned to Freq 7) comes from **the Resistance lab** if you're Resistance, and from **the black market dealer** if you're anyone else (the dealer now stocks the finished goods). Units make nothing: they're issued it. Gun parts come off dead units, out of bins now and then, from the black market, or from a smuggler.

**The Resistance lab** (editor: "Fixture: Resistance lab"; a layout's old workbenches are labs now). One of Dr. Kleiner's own lab workstations (his monitors and kit, from HL2's `props_lab/workspace` models; a plain desk with his kit on it if those aren't there), and a scientist (Kleiner's model) beside it, the Resistance's own. **He decodes Combine data chips**, and he's the only one who does: **Decode it** at the top of his window (or use the chip from your pockets beside him); he tells you if you've only got half a chip. Press E on him: everything he makes, each with its materials and his price. **Use my materials** and he makes it for nothing; **Pay** and it's `lc_lab_price` (60%) of the dealer's price, never less than the quartermaster would pay for it (so there's no money in buying from him to sell on). He takes a few seconds to a few seconds more (stay in the lab, or he hands it all back; with full pockets it lands at your feet), one job at a time. Die while he's at it and the materials are left on his bench for the cell (paid credits died with that life). Move the bench with the physgun and he goes with it. **Only for the Resistance**: anyone else is shown the door. **Every rebel base needs one** for its guns. A unit who finds the scientist shuts the lab down for `lc_lab_shutdown` (600) seconds: he's taken away (30 credits, 15 XP and standing for the unit), the cell hears about it on Freq 7, and nothing is made there until he's back. Quick menu (Resistance) > Resistance > Find the lab marks it on your screen. Each lab has its own scientist: Dr. Kleiner, Eli Vance or Dr. Magnusson, a different one at each. **A new face.** Papers in a dead man's name don't stop a unit who knows your face: the scientist can change it (**Change it**, at the top of his window; `lc_surgery_price`, 300 credits). Twenty seconds under (stay on the table, or he stops and hands your credits back), and you wake with someone else's face, bandaged and unable to run for three minutes. Nobody's looking for that face: your wanted status, any BOLO on you and the scanners' pictures are gone, a wanted notice won't match it, and a Civil Standing below `lc_surgery_standing` (-50) comes back up to it (the records can't put your old name to the face). Get wanted again and they learn the new one. Once every `lc_surgery_minutes` (60).

**Where materials come from.**
- **Bins, dumpsters and fridges:** E to search (scrap, rags, chemicals, now and then gun parts). On a map short of real bins, the editor's "Point: searchable bin" makes any spot one: a rubbish pile, a skip, a crate.
- **Salvage:** hold a crowbar, look right at a piece of junk and press E (or `!salvage`); the message names what you're prying apart. Junk: wrecked cars, vans and trucks, engines, generators, radiators, pipes, tyres, crates, pallets, fridges, washing machines, old computers, from the map's own props or from a "Point: salvage pile" placed in the editor. Six seconds of prying (nine for a car or an engine) gets two or three things: mostly scrap, plus rags and chemicals (cars give more chemicals), and now and then gun parts. Each piece then has nothing left for `lc_salvage_refill` seconds (900). Breaking a crate open, with anything, spills something about a third of the time. **It's loud:** units within earshot get a "metal being pried apart" call with where, and the nearest Civil Protection NPC on the street walks over to look. `lc_salvage 0` turns it off. **In rebel territory, rebels guard their own things:** a rebel who sees a civilian prying at or smashing a crate (the Resistance's supply crate, garden pots, any junk) warns them twice, then shoves them back, then hits them harder, and only if they carry on after that turns on them. Resistance members are never stopped. `lc_rebel_guard 0` turns it off.
- **Downed scanners:** a city scanner shot down comes apart in the street as two or three pieces: always some scrap, sometimes chemicals or gun parts. Reason enough for the Resistance to bring them down. A scanner flown from a Combine console leaves nothing at all when it's shot down, no debris and no scrap (it just winks out in a pop of energy), so nobody can farm them and nobody risks their neck for parts that aren't there. You can tell one apart: **a scanner someone is flying has a glowing red eye** that throws a little red light around it.
- **Union work orders:** citizens' Assignments sometimes include odd jobs that pay in materials: fix a streetlight (scrap and chemicals), clear rubble (2 scrap and rags), patch a leaking pipe, crouched beside it (chemicals, rags and scrap).
- **The black market** sells gun parts, chemicals and crowbars; **dead units** drop gun parts; the **Reclamation Crew** job lets you pocket some scrap; fishing pulls up junk now and then.

**The crowbar is a weapon.** It's contraband: a search always finds one, and a unit (NPC) who sees one in your hand makes you wanted for `lc_crowbar_wanted` seconds (120), outside rebel ground. Keep it holstered on the street and draw it to work.

**Union reclamation points** (editor fixture): press E to hand in scrap metal, empty cans and bottles. Scrap pays 2 credits, cans and bottles 1, and every four things returned is a point of Civil Standing, up to `lc_reclaim_cap` credits (40) an hour. The loyal way to use junk; the Resistance would rather you kept it for ammunition.

**The Resistance's money** (besides raiding supply drops):
- **Intel.** F1 > Resistance > Write down what you see (or `!scout`), with a sheet of paper in your pockets: fifteen seconds near a checkpoint, a unit on his beat, or the Combine's own ground, where no unit sees you doing it. The notes are a report in your pockets (contraband) that only the quartermaster buys: a patrol's beat 20 credits, a checkpoint 25, the Combine's ground 35 (and a little XP). The same place again is old news for `lc_intel_minutes` (30). A unit who sees you at it takes the paper off you and notes your face (10 standing).
- **Skimming the line.** On the factory line, R while holding a good part slips it into your coat: Union machine parts, which the dealer buys for 8 and the quartermaster for 12 (on the ration plant's sealing line, a sealed ration). A unit who sees it makes you wanted, and the foreman counts what goes through: take more than one and he may find the count short at the end of the shift (it comes out of your pay, 2 a part, and your standing, 5 a part).
- **Forged ration cards.** The lab's scientist makes them five at a time (3 paper and 1 chemicals, or credits). The dealer buys them (4 each) and the dispensers take them. Every forged card that turns up (sold to the dealer, or used at a dispenser) heats up the search for the forger: at `lc_forgery_heat` (15) cards, cooling by one every `lc_forgery_cool` minutes (3), a sweep is planned on the rebel territory nearest the lab that made the last batch (Civil Protection get their "Join the sweep" job), the city hears that counterfeit cards are about, and the Resistance are warned on Freq 7.

**The arms trade.** Homemade guns and ammo are contraband items in your pockets (stop-and-search finds them) until you draw or load them. The black market buys them; the Resistance quartermaster pays `lc_quartermaster_arms_bonus` times as much (1.5). Citizens and the Resistance get **Gun run** jobs (carry a crate of guns to the quartermaster, 60 credits) and **Parts pickup** jobs (bring a smuggler's gun parts home). E on the crate breaks it open, and **the supplies** go in your pockets (contraband, two and a half kilos) the whole way.

**Grenades.** Units are issued them by rank (smoke from Metrocop, a flashbang and chlorine gas from Division Leader; the Overwatch get a second gas) and can buy more at the Commissary. Issued grenades are Combine issue: a unit throws them, keeps them or hands them to another unit, but can't sell, drop, stash or pass them to anyone else (they come back with every respawn). The **Grenades** weapon throws them (R switches type). **Smoke** hides anyone inside or behind it from units and rebels for 20 seconds. A **flashbang** whites out and deafens players who see it, and stuns NPCs for four seconds. **Chlorine gas** chokes everyone without a mask (`lc_gas_damage` per second, 6) for 18 seconds and drives NPCs out of it; units, Overwatch and anyone in a CP uniform are masked. The Resistance can make smoke bombs.

**Disguises.** Resistance members come back to life in **plain clothes** (`lc_resistance_plainclothes`), with their own face, and change with `!gearup` / `!plainclothes` (or F2). Hunted fighters in plain clothes aren't hunted, until they do something wanted, or a unit sees a gun in their hand. Clothes are loot: dead citizens leave their clothes (20%), dead units their uniform (25%), dead rebels their **Resistance gear** (35%), and bins turn up citizen clothes and Union overalls (and rebel territory's bins, Resistance gear). A stolen **CP uniform** fools units on the street (with the existing risks). **Resistance gear** works the other way: the fighters in rebel territory take you for one of them, though one standing close may ask awkward questions (a blown cover means they open fire without a warning), and units treat anyone but a real unit dressed as a fighter as one. Real Resistance members staring at a fake one see "NOT ONE OF OURS". Changing in front of a unit always gives you away. Disguises now use player models, keeping your face where the clothes allow it.

**Getting people out.** F2 > Give, or `!give <item>`, hands one of something to the person you're looking at. Passing a prisoner something with a guard watching is a crime and it's confiscated. Unseen, a prisoner with a lockpick can work their own cell door (14 seconds, looking at it). If a guard sees, they lose the pick and two more minutes go on the sentence. Combined with the existing keycards and bypass charges, and the Jailbreak job, you can now get people out from either side of the bars.

**Skirmishes**, now and then (`lc_skirmishes`, on; one every 20 to 30 minutes (`lc_skirmish_interval`, 25, give or take a fifth; a server still on the old default of 9 is moved to 25); never two at once, never during curfew or lockdown): either a few fighters slip out of rebel territory and **raid** the nearest checkpoint or patrol beat, and the units nearby draw pistols and fight them, or Civil Protection sends an armed **sweep** team into rebel territory and the fighters there fight back. The Resistance radio and the CP net both hear about it. `lc_skirmish raid`, `lc_skirmish sweep`, `lc_skirmish end` (admin) start or stop one.

**Guerrillas, not an army.** The fighters don't open up on the Combine the moment it's in sight. A unit (or a convincing uniform) has to come onto rebel ground and within about 1100 units to be shouted at, and then within 1500 (or 900 unseen) to be shot at; the city's own units on rebel ground, within 1000 if they've come for a fight, and 650 if they've only wandered in. Out of their territory they keep their heads down, because **the city's units notice an armed rebel in the street** within `lc_units_spot_rebels` units (600, in front of them and in plain sight) and open fire (dispatch hears about it): a fighter out on his own walks rather than runs, and backs off out of sight rather than walk past one.

**Rebels at home** (`lc_rebel_life`) keep busy instead of standing about: someone always walks the edge of the zone, stopping to look out, once two or three are home; others work at the lab bench, a workbench or the camp table (reaching, tinkering, cleaning a gun), go through the crates, tend the garden pots, carry a box from one crate to another, sit round the burning barrel, sit on a chair, a couch or a bench, lean on a wall, or sleep on a mattress, a bed or a bunk (more of them sleep at night). What each one's doing shows under their name. It's all put down the moment they're needed: a shout of "Combine!", a fight in the zone, a lookout's warning (on their feet, guns up, facing the trouble; the shouting wakes the sleepers), someone asking them along, or a raid.

**Scavenging runs** (`lc_rebel_errands`): now and then (`lc_rebel_errand_minutes`, 4 on average, for each rebel zone you placed with three or more fighters at home, while anyone's on the server) one or two slip out to scavenge at a bin, a junk pile or a salvage point within reach, or just somewhere in the streets. They walk, keep out of sight of units (backing off and waiting it out; three times and it's too hot out there, so home), search for a while, and carry what they found home, into the zone's supply crate: a meal or two for the Resistance. A unit who catches one in the open opens fire, and they fight their way home. `lc_rebel_errand` (admin) sends one out of the nearest rebel zone now.

**The PA speaks when something happens.** No random announcements by default (`lc_announce_interval 0`): each line is tied to what's going on. "Evasive behaviour detected" plays when a unit takes a Disturbance chase and when someone is wanted for fleeing or escaping; "capital malcompliance" when someone is wanted for violence; "citizenship revoked" for a long sentence; "failing to report is conspiracy" when a citizen reports something; "anticitizen reported" when a rebel raid hits. Voice lines queue: one finishes before the next starts, instead of talking over each other.

**Ration units deducted.** When the Resistance act (a raid on a checkpoint, the lambda on a wall, rebel ground spreading), the PA charges the people nearby (graffiti only once a unit or scanner has seen it: as it goes up, or when one comes across it later; paint nobody has found costs nobody anything) with tolerating noncompliance, and it's real: every citizen within `lc_ration_dock_radius` (1500) and within about one floor above or below it (`lc_ration_dock_height`, 150 units: someone on the street below a rooftop camp isn't docked) loses their next ration allocation. They're told on the spot, and the dispenser tells them again, with the reason, when they try to collect. The allocation after that is normal. Units, Overwatch and hunted Resistance fighters aren't docked. `lc_ration_dock 0` turns it off.

**Announcements explain themselves.** Every PA announcement also goes into chat: what the voice is saying, in plain words, and on the next line what it actually means for you right now (how long it lasts, where to go, what happens if you don't, and the unit's side of it if you're CP). Ambient lines say they're just atmosphere. Events say so in chat when they end, too.

**Events** run automatically or from the panel: rations, inspection sweeps, block raids, curfew, lockdown. **Only the routine ones are announced ahead:** rations and curfew get a two-minute warning on the PA and the Union band. A sweep, a block raid or a supply convoy is a surprise: the PA says nothing until it starts, though the units are briefed early and the Resistance's informants whisper it on Freq 7. A lockdown is never scheduled; it answers violence (the escalation reaching alert 4, or the city falling into unrest), and an admin can still call one from the panel. During curfew and lockdown your HUD points you home to your housing block, and you get a **grace period of at least 60 seconds** (`lc_curfew_grace`) to reach it, counted down on the HUD. After that, a cop who sees you outside gives three warnings before you're wanted. **If you respawn in the middle of a curfew** you get a fresh grace period of your own, long enough to walk from where you spawned to your block (never under 60 seconds, at most four minutes), and the warnings after it come 15 seconds apart instead of 8. Citizens walk home into the nearest housing block when curfew starts and go inside, instead of disappearing from the edge of the map.

**Morning exercise** (mandatory for citizens). Every second morning at dawn (`lc_exercise_every`, 2), before work, the city holds one of three, taking turns (`lc_exercise_kinds`, "jog,drill,parade"; one the map has no points for is skipped). Everyone gets `lc_exercise_muster` seconds (75) to report where their HUD points; Civil Protection, the wanted, prisoners and the Resistance are left out.
- **The jog.** Plot a route with the editor ("Point: jogging route stop", placed in order, the first is the start; give routes a number to have more than one). Citizens gather at the start and run it (`lc_jog_laps`, 1). Units post themselves along the route at intervals (`lc_jog_gap`, 600, up to `lc_jog_units`, 8). A pace marker moves round at `lc_jog_pace` (150: faster than walking, slower than sprinting), green while you're keeping up with it and red once you've fallen behind; anyone not jogging, or more than `lc_jog_behind` (450) behind the marker, gets the stick from the nearest unit, who runs over if need be (with no unit posted near, any unit who can see you steps in, and after a while out of everyone's reach the nearest of the route's units comes for you). The city's own joggers (`lc_exercise_npcs`, 10; more come out of a doorway near the start if there aren't enough about) keep up with the marker: one who falls behind or loses the way catches up out of sight. The city's joggers each keep to a lane of their own, a little to the side of the route, and set off a moment apart, so they don't jostle round one spot. Someone who becomes a citizen after it's started (joins, or a new life) is expected too and can join in on the route. **Late?** Join the route anywhere (the start too): your own pace marker starts from where you join, and the jog runs long enough for you to finish it. Each blow gives a burst of speed to catch back up (`lc_jog_boost`, 1.5 times, for `lc_jog_boost_secs`, 4). It's over when everyone's in.
- **The drill.** Place an exercise yard ("Point: exercise yard": stand where the rows of citizens will be and aim at the spot where the leader stands). Everyone gets a place in rows of five in front of the leader (a ring on the ground) and copies the leader's moves: crouch, jump, turn right around, a step left, right, forward or back, each within `lc_drill_window` seconds (2.5). The leader is a player **Clinic Medic or Records Clerk** at the yard if there is one: their own moves are called out to everyone (one every few seconds; if they stand about, the moves are called for them). Otherwise a Union fitness instructor calls them (`lc_drill_pace`, 3.5 seconds). Halfway through comes **skipping** (`lc_drill_rope`): a rope turned to a beat, jump on every one. A wrong move, a slow one, a missed beat or wandering off your place is a mistake; `lc_drill_mistakes` (3) of them and a unit at the edge of the yard steps in with the stick, then again every two more. `lc_drill_cues` (14) moves in all.
- **The parade.** Plot a parade route ("Point: parade route stop", in order, the first is where the column forms up). A column of soldiers (Civil Protection if there are none) marches it (`lc_parade_units`, 6), with units posted along the way. Citizens line the route and cheer: `/cheer` (the most), `/salute`, `/wave`, chanting in chat ("Glory to the Union!": the right words count more, yelling them more still, the same line twice less), and jumping up and down. Your enthusiasm is a bar on screen that drains while the column is near; below `lc_parade_min` (35) as it passes and a unit sees to it (after which, funnily enough, you cheer).

The stick never takes you below 15 health (`lc_exercise_beat_dmg`, 8 a blow). Units who see someone skipping it send them there, then use the stick; skipping it entirely costs 10 Civil Standing, and doing it well earns up to 10 (less if you were late). Afterwards **breakfast** goes to citizens of good standing only (`lc_exercise_breakfast`): Trusted get a meal, Exemplary a hot one; everyone else eats at the usual distribution. A few NPC citizens take part too (`lc_exercise_npcs`, 6). `lc_exercise <jog|drill|parade>` starts one now, `lc_exercise next` says what the next morning holds, `lc_exercise stop` ends it, and `lc_event jog|drill|parade` works too. `lc_morning_exercise 0` turns it all off.

**Block raids** (`lc_event raid`): a few units walk into one housing block and go through it systematically, a string of stops spread across the block. At each stop they frisk anyone there and confiscate contraband lying around: contraband items, loose weapons, TVs and radios. Whoever dropped or spawned each item is traced, and **each owner becomes wanted for contraband, for longer the more was found under their name** (five or more is a weapons cache, and gets the street units after them). Residents' hidden stashes may be found too, and owners who are offline are wanted when they next log in. `lc_raid_units`, `lc_raid_stash_chance`.

**Prison:** (there's a margin of `lc_prison_buffer` units, 150 by default, around the prison area and 250 around your own cell, so a step over the edge isn't an escape) security NPCs arrest instead of kill, CP players arrest with the Arrest Baton, escaping makes you wanted, and sentences survive death and disconnects. **Marched to the cells:** arrested by a unit NPC (beaten down, or giving yourself up to one), your hands are tied and you're walked there: the unit who took you leads, holding you by the arm, and the nearest other unit follows behind. They walk the city's paths to a free cell, unlocking the Civil Protection and prison doors on the way (the perimeter's and the cell block's: tag them **cp** or, better, **prison**, which stays locked in a blackout) and locking them behind them, and lock you in. Where the paths don't reach a cell, they walk you as close as they can get (the nearest spot on the paths outside the prison zone) and take you through the doors from there. The Resistance hears about it on Freq 7, and anyone can try to get you out: put the leading unit down and, if there's another unit about (the one behind, or one nearby), he takes your arm instead; only with nobody left to take you are you free (still tied: F1 > Work at the cuffs, or have someone cut them) and wanted for escaping custody. A unit walking a prisoner alone is soon joined by another. Doors they open on the way stay open three times as long, and don't shut while the prisoner is near them; while he's busy fighting he lets go of you, and getting far enough from him is slipping away. No walkable route, or a walk longer than `lc_march_max` (240 seconds): you're struck from behind, everything goes black, and you come to in a cell. **Cuffed only once you're subdued:** a unit who catches up with you beats you down first (`lc_subdue_hits`, 3 blows of the baton, each leaving you dazed, slow for a couple of seconds), or you're shot or hurt down under 20 health, or down, or you stand still for them (a second or more). Running past a unit isn't enough to be cuffed, and someone in noclip is never laid hands on. **Losing them:** while you're wanted the units only know where you are while the hunt is hot: a unit sees you, or one did in the last `lc_wanted_lost` seconds (30). Out of sight that long (lying low in rebel territory, slipping away), nobody knows where you've gone: step out and only a unit who actually sees you comes for you (an Overwatch squad hunting you, alert 3 and up, doesn't give up). `lc_march 0` turns it off (editor panel, Prison and conduct). CP players' Arrest Baton still sends people straight to a cell (tie them and drag them along to walk them in yourself). **No cells on the map yet?** Then an arrest happens on the street. You're cuffed and made to kneel for 20 seconds (`lc_street_detention`), searched, fined 25 credits (`lc_street_fine`), and let go with a warning. (Arrests need prison **cell points** placed with the editor; a prison zone around them is optional.)

**Don't run in front of the cops.** A metrocop who sees you sprinting within `lc_run_range` units (500) shouts "STOP RUNNING!", in chat with his name and across the middle of your screen. Stop within `lc_run_react` seconds (2) and that's the end of it. Keep running, or start running again in front of a unit within `lc_run_memory` seconds (30), and you're wanted for fleeing Civil Protection (`lc_run_wanted`, 60 seconds) with the units nearby chasing you. Walking off from a stop-and-search gets the same shout. Running means holding sprint while you move, or moving faster than halfway between your walking and sprinting speeds, so it works with the gamemode's slow pace too. Units, anyone in a convincing uniform, rebel territory and the curfew grace period (when you're meant to hurry home) are exempt. `lc_running_cops 0` turns it off. If a unit never shouts, the debug report has a "running check" line for each player: their speed, whether they count as sprinting, whether they're exempt and why, and whether a unit can see them.

**Flying a scanner (Combine consoles).** Prison is long, so put a Combine console in a cell (editor: "Fixture: Combine console"), or anywhere else. A prisoner or a unit (or an admin) presses E on it and links up with one of the city's scanners: the nearest one is taken over, or a new one goes up over the street. Your view goes with the scanner, through red lenses (the Civil Protection mask's view, in red; a masked unit's own lenses come off while they fly), and your body stays standing at the console where everyone can see it. WASD flies, Space and Ctrl go up and down, Shift is faster, the mouse looks, left click is the camera flash, R (or E, or `!unlink`) disconnects. The scanner always appears somewhere with room for it, never inside a wall, and if it gets wedged while you're trying to fly it's shaken loose. The scanner can't go further than `lc_scanner_range` (6000 units) from its console. The link drops if you're hurt, if the scanner is shot down (it takes 60 damage), if the console goes, and when your sentence is up: you come out of the scanner and walk out of the release spot as usual.

- **The flash** reaches exactly as far as a photograph (about ten feet, `lc_scanner_photo_range`), so anyone it dazzles knows they've been photographed. It blinds anyone in front of it for a moment and stuns the city's people (units too) for three seconds. A prisoner who flashes a unit gets `lc_scanner_penalty` seconds (60) more on their sentence, and is told so on the spot, with the time left.
- **Snitching.** Every flash is also a photograph, but only of what's up close: within `lc_scanner_photo_range` (128 units, about ten feet), as close as a scanner's camera works, so photos from the top of the map don't count. The pilot's view brackets the nearest suspect in front of the camera with how far it is, and says MOVE CLOSER, IN RANGE or ALREADY REPORTED (and REBEL GROUND IN FRAME when you're in it or that close to it). Rebels (hunted fighters, Father Grigori, free vortigaunts, anyone in Resistance gear), the wanted, BOLO subjects, anticitizens, anyone standing on rebel ground, zombies, and rebel ground itself all go to Civil Protection as a **scanner report**. Every unit on duty gets it in chat, and whoever it caught is **outlined in red for `lc_scanner_spotted` seconds (60), through walls**, with what they were reported for and how far away they are. Rebel ground is marked on units' screens, and the city's own units nearby go after the hunted and the wanted. The person photographed is told, and it goes on their record. The pilot earns Civil Standing for it (Grigori 15, rebel ground 15, a free vortigaunt 12, a rebel fighter or rebel NPC 12, the wanted, a BOLO or someone on rebel ground 8, an anticitizen 6, a zombie 1 or 2) but **never time off**: prison is still a punishment, you just get the option to serve the Combine while you're in. It can't be farmed: the same person can't be reported again for `lc_scanner_spot_cooldown` seconds (300, rebel ground twice that), only four zombies count per photo, and a pilot earns at most `lc_scanner_snitch_cap` (100) standing from reports every ten minutes. **Rebels shoot back.** A rebel who sees a flown scanner (in front of him, in plain sight, or right beside him) fires on it after about a second, every second or so. Their aim is poor: it gets worse with distance and with how fast you're flying, and each hit only chips it (8 of its 60), so a close charge across open ground can still get a photo before it's shot down, while coming up behind a rebel who's looking the other way gets you the picture without a shot (`lc_scanner_rebel_fire 0` turns it off). **Broken feet show in the camera:** a crippled pilot's scanner is slower and lurches and rolls with every step. **Smoke blinds the camera:** nobody standing in a smoke grenade's cloud, or behind one, can be flashed or photographed, and a scanner flying into the smoke sees nothing at all, so smoke is the Resistance's answer to a snitch overhead (the pilot's view says SMOKE IN FRAME). Units can fly scanners too; their reports go out the same way, without the standing. `lc_scanner_link 0` turns consoles off.

**Giving yourself up:** while you're wanted, walk up to a unit and press E (or type `!surrender` near one). You get half the usual sentence, the search is called off, and serving it pays back more of your Civil Standing.

**Supply drops.** A city event (now and then by itself, or `lc_event supply`): Union supplies come in at a **loading dock** (the editor's "Point: loading dock"; with none, somewhere on the streets away from rebel ground and the infestation). With open sky over it, a **dropship** flies in with a cargo container slung underneath, sets it down and climbs away, and comes back to lift it out when it's over; under a roof, an **APC** is parked there instead. Beside it: a stack of crates and the open crate of Union supplies, for `lc_supply_minutes` (6). An Overwatch soldier stands guard (`lc_supply_guards`, 1), turning to look about now and then; anyone hanging about the crates where he can see them is warned off twice ("Step away from the crates"), then wanted. Two metrocops unload it the whole time (`lc_supply_porters`, 2): each walks to the stack, picks up a box in both hands, carries it off to a spot down the street, sets it down on a growing pile and comes back; between them, and behind the soldier's back, is when to sneak in. Units are told where and get a **Guard the supply drop** directive; the Resistance get a radio tip and a **Raid the supply drop** operation (and their fighters go for it themselves half the time, `lc_rebel_react`); citizens are told to keep clear. Anyone who isn't a unit can press E on the crate to take a load (eight seconds, keep still): gun parts, ammunition, chemicals, rations, bandages, now and then a smoke grenade, sometimes two things at once. It's done quietly; but a unit who sees you at it, at any moment of the eight seconds, stops you there and then and makes you wanted. The crate holds `lc_supply_loads` loads (5). If any are left when the convoy moves on, every unit within reach is paid `lc_supply_pay` credits (40) and some XP; if it's emptied, the whole city hears that a supply drop was looted.

**Stealing the Combine's data.** Put a **Combine data terminal** (editor fixture) in the nexus or a Combine-only zone; any Combine console prop the map already has works too (the scanner console and the commissary don't). Anyone with a multitool (a Resistance Hacker, or whoever bought one) aims it at one and fires (primary fire; E works too), and stays close for `lc_heist_seconds` (30) while it downloads. Halfway through it trips the alarm: every unit gets DATA BREACH with where, it's marked on their screens, the city's units nearby turn hostile and come running, a response squad comes through the door, and units get a **Data breach** directive. Being seen at it makes you wanted. The terminal then has nothing new for `lc_heist_cooldown` minutes (20). **One terminal only holds half the picture:** what you get is a **data fragment**; download a second, different terminal's and the two fragments make a whole **Combine data chip**. The Resistance get **Steal the Combine's data** on their Operations board (two terminals, 60 XP) whenever two are ready.

**The master terminal** is the motherlode: the editor's **Combine master terminal** fixture, or a `masterinterface` prop the map already has. A download takes `lc_heist_master_seconds` (60), every alarm goes off a third of the way in (MASTER TERMINAL BREACH), and the squad that comes has two more soldiers. What comes out is a **master data core**: the dealer pays 400 for it, the lab's decoding gives 150 XP and 150 credits and marks every unit for `lc_heist_master_intel_seconds` (600, ten minutes), and plugged into a terminal it wipes the record of **every Resistance member in the city**. It's dry for `lc_heist_master_cooldown` minutes (60) after. The Resistance get **The master terminal** on their Operations board when it's ready (120 XP).

The **Combine data chip** is contraband, and has three uses:
- **Sell it** to the black market dealer: 130 credits. (The quartermaster doesn't buy them.)
- **Take it to the Resistance lab** (only there: the quartermaster won't touch it): E on the scientist, and **Decode it** is at the top of his list (or Use it from your pockets beside him; `!decode` works too): 50 XP and 50 credits for whoever brought it, and for `lc_heist_intel_seconds` (300, five minutes) every Resistance member sees every unit in the city outlined through walls, NPC and player, with a countdown on screen. The scanner report, turned around.
- **Plug it into a terminal yourself** (Use, looking at one): your record is wiped clean, with no violations, no BOLO and no wanted status. (A Fighter's face is still known: hunted ranks stay hunted.)

**Street furniture and screens** (the editor's "Street" section): **bins** (searchable, somewhere to leave things, and stops on the sanitation crew's bin runs), **streetlights** (light the street around them), **loudspeakers** (the PA and broadcasts come out of these), a **giant Breencast screen** and a **small TV** (both city screens, like the wall one). Streetlights and loudspeakers break when shot (a unit who sees it makes you wanted for vandalism) or now and then by themselves (`lc_fixture_wear`). A broken streetlight goes dark; near a broken loudspeaker, nobody hears the PA or a broadcast (the banner says so). The maintenance crew's "Lights out" shift and the radio repair crew's "Loudspeakers" shift go to the city's own streetlights and loudspeakers, broken ones first, and fix them; so does the citizens' streetlight work order. With none placed, those shifts work as before.

**Broadcasts and the Director of Propaganda.** The City Administrator faction has a special, **Director of Propaganda** (one at a time), who carries a **microphone**. Left click with it to go on air: for up to `lc_broadcast_seconds` (60) their voice (hold your voice key) and anything they type go out to the whole city, every screen shows CITY BROADCAST with their name, and everyone sees who's speaking. Then the transmitter needs `lc_broadcast_cooldown` minutes (15) before anyone can go live again. A Combine broadcast that runs at least half a minute settles the city: rebel ground spreads more slowly for ten minutes. **Anyone holding the microphone can use it**: it drops when its holder dies, and can be taken off someone whose hands are up or tied (C menu > the person > Take their microphone). From anyone but the Director of Propaganda (or an Administrator) it's a **pirate broadcast**: screens show PIRATE BROADCAST, units are told to find the microphone, and half a minute of it makes rebel ground spread faster and slips sociostability to yellow. The Director of Propaganda gets a new microphone on respawn; searches take one from anyone else.

**The Administrator.** (Dormant: off unless `lc_breen_broadcasts 1`, until there are enough recordings in `administrator/ambient/`; `lc_breencast` still plays one as a test; a player Director of Propaganda is unaffected.) With no player as Director of Propaganda, every `lc_broadcast_cooldown` minutes (15, give or take) the Administrator addresses the city herself: her recordings go out over every loudspeaker (not near a broken one, or one whose district has lost power), and every screen shows CITY BROADCAST: THE ADMINISTRATOR. If the map has a **broadcast studio** (an editor point), she's there in person (Dr. Mossman's model by default), and the camera scanner films her live for every screen. A player Director of Propaganda can cut in on her; nobody else can go on air over her. **Kill her on air** and the transmission dies, her microphone falls to the floor, and the transmitter is free: pick it up for a pirate broadcast. Attacking the Administrator is a serious crime. She's back for the next one. Players set how loud her addresses are for them with `lc_breencast_volume` (0.5); anyone within earshot of her in the studio hears her there instead. `lc_breen_broadcasts 0` turns her off; `lc_breencast` (superadmin) puts her on air now.

**The broadcast camera.** When someone goes on air, a city scanner comes down out of the sky and films them, and **every city screen and TV shows its live picture**, with CITY BROADCAST or PIRATE BROADCAST and the speaker's name over it. It films whoever holds the microphone and nobody else: it can't tell the Director of Propaganda from the rebel who took it off him. It hangs in front of them at head height and keeps their head and shoulders in frame, drifting after them when they move rather than snapping to every step. Its eye blinks red, slowly, like a phone's record light. Shoot it down and the picture dies (the voice carries on). Off air, it flies back up and is gone. `lc_broadcast_camera 0` turns it off; players can set `lc_livecam_fps` (12; 0 turns the picture off for them). The picture reaches every screen wherever you are in the city, not just near the studio.

**The menus.** Press **F1** for the quick menu (nine things on the number keys) and **C** (outside build mode) for the full menu: everything you can do right now, for who you are and what you're looking at. At the top: **Guide / manual**, your pockets (F2), your board (F3) and factions (F4). Below that: the person in front of you (recognize, inform, give a work order, cuff, cut ties, drag, search), the door in front of you (rent, pick, hack), yourself (hands up, holster, struggle, surrender, poses, talk to an interrogator, leave a scanner, stuck), getting by (stash, fish, salvage, radio, request, foreman), Civil Protection's tools (backup, 10-20, records, voice lines, BOLO, sociostability) and the Resistance's (clothes, donate, decode, hack). Things that need words (the radio, a request, a BOLO) ask for them in a box. A body in front of you can be searched or hauled. Every chat command still works as a backup. `lc_actions` opens the full menu and `lc_quick` the quick menu, to bind elsewhere.

**Getting through Combine doors.** Keycards and bypass charges open them as before. A Resistance member (Member and up) can also hack one with nothing but time: the **multitool** on a locked Combine door (point it at the door and left click; the C menu and `!hack` work too, with a multitool in your pockets). The Resistance's **Hackers** (a Resistance special, Member and up, two at a time) carry one; anyone can buy one from the black market dealer for 250 credits. It's contraband: a search takes it, twelve seconds, and it's open for thirty before it shuts and locks itself again. A third of the time it trips an alarm ("door breach", marked for units). Rebel NPCs on an operation hack the doors in their way by themselves.

**Hacking vending machines and ration dispensers.** Aim the multitool at a water vending machine or a ration dispenser (one with power) and fire: ten seconds, keep still, and it spits out a random handful, `lc_machine_hack_out` to `lc_machine_hack_max` (5 to 8), cans of water or ration packets onto the floor in front of it: a risky crime, worth the risk. No credits, no coupon, no record. A unit who sees you at it makes you wanted for tampering with Union property. Then it's empty for `lc_machine_hack_cooldown` minutes (10).

**The Resistance's operations.**
- **Prison break.** When one of the Resistance is in a cell, the others get **Break them out** on their Operations board: get to the cells (keycard, bypass charge or `!hack` on the doors), then hold the cell door for twenty seconds while the alarm goes. Units get **Prison break**. The prisoner comes out free, and wanted.
- **Supply runs are watched.** Once the crate is taken on a Resistance supply run, every unit sees SMUGGLER on their screen where the carrier is, refreshed every few seconds, and gets **Intercept the smuggler**. Arresting or killing the carrier stops it. Delivered, it fills the rebel supply crate and everyone on the run gets a box of ammunition.
- **Hijacking a city screen.** Anyone with a multitool wires into a city screen (left click with it, E on it, or the C menu; ten seconds) and for a minute it shows the lambda while the whole city hears the Resistance's message. Units get **Retake the city screen** and cut it with E on the screen. If it runs its minute, rebel ground spreads faster and sociostability slips to yellow for ten minutes.
- **Ambushing an Overwatch landing.** See Overwatch drops below: the Resistance get **Ambush the landing**.
- **Freeing a vortigaunt.** **Free a vortigaunt** (Member and up): a shackled vortigaunt is being worked at a Combine site under guard. Get there, cut its shackles (twelve seconds beside it; a unit who sees you makes you wanted), and lead it home. It stays in rebel territory and charges your powered vest when you ask it (E on it: 25, once every `lc_vort_charge_minutes`, 10; see Powered vests). Up to two at a time.
- **Rebel NPCs answer what the Combine do:** an Overwatch landing or a supply drop gets a team of fighters sent at it `lc_rebel_react` of the time (0.5), straight away, whenever no other fight is on: an ambush on the landing party, or a raid on the supply crate (if they hold it, two loads are gone and the zone's supply crate gets food).
- **Rebel NPCs run operations too** (`lc_rebel_ops`), but sporadically: at most one every `lc_rebel_ops_minutes` (30, give or take), the first well into a session, so the city never turns into a running battle. The Resistance's fighters go out on one by themselves: a raid on a Combine data terminal (if they hold it long enough, every Resistance player sees every unit for five minutes), a prison break when one of the Resistance is in a cell (half the time), a screen hijack, or (as above) an ambush on an Overwatch landing. They hack the Combine doors in their way, the alarm goes off halfway (units get the matching directive), and they have to hold the objective to pull it off. The Resistance hear about it on the radio and can lend a hand; units can stop them.

**Informants.** A citizen can quietly report someone: hold C while looking at them and choose Inform (or `!inform <name>`), once every five minutes. If they really are Resistance, wanted or carrying contraband, units get the tip and see them outlined for a minute, it goes on their record, and the informant is paid 25 credits and some standing. A false report costs standing. But a Resistance member who sees you do it marks you a **traitor** for half an hour: the Resistance see TRAITOR over your head, and their fighters shoot you. `lc_informants 0` turns it off.

**Overwatch drops.** When sociostability stays red (or the city is in unrest) for a minute and a half, Overwatch sends soldiers down (`lc_drop_soldiers`, 4) to a landing zone (the editor's "Point: Overwatch landing zone", or a street), no more than once every `lc_drop_cooldown` minutes (30). They come by **dropship**, the way Half-Life 2 does it: the **troop container** stays in the dropship's claws the whole time. It comes in low over the rooftops, kicks up the dust as it comes down until the container hangs just off the ground, the container's door opens and the soldiers jump down out of it one by one, then the door shuts and it all climbs away together. **When the city is on red** (which is what brings a drop), the first one out is an **Overwatch Elite**, with three soldiers behind him. (Under a roof, where no dropship can come down, they arrive on foot at a run, from out of sight.) Units are told and get **Secure the landing zone**; the Resistance's own fighters go for it half the time (`lc_rebel_react`); Resistance players get a radio warning and **Ambush the landing**: be at the landing zone before the dropship, and bring down the landing party itself (any other soldier doesn't count). The soldiers take up posts round the landing zone and hold it for five minutes, then go. `lc_overwatch_drops 0` turns it off.

**Union foremen.** A Trusted citizen (150 Civil Standing) can become a foreman (C menu > Getting by; again to stop). C menu on another citizen > Give them a work order (or `!assign <name>`) hands them a Union work order (streetlights, rubble, pipes, litter): it goes straight onto their board, and when they finish it the foreman takes a cut of credits and a little standing.

**Custody: what it costs the worst.** A longer sentence isn't much of a deterrent, so the city doesn't just add time. How it treats you depends on how much of an anticitizen you are (Civil Standing, being a hunted fighter, how often you've been brought in, and whether it was for violence):
- **Release.** Units get their issue back. Everyone else walks out without anything the Combine would call a weapon: carried guns, homemade guns in the pockets and any radio stay in the evidence locker (other belongings come back), and everyone gets their fists back.
- **Interrogation.** Rebels get a visit (a Resistance fighter, or anyone hunted as one; a citizen locked up for a fight knows nothing, so is never questioned or crippled): a unit comes into the cell and asks questions, with a stun baton between them (it hurts; it won't kill). The interrogator offers it up front ("If you comply, I will grant you 50 social credits"). Choose Talk (the choice comes up on screen; it's in the C menu too) and it stops: you give up a Resistance hideout, marked for every unit, and gain 50 standing. If you're Resistance, the cell hears you talked and marks you a traitor. Say nothing and take it (Resistance members earn XP for that).
- **Broken feet.** The worse ones also have their feet broken: no running for `lc_cripple_minutes` (15), even after release, even after death or rejoining.
- **Stalkers.** The very worst aren't released at all (`lc_stalkers`): they're taken below and made into stalkers. **Only killers:** the Resistance's own (or anyone hunted as one) who have killed a unit, a soldier or a citizen in this life, or are brought in for murder; a citizen locked up again and again for small things never is. **How it happens:** the cell door opens and two units come in, batons out; a couple of blows, and everything goes black; in the dark, machinery, a long while of it, and a scream; you come to as one. Combine property: slow, unarmed (no guns can be picked up), unable to speak (what they type comes out as a wordless scream: their mouths aren't sewn shut; commands still work; E on a person only gets that scream, at half the volume it was), on the city's work orders, and treated as Combine by everyone: no unit warns, searches, beats or books a stalker, or tells it to pick up a can, and it can be anywhere in the Combine's ground (it wakes there, never at the station). Its papers are stamped STALKER across them. Whatever they were doing (a job, a quest) and whatever they had in their pockets goes with who they were; a stalker doesn't eat, drink or get tired; its board (F3) has its own work on it and nothing else; it can't take up another faction (not even as a superadmin) or switch to another character (`!characters`). `lc_unstalker <name>` (superadmin) lets someone off. **There is no way out but death**: no term runs out, and leaving the server doesn't end it. A stalker's death is the end of them, close calls or not (with permanent death off, they come back as a citizen on probation).
  - **Their body.** Stalkers wear a stalker playermodel: set `lc_stalker_model`, or leave it blank and the gamemode picks any installed playermodel with "stalker" in its name or path. Players who join are sent Workshop 268895264 (a stalker playermodel), so subscribe the server to it too. Without one, a stalker looks like a corpse.
  - **The Stalker's Lot.** A story on the board (F3) only for a stalker, worked below in the Combine areas (else the prison, else the checkpoints): processing (an Overwatch technician walks up and fits the harness to your spine while you stand), sweeping the holding floors with a push broom (E on each patch of grime), rewiring two power banks (the circuit-tile board), then building a Combine ball energizer: carry its four containment cells into the frame and weld its four seams (E on each), then its first charge (the board again, a bigger one). Half-Life 2's ball charger is part of its maps, not a model, so the energizer is a Combine generator with the ball glowing in it. At the end you choose: step into the light, and that person is gone for good (a new character, even with `lc_permadeath` 0), or turn away and go back to work, still a stalker. The energizer stays where you built it, humming: walk back to it any time and press E twice to step in after all.

**Radio jammers.** The Resistance lab's scientist makes one from a radio (the Freq 7 kind), 2 scrap and a bottle of chemicals, and the black market sells them (contraband). Set it down from your pockets and it hisses for `lc_jammer_secs` (240). Within `lc_jammer_radius` (1000 units): units can't call a 10-20 or backup or talk on the CP band, what reaches them on it is static, request devices don't get through, and a scanner's photographs never reach Civil Protection (the pilot's view says SIGNAL JAMMED, and nobody standing inside the jammer's reach can be reported). Units in it see RADIO JAMMED on their HUD. It beeps and blinks: a unit who finds it smashes it with E (15 XP), and anyone can shoot it. Being seen setting one down makes you wanted.

### Escalation

Violence gets a proportionate answer. Your alert level shows under the WANTED banner.

| Alert | What happens |
|---|---|
| 1 | Wanted. Units nearby come for you and arrest you if they can. |
| 2 | Every unit in the sector has your description, and they draw pistols. Killing a unit gets you here straight away. |
| 3 | Overwatch dispatched: a soldier squad deploys out of sight and hunts you, into rebel territory if need be. Lethal force: no more arrests. |
| 4 | A second squad, and the sector goes into lockdown. |

**City scanners are eyes for the units:** one that sees a fugitive (wanted, or a hunted rebel not passing as a citizen) within 900 units flashes them and tells every unit on duty where they are, and the units nearby come after them (once per 25 seconds per person). Shooting a scanner is Damaging city property (-10 standing, reported at most every five seconds); wrecking one is Destroying city property (-50). What raises it: hitting a unit, killing a citizen, a unit, or a soldier, or wrecking a scanner. **The first kill in ten minutes is answered lightly** (accidents and friendly fire happen): a citizen, a unit or a soldier killed raises the alert to 1 at most, with a short charge (two to four minutes wanted), and no Overwatch. A second kill soon after gets the full response. **The Resistance pay for killing civilians:** a Resistance member who kills a citizen (NPC or player) loses a rank each time, and their XP drops to the bottom of the rank they fall to. Getting arrested or killed resets it. Staying out of sight of every unit for 45 seconds cools it down (twice as fast in rebel territory), and the squads go home once you drop below alert 3. A unit you shoot remembers you however far away you were, and follows you into rebel territory. Settings: `lc_response`, `lc_response_soldiers`; admins can test with `lc_setalert <name> <level>`.

**Alarms bring a squad.** Set off an alarm (downloading a data chip, a prison escape) and about five seconds later a small squad comes for you: two metrocops with pistols and an Overwatch soldier, turning up out of sight a walk away (on a route to you, not on top of you) and coming through the door. When it's over (you're caught, or gone, or no longer wanted) they leave. Editor panel, Events: "Alarms send a response squad" and its sliders (`lc_squad`, `lc_squad_cops` 2, `lc_squad_soldiers` 1, `lc_squad_delay` 5).

### The dead

Citizens, metrocops and soldiers leave a body when they die. Press E on one and stay with it for a few seconds to search it.
- **Citizens:** rations, water, a few credits, sometimes their identity papers.
- **Metrocops:** pistol ammunition, credits, sometimes a stunstick or their **keycard**, which opens one Combine door before it's reported missing.
- **Soldiers:** rifle ammunition, grenades, health vials.

Searching a body in front of a unit is a crime. **The body cart** (Morticians, if they want one): F1 > Take a body cart, by the incinerator or the morgue. Let go of a body beside it and it goes on (four at a time); E on the cart pushes it (E again lets go); E on the incinerator with the cart beside it burns everything on it, paid for each. The same action leaves the cart. **Hauling:** anyone can drag a body (look at it: F1 > Haul the body); a **Mortician** does it with E. You walk slowly with it, it follows at your heels, and E lets go. Let go next to an **incinerator** (placed with the editor: a spent headcrab canister with a burner roaring out of its back end), or press E on it, and in it goes: Attendants are paid `lc_cremation_pay` credits (6) for each. Their **Collection** shift sends them to a real body the city has left lying about, or a reported one if there isn't one, and their **Clear the streets** shift (after a fight leaves two or more) pays for hauling all of them. With no incinerator, the morgue workplace takes the bodies; with neither, there are no collections. A body somebody is hauling, or a shift is about, isn't cleared away. Bodies stay for 5 minutes (`lc_body_time`), up to 16 at once (`lc_max_bodies`). Birds and headcrabs leave a carcass item instead of a ragdoll, so nothing appears twice. **A citizen attacked by a headcrab or a zombie** screams for help and runs, away from it; everyone round them scatters, and the two nearest units come and deal with it. Units deal with them anyway: one a headcrab bites stops whatever he's doing and fights it, and one who sees a headcrab or a zombie close by goes for it (`lc_units_spot_monsters`). **Guns hit the floor:** a dead Overwatch soldier drops his SMG or pulse rifle, and an armed unit or a rebel their gun, with about half a clip in it, for anyone to pick up (`lc_gun_drops`); the rest of their ammunition is found by searching the body. A gun nobody takes is cleared away, out of sight, after a few minutes, and there are never more than a dozen lying about.

## The city's people

Every citizen and Civil Protection unit is a **named person with a profile that never changes**: a name, a sex and voice, a model, a Union job, and a housing block. You'll see the same faces again. Look at one to see their name and what they do. They spend part of their day at their workplace (if the map has one for their job) and go to **their own block** at curfew. About one in eight citizens (`lc_rebel_pct`) is secretly a **Resistance sympathizer**: Resistance players see "one of ours" on them, and the Resistance's contacts and supply runs are drawn from them.

`lc_citizens` and `lc_cops` still set how many are out on the streets at once; the roster (`lc_roster_size` citizens, `lc_roster_cops` units) is everybody who lives in the city, and they take turns, the person who's been away longest coming out next. The roster is saved per map in `garrysmod/data/livingcity/<map>_people.json`, so it's the same people next session and next week. **A person who is killed stays dead**, and the city fills the gap with a new arrival. `lc_people [list]` summarizes, `lc_resetpeople` starts over.

### Trains

With a **train platform** on the map (an editor point; failing that, the Intake Processor's workplace), a train comes in every `lc_train_minutes` (12, give or take a quarter): a horn at the platform, a notice for everyone ("The train from City 14 has arrived..."), and two to four **new arrivals** step off, suitcase in hand (hanging upright from the hand, swinging as they walk), and walk to their housing blocks before getting on with life in the city. Intake Processors are told a train is in. For a minute and a half after, **anyone leaving the city** walks to the platform with a suitcase and boards, instead of going out through a doorway; then the train pulls out. Not during a curfew or lockdown. `lc_trains 0` turns them off; `lc_train` (admin) brings one in now.

### The power grid, and forcefields

**Transformers** (an editor fixture: the Combine's own generator, bolted down so it can't topple, humming with its lamp lit while it has power, dark and silent when it's down; move it with the physgun in build mode and it's stood upright and bolted down where you drop it) power the district around them, out to `lc_grid_radius` (1800 units): every streetlight, loudspeaker, city screen and TV, forcefield, ration dispenser, water vending machine and commissary terminal there, every one of the **map's own switchable lights** (the lamps, spotlights and glows the mapper named so they could be turned off), and the locks on its Combine doors, run off the nearest one. Anything with no transformer near it is on the city's mains and never goes out. **Sabotage** one (a multitool, twelve seconds; or enough damage: explosives, a lot of bullets) and its district goes dark: lights out, loudspeakers silent (nobody there hears the PA or a broadcast), screens and TVs dead, dispensers, vending machines and the commissary dead (no rations there until it's back, and NPCs don't queue at a dead dispenser), forcefields down, and **Combine doors unlocked**. **Prison doors stay locked.** A unit who sees you do it makes you wanted for sabotage; the Resistance gets XP. Units hear about it at once, the Resistance hears their doors are open, and Maintenance Technicians are told to fix it: E on it, ten seconds, paid. The city's own maintenance crews see to it after a while, and emergency crews after `lc_blackout_minutes` (8).

**The whole city goes dark** when half the grid is down at once (`lc_blackout_share`, 0.5 of the transformers; 0 turns it off; on a map with one transformer, that one). Then everything above goes out everywhere, the city's mains included, and so does **the map's own lighting**: everyone's going by flashlight (F). Prison doors still hold. Restore enough transformers and the lights come back. Admins can black out the city for an event from the admin tools ("The city": Black out the city, Lights on, Follow the grid) or with `lc_blackout on|off|auto`. `lc_blackout_light` sets how dark (a, pitch black, to m, normal; b by default), and night stays as dark as night if that's darker. Two things Source decides, not us: the sun's light is baked into the same lighting as the lamps, so outdoors goes as dark as indoors (like a moonless night; the sky itself stays bright); and players, NPCs and props are lit separately from the walls, so they may not darken as much as the room around them.

**Combine forcefields** (an editor fixture: place it facing you, across a doorway or a street, about eight feet wide) let units (and admins in build mode) walk through; nobody else, unless they **swipe a Combine keycard** (E on it, or the actions menu: a few seconds, and a unit who sees a citizen do it makes them wanted). A multitool **shorts one out** for a minute, and it drops with its district's power. Who may pass is worked out the same way on your machine and the server's, so walking into one is smooth. NPCs aren't stopped by them, and nobody gets trapped: if a field comes back up (power restored, a short or a swipe running out) around someone it wouldn't let through, they can step out of it, on whichever side they like.

### Checkpoints, turrets, and holding the infestation

A checkpoint is built round a **Combine power bank**: a glowing wall unit, placed in the editor ("Fixture: Combine power bank") or put up by a Civil Protection officer (actions menu: Civil Protection > Put up a power bank on this wall; Division Leader and up, one each). **Turrets and forcefields** within `lc_checkpoint_range` (700 units) of it run off it, on a cable you can see; a turret with no bank near it can run off a forcefield instead. The bank draws from its district's transformer, so a sabotaged transformer, a city-wide blackout, or a **multitool on the bank itself** (aim it at the bank and primary fire: eight seconds, dead for two minutes) kills its glow and everything on its cables. Units E on a bank to read what it's powering.

**Turrets** are Half-Life 2's Combine floor turrets. They shoot the infected, the wanted and known Resistance fighters, and leave ordinary citizens, people in custody and units alone. Place them in the editor ("Fixture: Combine turret", facing you), or **any unit deploys one** where they're looking (actions menu: Civil Protection > Deploy a turret here), near a power bank or forcefield: `lc_turrets_per_unit` (2) each, `lc_turrets_per_post` (4) on one bank or forcefield. A unit packs one up with E. Nobody else can pick one up or move it. Without power, a turret folds up, goes quiet and is held where it stands (so it doesn't topple on its own); powered, it stands on its legs and **can be knocked over** again, which is how you beat one. The editor's turrets come back (out of sight) if they're knocked over or blown up. **Combine barricades** (low, medium, tall) wall off the rest.

**Holding the infestation.** Forcefields stop the infected as well as people. **The infestation can't spread over or past a checkpoint that has power** (`lc_containment`). What the turrets kill holds it where it is without pushing it back (units still have to go in for that), and quietly: the radio doesn't report every kill. But kill a checkpoint's power within reach of the infestation (a blackout, a sabotaged transformer, a shorted power bank) and it's a **containment breach**: the whole city hears it, the infestation surges over the checkpoint, and `lc_breach_infected` (4) of the infected come pouring out. One breach in a place every five minutes at most.

### Talking to people

**Press E on a citizen** (when no job of yours needs that press for something else) and a small window opens: their name, their job and what they're doing, how they feel about you, and what you can do:

- **Chat:** whatever they've heard. The city's rumours (what's been happening: rebel ground taken, the infested spreading, arrests) come out here now, not in chat. A sympathizer who likes you will tell you where the Resistance gathers.
- **Share some food:** one of your rations (or bread, a can, whatever you have that's fit to eat). They remember it.
- **Pick their pocket:** a Guile check (d20 + Guile against `lc_pickpocket_dc`, 12). A few credits, now and then a ration, water, or a dead man's papers. Once per person per ten minutes. Fail and they shout "Thief!" and back away, they won't forget it, and if a unit saw it you're wanted for theft.
- **Inform on them** (not the Resistance): if they really are a sympathizer, they're picked up within the minute and you're paid (+15 standing); if not, you've wasted a unit's time (-5).
- **Ask them to keep watch** (the Resistance, a sympathizer): a lookout, below.

Units are units: Civil Protection players still stop citizens with E.

**They remember you.** Each person on the roster remembers how each of your characters has treated them (another character of yours is a stranger to them), saved with the roster: hurt them (-4), get caught robbing them (-3), report them (-4), and at -3 they're **afraid of you**: they won't talk, and when you come near they back away. Feed them (+2), heal them (+2), and at +3 they're **warm**: they greet you in the street and always tell you what they've heard. Memory runs from -10 to +10, and each person keeps their strongest twenty. `lc_talk 0` turns the window off.

### Resistance lookouts

**Spotting a sympathizer:** to the Resistance (and nobody else), the ordinary-looking citizens who are secretly with them carry a small orange lambda over their heads, when you're close and can see them; look at one and their name tag says "one of ours: a sympathizer". A Resistance Member (rank 2 and up) can ask a sympathizer to **keep watch** (E on them, then "Ask them to keep watch for us" in the talk window). They stay where they're standing for `lc_lookout_minutes` (15), and when a unit or Overwatch comes into sight (`lc_lookout_range`, 1100 units, and not through smoke) they call out "Combine!" and Freq 7 hears where, at most once every 45 seconds. Two each. The one who posted them always hears, radio or not. A lookout is still a citizen: a unit can stop them, and then the Resistance hears the lookout's been stopped (talking to your own lookout doesn't count). Resistance players see "one of ours: keeping watch" on them.

**Lookout posts** are the armed version, placed by an admin: the editor's "Point: rebel lookout post" keeps a Resistance fighter (SMG, pulse rifle or shotgun) standing guard there, at the entrance to rebel territory, say. Place it from where the Combine would come: he faces that way. When a unit, Overwatch, someone in a convincing uniform or one of the city's own units comes into sight within `lc_lookout_post_warn` (1400 units) he shouts and warns Freq 7 where; when they come within `lc_lookout_post_fire` (650) or onto rebel ground, he opens fire (after a moment's shouting). He holds his post, fights from it, and goes back to it after; killed, someone takes the post after `lc_lookout_post_respawn` seconds (120), out of sight.

## Factions and ranks

**Switching faction from the factions menu (F4) keeps you where you're standing**: the new uniform and kit go on right there, instead of sending you back to a spawn point. (A new character, another of your characters, or an admin setting your faction still starts you at a spawn point.)

**Joining Civil Protection needs good standing**: 100 or more Civil Standing (`lc_cp_min_standing`), so a new character has to earn their way in with jobs and compliance first. Admins can join regardless, to test, and `lcf_setfaction <name> <faction>` sets anyone's faction directly.

| Faction | Ladder |
|---|---|
| Citizen | Citizen, Union Worker, Loyalist, Model Citizen |
| Resistance | Sympathizer, Member (pass for citizens), Fighter, Lieutenant, Commander (hunted outside rebel zones) |
| Civil Protection | Record Keeper, Metrocop, Division Leader, then the Overwatch: Combine Prison Guard, Combine Soldier, Elite Soldier. One Elite can be the **Overwatch Commander** (F4). |
| City Administrator | whitelist only |
| Infested | Zombie, Fast Zombie, Zombine (claws only: no guns; the Zombie and Zombine are no faster than a citizen; joining from F4 puts you in a nest; what they kill isn't murder, so no wanted and no response) |

**One career, from the records office to the Overwatch.** Civil Protection is a single ladder: XP takes you from Record Keeper (a baton, a desk) to Metrocop (a pistol) to Division Leader (an SMG, and an officer: sociostability, power banks, city-wide broadcasts). Past that are the Overwatch ranks, and they need the **transhuman conditioning** first: the Overwatch Candidate story comes to a Division Leader (F3), and at its end a technician offers it. Accept, and the Overwatch ranks open at once (your XP already counts: enough of it and you go straight to Combine Soldier or Elite). Decline, and you keep your name, and stay a Division Leader for good (XP still counts, the bar says why it stops). Overwatch ranks don't eat, don't get stopped, have their own voice lines, and their own board of Protocols alongside Civil Protection's. **The Overwatch Commander** (one at a time, from the Elite: F4) calls in an Overwatch squad by dropship to the landing zone nearest them, once every fifteen minutes (F1 > Unit tools, "Call in a squad"). Admins promoting someone (`!setrank`, `lcf_promote`) skip the story. **The Scanner Operator** (two at a time, any rank: F4) flies a city scanner from a Combine console, a data terminal, or anywhere in the Nexus (a map with none of those: anywhere), from F1 > Unit tools. Their reports earn XP (up to `lc_scanner_snitch_cap` every ten minutes), and a rebel they photograph goes out to every unit as a **BOLO** for half an hour; anyone else they catch is reported as usual, without one.

**Half-Life: Alyx-style Combine.** Install a Combine model pack and the Overwatch wear it, players in the Overwatch ranks and the city's own soldiers alike; nothing to set up. The first choice is Workshop 3449180606 (soldier, shotgunner, prison guard, shotgunner prison guard and Elite), the fallback Workshop 2041528484 (a soldier and the red prison guard). Which model is which comes from its name and path, and players who join are sent whichever of the two the server has mounted. Metrocops stay Half-Life 2's. `lc_combine_models_list` (admin) shows what was found; `lc_pm_<kind>` and `lc_npcmodel_<kind>` (soldier, shotgunner, guard, guard_shotgun, elite) name a model by hand; `lc_combine_models 0` keeps Half-Life 2's.

**Squads.** Units hunting the same person move as one squad (`lc_squads`): the one nearest the target leads, the rest keep with him (running to catch up, walking once close) and, once with him, stand watch facing open ground instead of a wall. Whoever sees the target fights; one hurt while running stops and fights back. Soldiers call it on the radio (contact, lost contact), and they share Half-Life 2's own squad, so its tactics (taking turns to shoot, flanking) work too. Soldiers holding a landing zone face out the same way.

**The kick.** Every Civil Protection rank can kick: G (`lc_kick_key`), or F1 > Kick. An unlocked door bangs open, fast, away from you; a locked one gives on the second kick (`lc_kick_door_kicks`, 2) and an apartment's tenants are told; a Combine door opens; a prison door or a "locked" door doesn't give. Someone kicked is knocked back and hurt a little (`lc_kick_damage`, 12); something loose goes flying, and a boot breaks crates, planks and anything breakable the way a crowbar does. **The Overwatch's boots are heavier:** a Combine Soldier kicks an ordinary hinged door off its hinges and it falls over where it hung; an Elite sends it flying into the room, five to ten feet (never the same twice), and whoever it hits is **knocked out** (15 seconds of black, then the room swims; never a death or a close call, so kicking doors in during a curfew can't kill a block); a door falling over only leaves you dizzy; the Overwatch Commander, eight to fourteen (`lc_kick_door_power`, 1, scales the flight; 0 turns it off). Brush doors (a sliding warehouse door, a Combine shutter, a gate) don't budge for anyone's boot. **Rehanging it:** a Maintenance Technician presses E on the fallen door to lift it, carries it back to its frame (marked on screen) and presses E there (8 seconds; `lc_rehang_pay`, `lc_combine_models`, `lc_outfits`, `lc_people_heights`, `lc_citizen_tint`, `lc_squads`, 10 credits); the city's own maintenance crews come and do it if no player has. Failing all that it's back by itself after `lc_kick_door_back` seconds (600). Everyone sees the leg come up, on any model (the thigh and knee are bent in code), and in first person you see your own leg, so it's always the right boot. `lc_kick 0` turns it off.

**The Smuggler** (Resistance, two at a time, Member and up: F4) moves the cell's cargo: smuggling runs on their board (F3), collected from the quartermaster and taken to three drops in the city against the clock. No questions, no sender: they know it's guns and ammunition, and so does any unit whose search finds it. Better paid than a courier's run (6 a drop, 10 more for getting it all through, and XP). A courier who knowingly carries a hot parcel is a one-off; smuggling is the Resistance's trade.

**Special roles** (Hacker, Smuggler, Father Grigori, Free Vortigaunt, Director of Propaganda) are held on top of your rank, not instead of it: a Fighter who takes up the Hacker's role is still a Fighter, keeps earning XP toward Lieutenant, and carries the role's kit (the multitool) instead of the rank's while holding it; Join the faction again from F4 to give it up and go back to the rank's kit. F4 says so under each role, and your HUD shows both ("Hacker (Fighter)"). They come back with you: leave and rejoin and you're still the Hacker, as long as there's still a place. Nobody holds a place by being offline, so if the places filled up while you were away you're back to your rank, and told so; take it again from F4 when one opens. Whitelisted factions come back too, while you're still whitelisted.

**Powered vests (what HL2 calls suit power).** Nobody in the city wears an HEV suit: the armour number is the charge in a **powered vest**, kevlar plates with charge cells, and you hold only as much as your vest takes (the VEST bar bottom left, in place of HL2's SUIT). Combine units have one built into the uniform (their rank's armour is its capacity, and the Civil Protection Commissary tops it up). The Resistance's ranks come with one (Fighter 25, Lieutenant 50, Commander 75). Anyone else holds no charge at all until they put one on: the **powered vest** is made in the Resistance lab (or sold on the black market), holds `lc_vest_capacity` (75) and comes with 30 in it; Put on, from your pockets. It's lost when you die. Only these charge it (sleeping doesn't): suit batteries and chargers, the Commissary for units, **charge cells** (Charge your vest, from your pockets: 25; the Resistance lab makes them from 2 scrap and 2 chemicals, and a dead Combine unit drops one `lc_vest_cell_drop` (25%) of the time, a downed scanner twice as often), and **vortigaunts**: E on a freed one in rebel territory and it charges you (25, once every `lc_vort_charge_minutes`, 10, not by itself while you stand or sleep beside it), and a vortigaunt player's right click charges whoever they look at.

**Father Grigori, the man himself.** While no player is Father Grigori, the NPC keeps to a rebel territory with Annabelle (the largest at first), muttering scripture; every `lc_grigori_move_minutes` (12) or so he moves on to another of the Resistance's camps, when nobody's near enough to see him go (0: he stays put). E on him: **Ask for his blessing** (25 health, and blessed for five minutes: you take 15% less harm; once every five minutes each), **Ask him to preach** (he rants, and every Resistance member within earshot is emboldened for ten minutes: their shots hit the Combine 10% harder, and a little XP; once every ten minutes), or **Talk to him** (anyone). Only the Resistance get his blessing and his sermons; a unit who walks up gets a promise of Annabelle. Killed, he's back after `lc_grigori_respawn` minutes (10); `lc_grigori 0` turns him off.

**Father Grigori** (the city's own, in rebel territory) says something once a minute at most (`lc_grigori_talk_gap`, 60), and none of the engine's idle chatter. As a player, Father Grigori is a special Resistance role: his shotgun is Annabelle, and holds **two shells** at a time (the rest stay in his pockets); The Blessing heals, preaching draws Civil Protection, and he's hunted on sight outside rebel territory. Edit `gamemodes/livingcity/gamemode/modules/shared/sh_livingcity_factions.lua` to rename ranks, change XP, models and loadouts.

## Civil Protection's tools

**The quick menu** (F1) has what a unit does most on the number keys: 1 Call for backup, 2 Send my 10-20, 3 Issue a BOLO, 4 the mask's most used voice lines, 5 the records terminal, and on 6 to 8 search, cuff, drag and escort for whoever you're looking at.

**Supplies: the Civil Protection Commissary** (editor: "Fixture: Civil Protection Commissary"). Units don't make anything, so F2 has no crafting for them: it says to visit a Civil Protection Commissary, with a button that marks the nearest one on your screen (gone when you get there). Press E there and you're issued what your rank allows: grenades, ammunition for the issued weapons you carry (back to a full load), armour (50; officers 75, Overwatch 100), up to two bandages, and your ration once every `lc_cpsupply_ration` (600) seconds. Once every `lc_cpsupply_cooldown` (120) seconds; then the unit counter opens for anything more. **The unit counter is the only place units buy** ammunition, grenades, cuffs and suit batteries: the Union Commissary terminals sell citizens' things and nothing else. Citizens are turned away. A city with none placed: the Union Commissary terminal issues units their supplies, and sells them those, instead.

**Unit tools** (C > Unit tools, at the top of the full menu for units; or bind a key to `lc_unittools`) puts every one of these in one window, each button with a line saying what it does: **Dispatch** (call for backup, send your 10-20, issue a BOLO), **Records** (the terminal), **Your mask** (voice lines), **In front of you** (search, cuff, cut ties, drag, escort, take a microphone, for whoever you were looking at when you opened it), **Checkpoints** (a turret, a power bank), **Escorts** (workers waiting for one, and handing yours back), **Post office** (reading the mail), **Sociostability** (officers) and **Your work** (your directives, F3). The full menu's Civil Protection section has the same, as a list. The chat commands below still work as shortcuts.

- **The records terminal** (`!records`, or bind a key to `lc_records`; units only, and a stolen uniform doesn't get you in). Every citizen has a record kept by CID: violations, which the city writes in itself as they happen (wanted, custody, contraband found, escape), notes units add, and their **loyalty points** (Civil Standing). Units can log a violation, add a note, and award or deduct loyalty points: 10 at a time, 25 for officers, once every two minutes per citizen. Records survive restarts (`garrysmod/data/livingcity/records.json`), and people who aren't on can be searched by name or CID. Someone living on **forged papers** has a record under that number instead, and it starts clean.
- **Scanner reports.** When a scanner flown from a Combine console photographs a rebel, a free vortigaunt, the wanted, a BOLO subject, an anticitizen, a zombie or rebel ground, every unit gets the report and the people it caught are outlined in red through walls for a minute (see Flying a scanner, under The city). A unit can fly a scanner from a console too.
- **BOLOs** ("be on the lookout"): issued from a record, or on a description alone. Every unit hears it, and a citizen with one out on them shows it on their nametag to units. They expire after `lc_bolo_minutes` (30). One unit can issue one every 30 seconds (staff any time).
- **Cuffs and walking people along.** `!cuff` (or `!tie`), looking at someone up close, cuffs them in three seconds, if their hands are up (`!handsup`), they're down, a unit has stopped them, or you're behind them. Units carry their own; anyone else needs cuffs (Commissary for units, the black market for everyone else). Tied: no weapons, nothing in your pockets, walking pace. `!drag` takes them by the arm and walks them along with you (`!drag` again lets go); the baton's arrest takes them in, and their things go with them. `!uncuff` (or `!untie`) (instant for a unit, four seconds for anyone else). Left alone and unseen, a tied person can `!struggle` free in 30 seconds (`lc_struggle_seconds`).
- **Sociostability**, city-wide: green (nominal), yellow (marginal: half as many stops again), red (judgment waiver: twice as many, and every metrocop has his pistol out instead of his stick until it's lifted). The city sets it from what's happening (curfew, sweeps, raids, unrest, a skirmish, anyone at alert 2: yellow; a lockdown, an outbreak, anyone at alert 3: red), with the PA and a line in chat when it changes. Officers (Division Leader and up, the Administrator) and admins can set it for a while (an officer once every 30 seconds): `!socio <green|yellow|red> [minutes]`, `!socio auto`, or the buttons on the records terminal. Everyone sees it at the top of the screen when it isn't green.
- **The mask.** Units (and anyone in a CP uniform) see the city through the mask's lenses: darker edges, a cold tint, and a readout with their designation, sociostability and the number of BOLOs out. It stays on in build mode, and goes when you change faction. `lc_mask_overlay 0` turns it off for you.
- **Duty allocation.** Units don't queue at the ration line. Every allocation (`lc_ration_interval`, ten minutes) a unit on duty is issued a ration packet and their ration coupons as credits: `lc_cp_coupons` (8) plus one per rank. It's the same allocation a dispenser gives, so a unit can take theirs from a dispenser instead, just not both. Overwatch don't eat, and get the coupons only.
- **`/request`.** A citizen with a request device (Commissary, 10 credits) calls Civil Protection to where they are: `/request <what's wrong>`. Units get the message and a marker on screen for two minutes, and it's listed on the terminal; with no units on, the nearest Civil Protection NPC walks over. Once a minute (`lc_request_cooldown`). A false request is a violation a unit can log.
- **Voice lines.** Units speak in the mask's voice: `!vo` (or bind a key to `lc_vo_menu`) for a menu of about fifty lines, `/vo <word>`, or say a line's exact words in chat ("Move along."). Overwatch have their own. The list is in `gamemodes/livingcity/gamemode/modules/shared/sh_livingcity_cp.lua`.

`lc_cp_tools 0` turns all of this off.

## Jobs and quests (F3)

**Finding the objective.** The tracker on the right shows a compass for whatever you're after: a pointer relative to where you're facing, the distance in meters, a heading (ahead, behind left, ...) and whether it's on your level, a floor or more **UP**, or **DOWN**. It works even when the target is far enough away that the game isn't drawing it. Quests never send you to the infestation or onto Combine ground. Deliveries are made to **people**: the mail comes from the postal clerk and goes to named residents, Mrs. Kleiner hands you her parcel herself, luggage goes back to its owner, uniforms go to the unit at the checkpoint. Ration crates come from the truck driver and go to the dispenser attendant, and the Resistance's supply runs, the weapons ring and the proof-of-nerve parcel are handed over by and to contacts. Each person waits at a marked spot; walk up and press E.

Each faction has a board of radiant jobs built from your actual map: Directives, Protocols, Agenda, Operations, Assignments, Urges. Many have **choices with d20 checks** (Authority, Empathy, Guile, Nerve), and your choices build three traits that stick: **Loyalty, Sympathy, Corruption**.

Built for crowded servers: no job ever targets a player, boards are finite and scale with faction size, violent jobs are capped city-wide, and people can squad up.

**Directives for what's happening now.** The city's events bring their own jobs, posted two minutes before an event starts (units are briefed early even for a surprise sweep or raid) and as it begins, shown only while it's on, and withdrawn when it ends (whoever was already working one is paid; whoever hadn't started isn't held to it). Units get **Ration line supervision** (rations), **Civil status inspections** (sweeps: pat-downs), **Housing block inspection** (block raids: breach the door, get everyone down, search the place), **Curfew enforcement** and **Lockdown: hold the perimeter**; the Resistance get **Warn the block** (raids) and **Run supplies under curfew** (curfew and lockdown). When the infestation spreads or the Resistance take ground, **Containment** or **Retake the block** goes up at once for that patch, and every unit is told. These, and the chases (Apprehend, Disturbance), are squad jobs: up to 4 to 8 people work one together (the rest of the board takes `lc_quest_squad`, 3). Take it from the board and the others join you from theirs.

## Union jobs

**Everyone works.** A registered citizen without a job is assigned one by the Union, the post with the fewest hands (a notice says which, and where to report); ask for another on F3 > Jobs. The Resistance aren't assigned (one can still take a job, passing as a citizen). `lc_assign_work 0` leaves it to people to choose. Changing jobs ends the old job's shift, so the new job's can be taken. Citizens (and Resistance members who still pass for citizens) choose a job on their board. Promotions never take your tools away. Each job posts a steady stream of **Union shifts** at its workplaces, or in the streets if none are marked:

| # | Job | Shifts |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sanitation Worker | Litter sweeps, bin rounds (at the map's own bins), graffiti removal |
| 2 | Ration Production | The sealing line (split packs to the reject bin), supply runs, the count against the register, the counter (forged ration cards). Rations are made, not cooked |
| 3 | Clinic Medic (gets a medkit) | Clinic rounds, house calls |
| 4 | Factory Worker | Assembly quotas, the loading dock, a foreman who skims |
| 5 | Courier | Mail rounds, sealed envelopes, parcel runs |
| 6 | Maintenance Technician | Streetlights (or fuse boxes on a map with none), faulty terminals |
| 7 | Intake Processor | New arrivals to walk home, luggage, ticket checks |
| 8 | Records Clerk | Filing (*a game; open while `lc_jobgames` is on*) |
| 9 | Uniform Laundry | Sorting (*a game; open while `lc_jobgames` is on*) |
| 10 | Mortician | Hauling the dead to the incinerator, clearing the streets after trouble, searching the dead before the fire, bagging dead headcrabs |
| 11 | Reclamation Crew | Scrap runs, rubble that sometimes hides a weapons cache |
| 12 | Radio Repair | Repairs (*a game; open while `lc_jobgames` is on*) |

**Games only:** every Records Clerk, Uniform Laundry and Radio Repair shift is one of the games below (the city's own people still work there too). With `lc_jobgames 0` those three are closed: anyone who had one is told the post has closed, and picks another.

**Work you actually do.** Most jobs' shifts are small games played with your hands in the world, and pay on top of the shift for how well you do them:

- **Street sweep** (Sanitation): a wheeled cart waits at the depot. E grabs its handle and you push it; E again lets go. Rubbish lies about as real things (cans, bottles, scrap metal, chemical jugs, newspaper), outlined as you get near: pick a piece up (E) and throw it in (left click). A credit a piece, another for a long throw, a bonus for a quick round. Some pieces hide something (tokens, a ration, a blank form, ammunition, a note). Push the full cart back to the depot.
- **On the line** (Factory): a conveyor carries parts past you. The defective ones are rust-red and spark: grab them and throw them in the scrap bin before they reach the end, and let the good ones through. Twenty parts, faster as it goes; pay for every right call, docked for every wrong one, a bonus for a clean shift. One part rattles: R while you hold it slips what's inside into your pocket (a unit who sees calls it theft). Place the factory's conveyor with the editor ("Fixture: factory conveyor"); without one, a line is set up where you report for the shift. Every part is one you can grab, set square on the belt and kept from spinning; the city's own workers at a point beside the belt face it.

**A ration break.** Rations come out while you're on a shift or a job: F1 (or the C menu) > Take a ration break (`!rationbreak`) and the job waits, its clock stopped, till you've had your ration (or five minutes, or `!back`).
- **Papers check** (Intake Processor, the job once called Station Porter): travelers walk up from the platform (or from out of sight) one at a time, and walk off again out of sight. E on one opens their papers beside today's rules: their photo (look at them: is it them?), name, district, expiry, stamps, and the seal (it must read OCS). Approve, deny, or call a unit. Mistakes earn a citation and are docked; a forgery handed to a unit pays well, and one waved through is between you and the Resistance (unless there's an audit).

- **Rewiring** (Maintenance): every streetlight, fuse box and faulty terminal opens a board of scrambled circuit tiles (bends, straights and tees; 5 by 4, a terminal 6 by 5). Click a tile to turn it a quarter (right-click turns it back) until the current runs from the feed on the left to the lamp on the right; every tile the current reaches lights up, so you can see how far it gets. Only one winding way through works, and the odd tiles round it lead nowhere. **Live sparks** jump about the board (two, three on a terminal): turn a tile while it's sparking and it bites (a shock, and 4 seconds off the clock), so time your clicks. 55 seconds (75 for a terminal); too slow and the breaker trips and the board scrambles again, for less pay. Done in no more turns than it needs plus two ("par", on the board) is a bonus of 2 credits; during a blackout it pays double.
- **Triage** (Clinic Medic): three or four patients come in at once, bleeding out at different rates (a bad bleed, a stab wound, a stunstick beating, burns, a broken arm). A bar over each head shows who's worst; the medkit (primary fire) brings them up, and the wound takes them back down. Get them on their feet before they're gone. Paid for every one who walks out, docked for every one lost, and a bonus when nobody is. The beaten man asks you not to write down what did it.
- **Rubble** (Reclamation Crew): each rubble pile is a heap of concrete blocks to break up with a crowbar (lent for the shift if you haven't one, and no unit books you for carrying it). Each block takes a few blows and leaves scrap where it broke; now and then something else. The weapons cache under the second pile is still there.
- **Parcel run** (Courier): three parcels against the clock, each door marked on your screen. They aren't letters. **First, the sender hands it over**, in a small window, and the clock doesn't start till you decide. Most say what's in it (and it's harmless); some won't ("Don't ask. Just get it there."); some own up to contraband and offer 8 credits extra when it gets there. You can **take it**, **hand it back** (no parcel, no pay), **report the sender** to Civil Protection (only one who wouldn't say or owned up: Civil Standing, more if there really was contraband in it), or **open it** to see for yourself (Civil Standing lost, and 1 credit a delivery instead of 3 if you deliver it; after that you can still take it, hand it back or report it). Close the window and E brings it back. A unit who gets a good look at you (a meter on the panel fills while one can see you; crouching keeps it low) calls you over: stand still for five seconds while he searches, or run. Run, and you're wanted; stand, and most of the time he hands it back, but if there's something in it, it's seized and so are you. Paid per parcel, more for time to spare, a bonus for never being stopped.
- **Searching the dead** (Mortician): three bodies for the incinerator, one at a time on the slab. E on one shows its pockets: search each, then **log** what you find (paid, and contraband logged earns standing), **keep** it (it's yours: dead men's papers, a ring, ration coupons, rounds, a data chip sewn into a hem), or set it aside **for the family**. The overseer looks in now and then (the panel says when): keep something while he's watching and it goes in the tray, and in his notebook. Then into the fire.
- **Sorting** (Uniform Laundry): Civil Protection uniforms, one at a time, by their unit tag into the bins for its division (each bin takes two); bloodstained ones to the incident wash. Twelve, against the clock. Units leave things in their pockets: pocket it (the supervisor sometimes counts) or turn it in.
- **Filing** (Records Clerk): ten forms into the right drawer by the first two digits of the citizen number, beside what the register says. Anything that doesn't match it (a misspelt name, two digits swapped) goes in the discrepancy tray. The cabinet of blank identity forms is rarely locked: one a shift, if no unit is watching.
- **Repairs** (Radio Repair): four sets off the shelf, two minutes each (`lc_radio_set_secs`, 120; the time left ticks down on the bench, red near the end). Finishing a set (or running out of time on one) shows it a moment before the next comes off the shelf: clean, or back on the shelf unfixed. A set is fixed when it's on its station **and** it comes through clean, and that's the repair: three **trimmer screws** inside it, left somewhere wrong, that you turn with the screwdriver (hold the mouse on one and go round it, clockwise to turn it up, anticlockwise to turn it down, like a real screw; or the mouse wheel for the last little bit) and set by ear. The **volume trimmer** sets how loud the station comes through, the **squelch** how much hiss is left under it, and the **alignment** how sharp it is (off, and the station is weak and smeared across the dial, with more static bleeding in). Find the station on the dial first, then work the screws until the hiss drops away and the station is full; two meters, Station and Static, show what you're hearing. **Test it** when it sounds right: on the station but still off, the bench tells you which screw it sounds like (and costs you a couple of seconds). Now and then the clearest thing on the band is **Freq 7**: report it (standing), write it down where nobody will look, or tune past it (the dial stays where you left it). **Written down**, it's a **Freq 7 sheet** in your pockets: Read it for the rule and the seven frequencies (and where you heard it). It's contraband, so pass it on to someone who wants it, or burn it before a search. The city's own repairers (NPCs on the job) work on a radio sitting on each bench point: put the Radio Repair work points on a table or a bench, and the radio sits on top, with the repairer standing at it. **It sounds like a radio:** static plays the whole time, and as the dial nears a station the station fades in through it (they overlap; the hiss never quite goes until you're on it). Anyone standing near the set hears it as you tune it, Freq 7 included: full volume beside it, quieter as they step back, and nothing past about twenty feet (240 units). The recordings are yours to make, in the same folders as the announcements (below): `radio/static/` (the hiss), `radio/union/` (an ordinary station; with none, the Administrator's `ambient/` recordings) and `radio/freq7/` (the pirate broadcast; its `.txt` is the line shown on the dial). Long files that loop cleanly work best.

**Talking over your radio.** A radio only listens. There are two frequencies people transmit on: **the CP net** (every unit, issued) and **Freq 7** (a Freq 7 radio plus a **radio transmitter**, contraband: the black market, 70, or the Resistance lab, from a Union radio and 2 scrap). Nobody transmits on the Union band: it's the city's, for listening. Anyone who can transmit has a **Radio transmitter** next to their Hands: switch to it like any weapon (your hands are full: no gun at the same time). While it's up, everything you type in chat and everything you say on voice goes out on the frequency it's tuned to, and only someone standing right beside you (`lc_radio_overhear`, 110 units, about two metres) hears you directly; nobody else nearby hears a thing. Commands, `/me` and out of character chat work as usual. While it's up you hear a soft hiss of static (the radio's static recording, `radio/static...`), just you, as a reminder that you're on the air (`lc_radio_static_volume`, your own setting, 0.08; 0 turns it off). R, or switching to your hands or a gun, puts it away. **Right click switches frequency** for someone who can send on both, a unit carrying a Freq 7 radio and a transmitter (a Resistance Insider): the screen says which one you're on, so mind it. `!r <message>` sends one line without raising it.

**Your own radio.** A Union radio or a radio retuned to Freq 7 has **Listen** in your pockets: a small window with the same dial, Station and Static meters, and the same sound (static between stations, the station fading in through it), and anyone within about twenty feet hears it too. The Union band always sits at 96.4; **Freq 7 moves** (below), and a retuned radio starts on wherever it is now and follows it when it hops (a Union radio has to find it). Close the window and it keeps playing (Listen again, or `lc_radio`, brings it back); **Switch it off** stops it. It goes off if you lose the radio, die or are taken into custody. Playing Freq 7 out loud where a unit can see you (about 300 units) makes you wanted for listening to an illegal broadcast.

**Freq 7, the Resistance's pirate broadcast.** "Frequency 7" is a rule, not a number: the Resistance only broadcast where the digits add up to seven, and there are exactly seven such frequencies on the dial (**100.6, 101.5, 102.4, 103.3, 104.2, 105.1, 106.0**). It's one station: every radio on it hears the same thing at the same moment, a **voice line, then a quiet spell** (just static, about `lc_freq7_quiet` seconds, 120) so the band stays clear for people talking on it, then another voice line. No music by default: `lc_freq7_songs 1` puts songs back in the gaps. When something's coming for the Resistance, a **warning cuts in** at once, even mid-line (two minutes before a sweep, a block raid, curfew or a supply convoy; when Civil Protection sweep into rebel territory, a lockdown falls or an Overwatch dropship comes in), and a quiet spell follows it. **The Combine hunt it:** after a while on one frequency it's located and jammed (a harsh whine there, units told on their net), the broadcast goes dark for a minute or so, then comes back on another of the seven; a jammed frequency stays dead for `lc_freq7_jammed` minutes (8). How long it lasts depends on the city's mood: about `lc_freq7_hold` minutes (18) on green, 60% of that on yellow, 30% on red. **Jamming runs on city power:** in a blackout the hunt stops and Freq 7 holds where it is (a jam in progress lifts). A retuned (Resistance) radio follows each hop by itself; a Union radio has to be retuned by hand. Recordings go in the sound addon's `radio/` folder: `freq7_1.wav`... (voice lines), `freq7_song_<anything>.ogg` (songs: `.ogg` or `.wav`, so the server can tell how long they run; public domain recordings, such as anything published before 1926), `freq7_sweep1.wav`, `freq7_raid1.wav`, `freq7_curfew1.wav`, `freq7_supply1.wav`, `freq7_incursion1.wav`, `freq7_lockdown1.wav`, `freq7_dropship1.wav` (the warnings) and `jam1.wav` (the whine; a stock sound otherwise). `lc_freq7_status` (admin) says where it is and what's on.

The Resistance and Civil Protection have their own (on their boards):

- **Doctor the records** (Resistance Operations): get into a civil records terminal (or a Combine console). Stop the needle in the band four times; every hit makes it faster and the band narrower, and a miss loses one. A scanner comes over every few seconds (the panel warns you): touch the panel while it's overhead and it sees you, twice and the alarm goes and you're wanted. "Step away from it" closes the panel; E brings it back. Done clean, it wipes a wanted comrade's record.
- **Dead drop** (Resistance Operations): after the contact, three hiding places near the drop. E on one, then crouch beside it and keep still. Anyone who sees you at it (a unit from further away, a citizen up close) burns that place; try another.
- **Civil status inspections** (Civil Protection, during sweeps): a **pat-down**. E on a citizen stops them; pat them down a place at a time (coat, pockets, waistband, boots, bag). Watch their hands: what they do usually says where. Each empty place costs them nerve, and with none left they bolt. Then release, confiscate (once you've found it) or detain: right calls pay, detaining someone clean is docked, and letting contraband walk is a miss.
- **Housing block inspection** (Civil Protection, during block raids): a **breach**. E on the block's door sets a charge; it blows the door open. Then everyone on the floor (E on each) before they run out the back or burn the evidence, and search the furniture (E) for contraband.

Whatever a game wants you to look at is outlined: hiding places, residents, furniture to search, the body on the slab. If you close a game's panel, E (on whatever you're working on) brings it back.

`lc_jobgames 0` (editor panel, Survival and economy) puts the old shifts back.

Place each job's workplace from the editor's "Workplaces" section. Shifts pay Credits and Civil Standing, and many carry small choices: the mother with one ration card, the beaten man who begs you to write "fell down the stairs". The newer jobs are also where contraband comes from, if you want it: a dead man's papers from the morgue, a suitcase that's heavier than clothes, a pistol under the rubble. (Blank forms, uniforms and Freq 7 radios come from the black market, the dead, scavenging and the Resistance lab.)

### Escorts through Combine doors

A Union worker whose job is behind Combine doors (a generator in the nexus, a fuse box in the precinct) asks for a **Civil Protection escort**: the actions menu (Getting by > Ask for a Civil Protection escort), or `!escort`. You have to be employed and on a job (a shift or assignment you've accepted). The nearest free unit comes for you, or a player unit takes it on from their own menu (Civil Protection > Escort requests, or "Escort them" when they look at you).

With your escort beside you, **Combine doors open for you** (E: the unit buzzes you through), forcefields let you by, and you aren't trespassing in Combine-only areas. The unit follows you about and opens the doors it walks through. Your HUD shows who's escorting you.

The unit watches the doors it opens. If **someone slips through behind you** (a rebel, anyone with no business in there), it breaks off and goes after them, and they're wanted for unauthorized access. If **a prisoner gets out** through a door it opened, it goes and marches them back to their cell. Either way it radios that you're unattended, and the nearest free unit comes to take over (you're still covered while it comes, `lc_escort_handover` seconds). It goes on your file and costs you standing; **twice on one job and dispatch won't send anyone else with you** for `lc_escort_ban_minutes` (10). A unit called away by anything else (a crime nearby, being shot at) hands over the same way. Walk away from your escort for fifteen seconds and you're on your own (trespassing, if you're inside).

**When your job's done**, your escort walks you out to the **release point** (the way out of the prison, outside the Combine area) and leaves you there. With no release point on the map, they see you out of the Combine area; if you never went through a Combine door, they just go back to their beat. Player units can hand an escort back to dispatch from their menu.

(Half-Life 2's own "follow me" squads only work for rebels, not Civil Protection, so units follow the city's own way, the same as a Resistance fighter does.) `lc_escorts 0` turns escorts off; `lc_escort_range` (6000) is how far a unit is sent from.

### The city does its own work

The city's own people have these jobs too, and when no player is doing the work, **they do it, with the same effects**: nobody walks across the map for nothing. **Every job is staffed:** at least one of the city's people works each Union job in a city of about a dozen, rising to four each from about a hundred (the roster plus the players), filled from the unemployed first and then from the most crowded trades; and a trade nobody on the streets is doing sends its people out first, so the city keeps itself running. When one of them picks what to do next, it's nearly always their work (`lc_npc_work_pct`, 85):

| Job | What the city's workers do |
|---|---|
| Maintenance | Fix broken streetlights; otherwise, the maintenance yard |
| Radio Repair | Fix broken loudspeakers; otherwise, the repair bench |
| Sanitation | Scrub graffiti, pick up empty cans and bottles left in the street, empty the bins and take the bags to the depot |
| Mortuary | Haul the dead to the incinerator (dragged, like a player does it); bag dead headcrabs left lying about a minute and a half |
| Clinic Medic | Run to a citizen who's down and get them back on their feet (not the wanted, not in a fight on rebel, infested or Combine ground), patch up wounded citizens, and at the clinic see to any player standing with them (+10 health a few seconds apart) |
| Ration Production | Carry crates of sealed packs in from the loading dock; work the sealing line |
| Factory | Carry parts from the dock and between stations, work the line |
| Courier | Collect the mail at the post office and carry it to people's doors |
| Intake Processor | Carry luggage from the platform to the housing blocks |
| Records Clerk | File at the records office (at a desk, if there's a chair near it), take papers to the checkpoint |
| Laundry | Run the machines, take pressed uniforms to the checkpoint |
| Reclamation | Clear rubble piles, carry the scrap to the reclamation point |

And **Civil Protection units take back a hijacked city screen** if no player unit does it first.

**How they carry things.** A box or a crate is held out in front in both hands, the arms bent under it; luggage hangs from a hand at the side; small things (a clipboard, a can) are in the hand.

**Players come first.** Something broken waits `lc_npc_work_grace` seconds (60) for a player before the city's crew comes for it, or `lc_npc_work_grace_players` (300) while someone with that job is on the server, so their shifts always have work. Two of the city's workers never go after the same thing. What a citizen is doing shows under their name when you look at them ("Maintenance Technician, fixing a streetlight"), and what they carry is in their hands (a crate, a letter, a suitcase, a bag of rubbish). The things they need are placed with the editor: workplaces, loading docks, rubble piles, the incinerator, bins; whatever a map hasn't got, that part of the work doesn't happen. `lc_npc_work 0` turns it off; `lc_npcwork` (admin) says how it's going, and so does the debug report.

### The city's stores

The rations the dispensers hand out come out of the city's stores (`lc_stock_max`, 400 rations), shown to everyone under the clock. **In:** a Ration Production shift (+12) or a factory shift (+8) worked by a player, the city's own ration and factory workers (a few for each task they finish), a train's freight (+15), a supply convoy (+15 for every load that wasn't stolen). **Out:** every ration handed out, and the city eating (one a minute for every `lc_stock_eat` (40) citizens out; with every fifteen, as it was, a city of 80 ate more than it could ever make). **Low** (under a quarter): allocations come half again as slowly, the dispenser is sometimes empty, the PA says so, and hungry people listen to the Resistance (rebel ground spreads faster). **Empty:** no rations at all (a forged card won't help either) until something comes in. **Well stocked** (over four fifths): the city's calmer. Saved per map. `lc_stockpile 0` makes rations endless again; `lc_stock [n]` (admin) shows or sets it. Editor panel, Survival and economy: the stores on or off, how much they hold, how fast the city eats, and **Fill the city's ration stores**. A dispenser that turns you away (a shortfall, too soon) doesn't try again by itself while you stand there: step away from the counter and come back.

## Survival

- **Hunger and thirst** drain while the city runs. At zero you start dying. On the quiet HUD the bars show once either is under half (or for a moment after you eat or drink). Prisoners are fed; Overwatch and the Infested don't eat.
- **Pockets** (F2): eat, drink, cook, fill, drop. What you carry is limited by **weight and size**: `lc_carry_kg` (12 kg) and `lc_carry_bulky` (1 large thing: a shotgun, an SMG, a uniform, a smuggled crate, a melon). A **satchel** (Commissary, black market) adds 8 kg and one more large thing while you have it. Carry more than you can manage (say, after putting the satchel down) and you're slowed until you drop something. Weights are in `LCS.Weights` in `gamemodes/livingcity/gamemode/modules/shared/sh_livingcity_shop.lua`. `lc_inventory_weight 0` goes back to counting items (`lc_inventory_size`, 12).
- **Places to keep things.** Looking at a bin or dumpster, your pockets screen shows what's in it: hide things there, and anyone who looks can take them (a dead drop; thrown out after `lc_bin_minutes`, 40). **Lockers** are placed with the editor ("Fixture: locker"): press E, put something in and it's yours, locked to everyone else; give it up when it's empty. A **storage crate** (Commissary) is set down from your pockets ("Set down") and opens only for you; pack it up when it's empty (two each, `lc_crates_per_person`). Units can open any locker or crate: they see what's in it and confiscate the contraband, and the owner is wanted and it goes on their record. Lockers and crates keep their contents across restarts (`<map>_storage.json`). Capacity: `lc_bin_kg` 10, `lc_locker_kg` 25, `lc_crate_kg` 35.
- **Water:** wade in and press E to drink. Untreated water can give you **parasites** (hungrier, thirstier, coughing; each cough costs health, never armor).
- **Toilets.** Parasites also build an **urge** (`lc_urge_minutes`, 6, from nothing to can't-hold-it; a line under your needs says how it's going, and What now? points to the nearest toilet). Sit on a toilet (E on it: the map's own, or the editor's "Fixture: toilet"; eight seconds) and it's gone, for now (a splash, then the flush: your own recording, if you put one at `garrysmod/sound/livingcity/flush1.wav`), but it **dehydrates you**: drink something after. Only antiparasitic tablets end it for good. **Leave it too long and you soil yourself**: a stain on your trousers and a mess on the ground under you. Then you're **filthy** until you've done all three: the toilet, **a wash** (E on a bathtub or a shower, the map's or the editor's "Fixture: bathtub"; ten seconds) and **clean clothes** (spare citizen clothes or Union overalls from your pockets: put them on, or What now? > Change into clean clothes). Filthy, you stink: flies follow you, the city's own people step back from you, players near you are told why, and anyone who stays close may catch your parasites (`lc_stink_spread`, 3% every five seconds). The **mess on the ground** stays until it's scrubbed: E on it (five seconds; a Sanitation Worker is paid 5 credits, and is told when there's a new one; anyone who does it risks catching something), or the city's own sanitation crew walks over and scrubs it. `lc_hygiene 0` turns it all off.
- **Sinks** the map has work with E even when the map gives them nothing solid to aim at (many do); one the other side of a wall doesn't.
- **Sleep.** Everyone needs a night's sleep, once in every in-game day (`lc_tired_days`, 1). Go without and you're **tired**: your eyelids keep drooping shut (all the way, a moment, then open with a start), the world swims a little, you can't run, and you've 25 less health at most until you sleep. **Sleep in a bed** (E on it: the editor's "Fixture: bed", a bunk point, or any bed or mattress the map has, a cell's too) at night, or any time you're exhausted: you lie still (you can't move; **Jump** gets you up), the screen dark, and heal as you sleep, to full by the end of `lc_sleep_seconds` (60); then you stay asleep till you press Jump. Get up before then, or get hurt, and you've not slept. The drooping eyelids have soft edges, and the world blurs as they close. How long you've been awake is kept over a server restart. A good night leaves you **well rested** for the next in-game day: hunger and thirst come on a quarter more slowly. What now? tells you when you need sleep and finds a bed. `lc_sleep 0` turns it off. They never wear off by themselves: only antiparasitic tablets from a medic or the black market dealer clear them. A sink only counts if you can see it: a sink behind a wall (the other side of a ladder, say) is not one you can drink from.
- **Breen's Private Reserve** from the vending machines quenches thirst and leaves you dizzy. Sometimes you forget what you were doing, and so does your quest tracker. You keep the empty can.
- **A full stomach** (hunger 85 or more): jobs and shifts give 15% more XP. `lc_fullstomach 0` turns it off.
- **Rain** washes blood, scorch marks and bullet holes off open ground near you while it rains (`lc_rain_wash`, 1; never graffiti, and nothing under a roof).
- **Healing** comes only from sleeping (in a bed), eating (food mends you as you eat it, a quarter of what it fills you: a ration, 40 hunger, is 10 health; drinks don't) and medicine (medkits, bandages, the clinic, Father Grigori). Nobody mends on their own while awake.
- **Bottles and cans:** fill them in water, then boil them over a stove or fire.
- **Hunting:** birds and headcrabs leave carcasses. Cook them at a stove or burning barrel (any stove, oven or fire already on the map works too). Raw meat is a gamble.
- **Stoves, sinks and vending machines** the map already has work as fixtures, including ones baked into the map as static props: the game reads their positions straight from the map file. Stand by a stove to cook (F2). Press E at a sink you are looking at to drink (a small parasite risk) and fill bottles and cans from your pockets, then boil them. Press E at a vending machine to buy a can. The map's **dumpsters and bins** are read the same way: jobs send you to real ones (the Union bin round, and "Something you saw", where two people are swapping a bundle behind a dumpster; if the map has none nearby, a dumpster is put out for it), and a can put in one counts as put in the trash. `lc_scanprops` rescans the map and says what it found.
- **Fists:** everyone has them. One punch kills a bird. A headcrab takes several. People barely notice. Don't go AFK in an alley with headcrabs around.
- **Ration dispensers** give a ration packet once per allocation (twice as often during distribution), unless your standing is too low.

## Social credit

**Getting your standing back:** anyone below zero gets **community service** on their board (F3), no job needed: litter sweeps and bin rounds that restore 40 standing each. Serving time also pays it back: a quarter of what you owe plus 100, and 75 more if you turned yourself in. It never takes you past zero, though. Quiet compliance adds 10 every 10 minutes while you're below zero.

**Credits** are money: earned from shifts, jobs and ration coupons, spent at the Commissary and vending machines. **Civil Standing** runs from -1000 to 1000: work, compliance and reporting raise it; being wanted, jailed, escaping, curfew violations and conduct strikes lower it. Both are at the top of your pockets (F2), and on the HUD too if the server turns the quiet HUD off. Changes are told in one line once things go quiet for a moment, added up, with what each offence came to ("-150: Assaulting Civil Protection (x5, -125), Murder of a Civil Protection officer (-25)"), not one line per offence. Your tier (Anticitizen, Probationary, Citizen, Trusted, Exemplary) decides what the **Union Commissary** will sell you, from rations and water for everyone, to medkits for the Trusted, to watermelon and real coffee for Exemplary citizens. Units can buy ammunition and batteries. Edit `gamemodes/livingcity/gamemode/modules/shared/sh_livingcity_shop.lua` for items, prices and tiers.

## Your characters

You can have more than one person in the city (`lc_char_slots`, 3). `!characters` lists them: switch to one, start someone new in an empty slot, or delete one you're not playing. Each is a whole life of its own: name, number and face, description, who they know, how long they've lived in the city, credits, standing, faction and rank in every faction, job, pockets, stash, housing block, story and traits, and their Civil Protection record. Switching is like one person walking off and another walking on: you respawn as them. Not while you're wanted, in custody, down, tied up, in a fight or on a job, and not more than once every `lc_char_switch_cooldown` seconds (300; admins can switch freely). Permanent death still applies to whoever you're playing; the others aren't touched. What belongs to you rather than the person stays the same whoever you play: conduct strikes, tips you've seen, whitelists. (A stash is kept per map, and only this map's is swapped.)

## Talking, knowing people, and going down

**Chat is in character and spoken out loud.** Ordinary chat only reaches people within `lc_chat_range` (350 units).
- `/y` yells further; `/w` whispers to whoever is right next to you.
- `/me` describes an action (`/me leans against the wall.`); `/it` describes the scene (`/it A siren starts up somewhere.`).
- `/roll` rolls 1-100 for everyone near.
- `//` or `/ooc` is out of character, server-wide, with a cooldown (`lc_ooc_cooldown`, admins exempt; `lc_ooc 0` turns it off). `/looc` is out of character, nearby only.
- `!` commands still go to the game as before. A mistyped `/` command is swallowed, not said out loud.
- **Voice** is proximity too: `lc_voice_range` (900 units, 0 for everyone hears everyone).
- `lc_ic_chat 0` turns all of this off.

**Recognition.** Strangers don't know your name: they hear and see your description instead ("A man in Union blue").
- `/chardesc <text>` sets your description (8 to 120 characters).
- `/recognize` introduces you to the person you're looking at; `/recognize all` to everyone in earshot. They hear you by name from then on, and nametags follow the same rules.
- Who knows whom is remembered across sessions. A new character (after a permanent death) starts with nobody known.
- Units are known by their designation while masked.

**Acts.** Poses you hold: `/sit`, `/kneel`, `/lie`, `/lean`, `/cower`, `/pose 1-4` (`/stand`, or just move, to get up), and `!handsup`. Gestures: `/wave`, `/salute`, `/bow`, `/nod`, `/shake`, `/point`, `/beckon`, `/halt`, `/cheer`, `/laugh`. All work with `!` too. While you hold a pose the camera pulls back so you can see yourself (`lc_act_thirdperson 0` keeps it first person). Each pose tries a list of animations, the GMod player ones first, then Half-Life 2's; a model with none of them still holds you still and says so. `lc_acts 0` turns them off.

**Going down instead of dying.** A killing blow puts a player on the ground, bleeding out, for `lc_downed_secs` (90 seconds).
- Downed players can only whisper.
- Press E on a downed body to help them up: 8 seconds bare-handed (back at 10 health), 4 with a bandage (back at 30).
- A unit pressing E on a wanted person cuffs them where they lie.
- Shooting or hitting a downed body finishes them. Damage of 300 or more (explosions and the like) kills outright.
- `!drag` while looking at a downed body drags it along; `!drag` again lets go.
- `!giveup` lets go if nobody is coming: your body stays where it lies (no second ragdoll), and the screen goes to the death view.
- Zombies and build mode skip all this. `lc_downed 0` turns it off.

## Tips as you play

So nobody has to read all this first, the game explains itself as it happens. The first time you run into something, a small card slides in on the right of the screen saying what it is and what you can do with it, using your own key bindings. Each stays up 14 to 28 seconds, depending on its length. The getting-started tips come half a minute apart; tips about what's around you come as you meet things. Each tip shows once per player and is remembered across sessions; a few (hungry, thirsty, hurt) come back now and then. One shows at a time, and anything urgent (you've gone down, you're wanted, a unit is stopping you) goes straight to the front.

- **What happens to you:** going down, getting back up, wanted, the escalation levels, stop and search, being told to stop running, custody, release, escape, curfew warnings, putting on a disguise, a blown cover, a conduct strike, a new character, promotions, your first ration, making a gun.
- **What happens around you:** rations, inspection sweeps, block raids, curfew, lockdown, rebel raids and CP sweeps, an outbreak, the city tipping into unrest or full compliance. Units and citizens are told different things.
- **What's near you:** someone down (if you can see them), a body, bins, water, sinks, stoves, vending machines, ration dispensers, the Commissary, the black market dealer, the quartermaster and his fighters, rebel supply crates and pots, power banks and turrets, graffiti, citizens, units, someone wanted (for units), strangers, birds, headcrabs, rebel ground, the infestation, your own block, a Combine-only zone, a gun out while in plain clothes, the Grenades weapon in your hand.
- **What's in your pockets:** seeds, a fishing line, a spray can, a lockpick, a bypass charge, a keycard, raw meat, empty or untreated water, clothes, a uniform, Resistance gear, papers, a blank form, a forged ration card, a radio, a permit, materials, a bandage, guns and ammo, a smuggled crate, grenades, tablets, a wedding ring.
- **How you're doing:** hungry, thirsty, parasites, hurt, standing below zero, or high enough to join Civil Protection.

`!hints` shows the last few again in chat, `!hints off` and `!hints on` turn them off and on for you, and `!hints reset` starts them over. `lc_hints 0` turns them off for the whole server. The tips themselves are a table at the top of `gamemodes/livingcity/gamemode/modules/server/sv_livingcity_hints.lua`, easy to reword or add to.

## Homes, trade and the written word

**Apartments.** Tag a door "Door: apartment people can rent" with the editor. Look at it and it says who holds it, or that it is available and what it costs. **No commands needed:** with your hands out (nothing in them), right click at a free door and a yes or no asks whether to rent it; once it is yours, right click locks and unlocks it. The commands still work: look at it and `!rent`: `lc_rent_price` credits (15) for `lc_rent_hours` (3) hours of real time; `!rent` again extends it, up to a day ahead. A double door is rented together. It's locked to everyone but you and whoever you `!givekey <name>` (`!takekey` to take it back). **Locked is locked, for you too:** pressing E on your own locked door just rattles the handle; unlock it first (right click it with your hands out, or `!unlock`). `!lock` and `!unlock` at the door, `!apartment` for where it is and how long is left, `!unrent` to move out. Units force their way in (E, three seconds). One each (`lc_rent_max`). Rentals are kept across restarts (`<map>_rentals.json`); when the rent runs out the apartment is free again.

**Trade.** A **CWU business license** (Commissary, Civil Standing 50) lets you set down a **market stall** (Commissary) and sell from it. Look at your stall and open your pockets: Sell puts one of something on it at the price you name, Price changes it, Take takes it back. Customers press E on the stall and buy; the money waits in the till until you collect it. An officer can revoke a license from the records terminal: it's taken, and the trader's stalls close until they have a new one. Units can search a stall like a crate. One stall each (`lc_stalls_per_person`), `lc_stall_kg` 30.

**Notes and posters.** Paper (Commissary, or bins): Write from your pockets. Leave it on the ground as a note, put it up on the wall you're looking at as a poster, or keep it (up to `lc_notes_kept`, 8; your pockets list them). Sign it or don't. Anyone presses E on a note or poster to read it and take it. Units, and whoever put it up, tear posters down. They stay up `lc_poster_hours` (12), across restarts; three each at a time (`lc_posters_per_person`).

**Letters.** A note you're carrying can be sent as a letter: read it from your pockets and **Send as a letter**, to anyone the city knows (who's here first, then whoever was here most recently). Hand it in at the **post office** (the Courier's workplace; on a map without one, anywhere). After `lc_mail_minutes` (4) of sorting it goes out for delivery: a **Courier** carries it to the recipient's housing block (a player Courier gets "The real mail" shift, paid for each letter; the city's own couriers carry it too), and it turns up in their pockets with a note in chat. If nobody takes it, the Union's van does, eventually; if they aren't in the city, it waits for them. Signed or not, **the post office knows who posted it**: while a letter is waiting there, **Civil Protection can read the mail** (actions menu > Civil Protection > Read the mail, at the post office), see who wrote what to whom, and **confiscate** any of it. The sender never knows.

**The Administrator's broadcasts.** The City Administrator (or an admin, or Civil Protection from Divisional Leader up) speaks to the whole city: `!broadcast <text>`. It crosses everyone's screen, goes into chat, and stays on every **city screen** (editor: "Fixture: city screen") until the next one.

**The law.** `!laws` shows the city's laws, and so does any **law board** (editor: "Fixture: law board"; press E). The Administrator and admins write them, from that screen or with `!addlaw <text>`, `!setlaw <n> <text>`, `!dellaw <n>`. They're kept in `garrysmod/data/livingcity/laws.json`, the same on every map.

## Vortigaunts

**Freed vortigaunts** live in rebel territory: `lc_rebel_vorts` (1) of them from the start, more when the Resistance free one (the "Free a vortigaunt" job). They charge a Resistance vest when asked (E). **Shackled vortigaunts** are their own faction ("Vortigaunt", ranks Laborer, Trusted Laborer, Overseer's Hand): Combine property, working the Union's shifts like citizens, with a collar that holds their vortessence in (the Vortessence weapon only hums). A Resistance Member or above can cut the shackles: `!unshackle`, looking at one up close, ten seconds, with no unit watching. The vortigaunt joins the Resistance as a **Free Vortigaunt** (two at a time): left click throws a bolt of vortessence (25 shock damage, a three-second recharge), right click charges the powered vest of the person in front of you, or your own (25, every 15 seconds; it charges a vest, it doesn't heal flesh), and Civil Protection hunt it on sight outside rebel territory. Vortigaunts use the vortigaunt model with its own standing, walking and running animations; strangers see "a shackled vortigaunt" or "a vortigaunt" rather than a name. The "Free a vortigaunt" job: it waits at the work site shackled and harmless, with its keepers. Cut the shackles there (they may see), or walk it out still shackled as if you were moving Combine property (a unit may stop you and ask where you're taking it: then it's a fight), and the shackles come off in rebel territory, any of it.

## Day, night and weather

- **Night maps.** On a map that's always night (its sky says so: `lc_daynight -1`, the default, decides from the map's sky; `lc_daynight 0` forces it), the map's lighting is left alone, the screen isn't tinted, and there's no overcast weather (it comes as fog instead). The clock still runs, for curfews and the like.
- **Day and night.** A city day lasts `lc_day_minutes` (60) real minutes, with the clock on everyone's HUD. Night (21:00 to 05:00) is dark: the map's own lighting dims at dawn, day, dusk and night (`lc_daynight_light`; clients have a short hitch when it changes, four times a day), and the screen is tinted. Curfew is three times as likely at night, rations half as likely.
- **The city's day.** The city keeps to the clock (`lc_daily_rhythm`, on). Morning (05:00 to 07:00): people come out and go to work, about seven in ten of `lc_citizens` out. The work day (07:00 to 12:00) and afternoon (14:00 to 18:00): everyone's out and most of the employed are at their jobs. Midday (12:00 to 14:00): the ration lines fill up. Evening (18:00 to 21:00): work winds down, people sit, talk and wander. Night (21:00 to 05:00): most go home, `lc_night_population` (40%) of `lc_citizens` stay out, and only the night shift works (maintenance, radio repair, the mortuary, the clinic, sanitation). When there are more people out than the hour calls for, some head home. The HUD clock says what time of day it is.
- **Weather.** Clear, overcast, fog, rain or a storm, changing by itself every so often (`lc_weather_auto`). Rain and storms fall around you outdoors (thin streaks, lots of them), with the sound, and a mist closes in around you while you're out under the open sky, clearing again a couple of seconds after you step under cover; storms bring thunder and lightning (the flash only where you can see the sky; indoors you just hear the thunder); fog closes in. Standing out in the rain with an empty bottle or can fills it with rainwater, clean enough to drink as it is (no boiling).
- **Music.** The city's music now and then: calm by day, tense under curfew or yellow sociostability, urgent in a fight or a red one. A lockdown starts its music the moment it begins. Urgent music (an alarm, a fight) fades in about five seconds after it starts. The music plays at 80% of `lc_music_volume`. Players: `lc_music 0` turns it off, `lc_music_volume` sets the level.
- `!time` tells you the time and the weather. Admins: `lc_time <hour>`, `lc_weather <clear|overcast|fog|rain|storm|auto>`. `lc_atmosphere 0` turns day, night and weather off.

## Staff tools

**Safeguards for a public server.** A player's game can send the server messages (from windows, menus and chat) as fast as it likes, so the server keeps everyone to a sensible pace: each kind of message `lc_net_rate` a second (10, in bursts of up to `lc_net_burst`, 25), all of them together four times that; anything more is ignored, and someone who keeps at it (`lc_net_kick`, 400 ignored in ten seconds) is kicked, with a line in the staff log. Chat is `lc_chat_flood` lines in three seconds (6), then "Slow down". Things dropped from your pockets: `lc_drops_kept` (30) of your own on the ground at once, then the oldest is cleared away. Two people pressing E on the same thing at the same moment: only one gets it. Nothing at the Resistance lab costs less than it sells back for (no credits out of thin air), and cuffs sell for 1 (units buy them three for 6). A new name and face (the registration form) can't be had while you're wanted or in custody. The day's log file is written every few seconds instead of once a line. Staff are never held back by any of it, and nobody playing normally comes near it. Admin-only menus, settings and commands already check who's asking, building (props, physgun, tools, noclip) is for build mode only, and no player can name a file the server reads or writes.

`!staff` opens the staff panel (admins).

- **Logs.** Chat (in and out of character), kills and who did them, arrests, wanted, warnings, tickets, staff actions and connections. The last 2000 lines are in the panel, and every line also goes to `garrysmod/data/livingcity/logs/<date>.txt`.
- **Tickets.** A player asks for help with `@ <message>` (or `!ticket <message>`). Staff online hear it; the panel lists open tickets to claim, answer, go to, bring the player, and close. Another `@` adds to your open ticket (one every three seconds, thirty to a ticket, so nobody can flood staff).
- **Warnings.** `!warn <name> <reason>`. The player sees it across their screen; it's kept (`garrysmod/data/livingcity/warnings.json`) and listed in the panel, where it can be struck off.
- **Character bans.** `!charban <name> <reason>`: that person, not the player, is gone for good. The character is wiped as if they'd died, and nobody can take the name again. Lifted from the panel.

## Contraband, searches and the underground

**Stop and search.** Civil Protection NPCs stop people and frisk them. It happens sometimes at checkpoints, rarely on the street, and constantly during inspection sweeps. Each person has a cooldown, so on a big server it doesn't keep landing on the same few people. When you're stopped, stand still. Walk off and you're wanted for evading inspection. They check your papers, then your pockets. Each illegal item has a chance to slip past (Guile improves it). Anything they find is confiscated, and you lose standing and become wanted. Units (players) search people with the Arrest Baton (reload) or `!search`, and get credits and XP for what they seize. That works on the city's NPC citizens too: press E to stop one, R to pat them down (sympathizers are much likelier to be carrying something), and right click to detain them. A detained citizen is cuffed for a moment and then put straight in a free cell, the same way an arrested player is, since cells are behind doors that lock; they're let out at the release point when their time is up. Getting arrested confiscates everything illegal you were carrying.

**Stash.** Eight hidden slots in your own housing block (`!stash`, or F2 while you're home, or E on a stove or your own fridge there). A street search doesn't reach it, but a raid on your block can. A Freq 7 radio warns you before a sweep.

**Keeping out of sight.** Crouch to sneak. Crouched, a Civil Protection unit or soldier sees you at half the distance, creeping slowly a little less, and in the dark less again (your own game measures how lit you are, and the server checks it: a flashlight on, or open sky over you in the daytime, is never the dark); and crouched, he doesn't see you behind his back. While you crouch, a bar at the bottom of your screen says how easily you're seen ("A UNIT CAN SEE YOU" when one does), and each unit near you has his sight painted on the ground (red if you're in it; `lc_stealth_cones 0` hides it, your own setting). It counts for everything units notice: thefts, looting a supply drop, a curfew. `lc_stealth 0` turns it off. The sight is shown in 3D: on the ground (up slopes and steps), with see-through walls about waist high and a lid, so it shows from below too.

**Running from the law.** Someone wanted who has hurt nobody is run down and cuffed: units (soldiers too) hold their fire and lay hands on you. The violent (you hurt someone in the last two minutes, are wanted for something violent, have a gun in hand, or are at a sector-wide search or above) get shot at. A curfew or lockdown called while you're wanted, or still hot from the trouble, is about you: the first unit who sees you knows you, and won't stop at a warning. A curfew in the daytime only comes as a punishment for unrest, and says so.

**Black market.** Place a dealer with the editor, or on maps without one, trade in rebel territory (`!market`). The dealer sells contraband and a crowbar, and the finished goods nobody but the Resistance lab makes (ammunition, homemade guns, armour vests, smoke bombs, jammers: everyone who isn't Resistance buys them here), and buys scrap, wedding rings, uniforms and spare papers. **Selling:** the dealer buys nearly everything in your pockets (40% of the cheapest shop price, so there's no profit in reselling; scrap, rings, radios and the like have their own prices) and the weapons you carry, unless your faction issued them to you. There's a "sell all" button for stacks.

**Lockpicks:** besides buying one from the dealer, you can **make one from four pieces of scrap** (F2, "Make pick"; scrap comes from the Reclamation job). Use it by looking at a locked door and typing `!pick`, or bind a key: `bind p lc_pick`. `!pick` also uses a keycard or bypass charge on a Combine door.

A unit who walks up to the dealer busts it: it vanishes for 15 minutes, the unit gets paid, and Freq 7 hears about it. Edit `gamemodes/livingcity/gamemode/modules/shared/sh_livingcity_shop.lua` for prices and items.

Admin helpers: `lc_giveitem <name> <item> [count]`, `lc_search <name>`.

## Conduct

Every player kill is judged; unjustified ones earn strikes (2: prison, 4: kick), and leaving mid-fight (or wanted, or being chased) is remembered, but only if you chose to leave: a server restart or map change, a crash or dropped connection, or a kick isn't held against anyone. Admins clear someone's strikes from the admin tools (Handy > Clear conduct strikes) or with `lc_pardon <name>`. New spawns get 8 seconds of protection. Logged to `garrysmod/data/livingcity/conduct_log.txt`.

## Secret endings

Joke "end of the city" events, mostly about the motherlode (the master data core). **Nobody really dies and nothing is lost:** characters, Civil Standing and credits carry on (the usual inheritance caveat applies), nobody can be hurt while one runs, and afterwards the map reloads with its Living City layout.

**How every one goes.** Something triggers it. A few seconds later (`lc_ending_pause`, 4) **the G-Man stops time**: everyone freezes, every NPC stops, the colour drains out of the world, the G-Man is standing right in front of each person (on their own screen, whichever way they were looking: one each, only they see theirs; the view turns to him and stays on him for the vote; he idles, straightens his tie now and then, and his mouth moves as he speaks), the city's announcements stop (and stay quiet until it's over), his end-of-Half-Life-2 lines play for everyone (`lc_ending_gman_lines`, the game's own `vo/citadel/gman_exit01.wav` and `02` by default), and a vote comes up on every screen: **1** to let it happen, **2** to skip it (`lc_ending_vote_secs`, 30). It plays out with its song until everyone has been "taken" (or `lc_ending_max`, 180 seconds), the screen goes white, and the map reloads. One ending, or one skipped, every `lc_ending_cooldown` minutes (60), remembered across the reload. The city's events wait while one is on.

**Photosensitivity.** The endings with strobes or screen flashes (`bus`, `bloom`, `marrow`, `base`, `hard`, `water`, `plane`) warn on the G-Man's vote screen and again with a popup as they start: Reduce flashing, or I'm fine (the default: doing nothing for 15 seconds keeps it). `lc_ending_calm 1` (each player's own, remembered) turns the strobes into a steady glow and leaves out the screen flashes, the lightning and the static; the music, the dancing and everything else stay.

## How the code is laid out

**For whoever works on the code (people or AI):** `CLAUDE.md` and `CORE.md` at the top of the repo say what must not change without the owner's OK and how the owner's server is actually set up; the test suite checks a snapshot of the core (`_dev/core_snapshot.txt`) and fails when it changes.

## Big servers

`lc_npc_budget` caps NPCs (40 to 60 is sensible for a full server). Set lower than what the city's settings add up to (citizens, units, soldiers and the rest), nothing more spawns, Civil Protection included: a superadmin is told so (every ten minutes while it's holding things back) and the debug report's setup notes say so too. The Combine loom: Civil Protection NPCs are drawn 5% bigger than HL2's (`lc_cp_scale` 1.05) and Overwatch soldiers, who hunch as they walk, 10% (`lc_soldier_scale` 1.10); only how they look, not the room they need (editor panel sliders). NPCs far from every player, out of sight and doing something calm sleep (`lc_npc_sleep`, editor panel): the engine stops running their AI until someone comes near, sees them or hurts them, and one walking somewhere is moved along their route out of sight. The NPC loop runs ten times a second and spends at most `lc_think_budget_ms` (2.5 ms) each time, carrying on next time with whoever it didn't reach, so a city of a hundred NPCs is spread across frames instead of landing on one (on a listen server that was a hitch twice a second). Lower it for smoother frames; raise it if NPCs seem slow to react. Distant NPCs think less often, and relationships only touch the engine when something changes. The city keeps its own entities, zones and points indexed by kind (nothing searches every entity in the map in a loop), each player is looked at once per relationship pass rather than once per NPC, doors are on a grid so walkers far from any door aren't checked, hints are spread over the second, quest trackers are only re-sent when something on them changes, and the busiest screen effects (scanlines, rain, streetlights, the broadcast picture at `lc_livecam_fps` 12) draw without making anything new each frame. NPC stops are capped at a few at a time (one per 16 players online), and each person has a cooldown (`lc_stop_cooldown`, 8 minutes by default). The debug report includes timings.

## When something breaks

Run **Copy debug report** in the editor panel (or `lc_debugreport`). It copies settings, layout, every NPC's state, jobs in progress, player status, timings and recent errors to your clipboard, and saves a copy in `garrysmod/data/livingcity/reports/`. It says why people have been leaving the city since the map started (their time out was up, `lc_citizen_lifetime`; a curfew; the time of day; their ration had; prison), and for each ration line whether its dispenser has power (nobody queues for a dead one) and whether the line was turned round because it was placed from the back. It also lists setup notes, such as a missing prison, missing doorways, or a ration line with no dispenser at its front. Paste it to Claude.

## Honest limits

The server logic and the gamemode's rules are exercised by 1925 automated scenario tests, and the client HUD, menus, editor overlay and every entity's drawing by a smoke test, all against a simulated Garry's Mod (see `_dev/`). Real-game playtesting has only just started, so expect rough edges in anything visual or audible: animations, sounds, menus, HUD layout, and entity models. Send the debug report and they'll get fixed.

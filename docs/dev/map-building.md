# Building pieces of map (`_dev/tools/map_build.py`, `map_forest.py`, `map_ring.py`, `map_raven.py`)

The owner, 19 November: "It is SO labor intensive to do all the menial map building ... I wanted to add more of an 'edge of city' or even outside of city area to the map for the resistance to hide in the woods outside the city, or in the canals." They use **Hammer++**. Their map in progress is `c18_lc.vmf` (27 MB, a decompile of `rp_c18_ug` they are changing; on their Google Drive, see `docs/dev/map-survey.md`).

**Start from Valve's maps where they have the thing** (the owner, 21 November: "I am totally ok with them just
copy/pasting sections, rooms, etc out of the HL2 and episodes VMF files and adapting them to the gamemode"): lift the
room, street or set piece with `map_prefab.py extract` and adapt it (CLAUDE.md, "Map work"; the lesson
`owner-copy-valve-rooms`; the pieces already lifted: `docs/dev/prefabs.md`).

**The rule:** the tools write a NEW, separate VMF for each piece. They never change the owner's VMF. Since 20 November `map_merge.py` (below, "One file") also writes ONE new VMF with the owner's map and all the pieces in it, at the owner's asking; the owner's own file is still only read. The owner brings a piece in with a `func_instance` at 0 0 0 (a new version is then just a new file) or with Paste Special at no offset, looks, and compiles. A chat cannot compile or see the map in 3D: the owner is the eyes, and only their compile proves a piece is sealed. **Nothing here has been opened in Hammer or compiled yet** (19 November): the first load of each file is the real test.

```bash
python3 map_build.py canal                                   # lc_canal.vmf
python3 map_build.py ring                                    # lc_ring.vmf: the canal round the north of the city (map_ring.py)
python3 map_build.py ring --against c18_lc.vmf               # ...and check nothing in it runs into anything in the owner's map
python3 map_build.py forest_bunker --preview bunker.png      # lc_forest_bunker.vmf, and a picture from above
python3 map_build.py forest_cave --preview cave.png          # lc_forest_cave.vmf, cave.png and cave_cave.png
```

`--origin X Y` moves a piece, `--floor Z` sets a forest's gate floor (400: the canals' floor), `--first-node N` its first path node id (4000 canal, 4400 and 4600 the forests, 5000 the ring).

**`--test` writes `lc_PIECE_test.vmf`: the piece made to compile and be walked BY ITSELF** (a spawn point, a sun (its `light_environment`, and since 22 November the `env_sun` that shows the sun in the sky, at the light's own angles), and for the canal a sky wall where the road comes in), all in a visgroup "for testing alone (never in the real map)". Since 24 November (the owner: "can we get a test skybox texture? I can't tell what's void and what's skybox and what's broken cause everything is black. can we get a test env sun too? it's so hecking dark") a test map names Half-Life 2's day sky `sky_day01_01` (`TEST_SKY`; the pieces' own `painted` is Garry's Mod's sky that only `env_skypaint` draws, black without one) and a day's sun at 50 degrees, 300 with ambient 60, its `env_sun` 40 across and warm (`TEST_SUN`; it was City 18's dim 50 and 13). The file for the real map has neither (City 18 has its own); `map_assets.py` checks a sky name's six faces. The owner's first compile (19 November, vbsp++ on `lc_canal.vmf` alone) leaked at the quay's path node, 3808 1952 672: not a fault in the piece, the canal is open to the road by design and was compiled without the city. The tool's flood check gives the same leak when the road's opening is not stopped up, and now also fails if any entity is outside the sealed space. What that compile did show: vbsp++ read the file and its brushes without complaint.

## How a piece is made

- **Boxes on a grid (`Plan`):** a piece is drawn on 64-unit squares from above (`Plan.paint`): each square is open under the sky, open under a ceiling ("low"), or nothing. From that come a floor block under every open square, a lid over it, and a solid block in every square touching an open one (corners too), so it is sealed by construction; alike squares are joined into the biggest rectangles. Walls have looks by height (`bands`). Used for the canal and the bunker.
- **Displaced ground and skin (`Vmf.box_text(disp=...)`):** a box face can be displaced, every point of its grid moved to any place. Box corners are written clockwise seen from outside, with Hammer++'s `vertices_plus`; a displaced face's rows run from its first corner to its second and its columns to its fourth. **This was checked against the owner's own VMF:** with that reading, all 473 edge points that its 74 displacements share meet exactly; with rows and columns swapped, 98 do not.
- **Gates:** every piece ends in the same culvert, 512 wide and 256 high (`GATE_WIDE`, `GATE_HIGH`), on the plane where the next piece begins, closed by a black wall and bars in a visgroup of its own ("... gate's cap (delete to join)"). To join two pieces, delete the cap on each side. Each piece is sealed by itself with its caps in.
- **Checks before writing:** every corner on the 1-unit grid; a flood from the air inside must not reach outside (taking away any sealing brush makes it fail: tried); for forests, every ground patch meets its neighbours, the ground stays inside the shell, the cave's skin meets itself everywhere it should, and folded quads are counted. `map_survey.py` on the pieces and the owner's map together showed no piece overlapping another or the city (apart from the two brushes the owner is to delete).
- **Craft, as the file is written** (`map_build.craft`, 21 November): lightmapscale by where people walk, the canal's concrete at texture scale 0.5 and every wall's texture starting at its foot, as Valve builds (`docs/dev/map-modules.md`, "Valve's building craft"). Every piece, the forests and Ravenholm too.
- **Assets:** every model and material name in the three files was checked against file lists of Half-Life 2, Episode One and Episode Two (the Garry's Mod spawnlists, and decompiled QC and VMT trees: `github.com/Facepunch/garrysmod` `settings/spawnlist_default`, `github.com/Pinsplash/ValveQCs`, `github.com/Pinsplash/ValveVMTs`), and every `prop_static` model was checked to be compiled as a static prop. Canal and bunker surfaces use materials already in the owner's VMF. How any of it looks is unchecked.

## Combine structures and a Combine scene from the study's recipe (`map_combine.py`, `combine/`; 21 November; the owner compiled the scene on 22 November)

The owner asked for the Combine's odd, unsymmetrical architecture ("something we don't do well and the C18 map doesn't do well"), and said of the first test map: "3 little wall sections that look combine-esque, but it's not like it generated a building or scene". The generator now composes whole structures at Valve's scale by the recipe measured in Valve's Citadel maps (`_dev/reference/valve_citadel/notes/architecture_notes.md`, lessons `valve-combine-*`), and writes a scene to walk.

```bash
python3 map_combine.py scene --out lc_combine_scene.vmf [--seed N]   # old street, wall chain with a gate, building, intrusion (about 35 s)
python3 map_combine.py test --out lc_combine_test.vmf [--seed N]     # one of each small piece (wall, gate, tower, skin)
python3 map_combine.py check FILE.vmf                                # the study's numbers for the Combine in any VMF
python3 map_combine.py perimeter --out lc_combine_perimeter.vmf [--length 3072] [--height 768] [--seed N]   # the city's perimeter wall, a template
```

**The perimeter wall** (`combine.chain.perimeter`; the owner, 23 November: "a template for a simple combine exterior wall for the city, to see from the outside ... simple combine metal and maybe simple designs on it ... just not a rectangle bare wall"): `wall_chain`'s runs laid straight along one line, 768 to 1024 long and each drawn afresh (no two alike), so it repeats round the city without reading as wallpaper: the outer face battered about 4.5 degrees with ribs on a 256 rhythm broken by pairs, a staircase of stepped plates, a band at the foot; the inner face plumb over a plinth, a stack and paired ribs; the head leaning over, fins on two heads in three; a pier with its Combine lamp over every joint and at both ends. Citadel metal (077a, with 063a or 072a plates, 078a trim). Not yet placed in any map: the template is for the owner to look at first.

The parts (`_dev/tools/combine/`, each under 20 KB): `geom.py` (outlines, and frames turned by whole-number vectors such as (1, 1), (2, 1), (4, 1), (8, 1), so a turned piece keeps whole corners and flat faces), `piece.py` (the pieces and the skin every structure wears: masses in 128 courses jointed where each stack ends, stacks that staircase to one side with a first step of 48 to 64 and then 4 or 8, each layer jointed where the next ends, ribs on 256 broken by pairs 16 to 36 apart, light panels flush in the plates, and only a few small 32 by 32 plates, beside doors), `building.py`, `chain.py`, `tops.py` (22 November; also `fin_row`, fins against a wall in pairs), `seat.py` (22 November: props stood where nothing else stands, and the creeping-wall check), `measure.py` (the study's numbers, and how far a map is from Valve's), `scene.py`.

- **tops** (the owner, 22 November: "are combine structures as 'flat' on top? ... they are either curved, like the combine bunkers, or pointed or some weird needless slope"; then "please add this concept to the generator"). Measured first (`architecture_notes.md`, "Tops"; lesson valve-combine-tops, proposed): Valve's Combine brushwork is flat on top (21 of 28 textured tops seen against the sky, 4 stepped, 3 with a sloped edge, none curved or pointed; another 25 tops are nodraw, never seen), but two of three flat tops have their skyline broken (fins, a creeping wall hung over the edge, a parapet or higher step, a generator); the curves and points are Valve's models (combine_bunker01, combinetower001, the Citadel). So `crown(piece, kind, ...)` gives a top in brushes, lopsided: **vault** (segments of 11 to 13 degrees, Valve's arc step, springing 22 to 46 off upright so the crown is off the middle and one side stands on an upright cheek, as the bunker's), **ridge** (two slopes of different ratios from 1 in 2, 45, 3 in 4, 1 in 4, sometimes a narrow flat crest), **shed** (one slope across, sometimes stopping short and dropping: the "needless" slope), **steps** (courses stepped 48 to 64 in, then a slope or a ridge; in two parts end to end with different steps, since one stair run is its own mirror), **flat** (Valve's broken skyline: fins in pairs or on a beat, at least one, a parapet on part of one edge). No points: the owner, 22 November, "the off-kilter pyramid structures on the pillars/columns at the entrance ... look very wrong for a combine structure, so please remove those from the generator". A top is a line across the piece pushed along it, each edge a solid with upright sides, so faces are flat and corners whole (on the 4 grid in square frames); ribs across a long crown on the 256 beat lifted 8; slopes 45 or less (steeper faces count as leaning). Shares in `WEIGHTS` (the owner's shapes 11 in 13; set them toward `flat` for Valve's way).

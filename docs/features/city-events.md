# The city: events, raids, prison and the Combine's data

**The PA speaks when something happens.** No random announcements by default (`lc_announce_interval 0`): each line is tied to what's going on. "Evasive behaviour detected" plays when a unit takes a Disturbance chase and when someone is wanted for fleeing or escaping; "capital malcompliance" when someone is wanted for violence; "citizenship revoked" for a long sentence; "failing to report is conspiracy" when a citizen reports something; "anticitizen reported" when a rebel raid hits. Voice lines queue: one finishes before the next starts, instead of talking over each other.

**Ration units deducted.** When the Resistance act (a raid on a checkpoint, the lambda on a wall, rebel ground spreading), the PA charges the people nearby (graffiti only once a unit or scanner has seen it: as it goes up, or when one comes across it later; paint nobody has found costs nobody anything) with tolerating noncompliance, and it's real: every citizen within `lc_ration_dock_radius` (1500) and within about one floor above or below it (`lc_ration_dock_height`, 150 units: someone on the street below a rooftop camp isn't docked) loses their next ration allocation. They're told on the spot, and the dispenser tells them again, with the reason, when they try to collect. The allocation after that is normal. Units, Overwatch and hunted Resistance fighters aren't docked. `lc_ration_dock 0` turns it off.

**Announcements explain themselves.** Every PA announcement also goes into chat: what the voice is saying, in plain words, and on the next line what it actually means for you right now (how long it lasts, where to go, what happens if you don't, and the unit's side of it if you're CP). Ambient lines say they're just atmosphere. Events say so in chat when they end, too.

**Only the first few times (the owner, 10 November).** The plain-words line ("  > what it means for you") comes the first `lc_pa_explain` times (3) each player hears each kind of announcement; after that, just what the voice said. Details that change (where, how long) always come. `lc_pa_explain 0` is every time, as before.

**Events** run automatically or from the panel: rations, inspection sweeps, block raids, curfew, lockdown. **Only the routine ones are announced ahead:** rations and curfew get a two-minute warning on the PA and the Union band. A sweep, a block raid or a supply convoy is a surprise: the PA says nothing until it starts, though the units are briefed early and the Resistance's informants whisper it on Freq 7. A lockdown is never scheduled; it answers violence (the escalation reaching alert 4, or the city falling into unrest), and an admin can still call one from the panel. During curfew and lockdown your HUD points you home to your housing block, and you get a **grace period of at least 60 seconds** (`lc_curfew_grace`) to reach it, counted down on the HUD. After that, a cop who sees you outside gives three warnings before you're wanted. **If you respawn in the middle of a curfew** you get a fresh grace period of your own, long enough to walk from where you spawned to your block (never under 60 seconds, at most four minutes), and the warnings after it come 15 seconds apart instead of 8. Citizens walk home into the nearest housing block when curfew starts and go inside, instead of disappearing from the edge of the map.

**Morning exercise** (mandatory for citizens). Every second morning at dawn (`lc_exercise_every`, 2), before work, the city holds one of three, taking turns (`lc_exercise_kinds`, "jog,drill,parade"; one the map has no points for is skipped). Everyone gets `lc_exercise_muster` seconds (75) to report where their HUD points; Civil Protection, the wanted, prisoners and the Resistance are left out.
- **The jog.** Plot a route with the editor ("Point: jogging route stop", placed in order, the first is the start; give routes a number to have more than one). Citizens gather at the start and run it (`lc_jog_laps`, 1). Units post themselves along the route at intervals (`lc_jog_gap`, 600, up to `lc_jog_units`, 8). A pace marker moves round at `lc_jog_pace` (150: faster than walking, slower than sprinting), green while you're keeping up with it and red once you've fallen behind; anyone not jogging, or more than `lc_jog_behind` (450) behind the marker, gets the stick from the nearest unit, who runs over if need be (with no unit posted near, any unit who can see you steps in, and after a while out of everyone's reach the nearest of the route's units comes for you). The city's own joggers (`lc_exercise_npcs`, 10; more come out of a doorway near the start if there aren't enough about) keep up with the marker: one who falls behind or loses the way catches up out of sight. The city's joggers each keep to a lane of their own, a little to the side of the route, and set off a moment apart, so they don't jostle round one spot. Someone who becomes a citizen after it's started (joins, or a new life) is expected too and can join in on the route. **Late?** Join the route anywhere (the start too): your own pace marker starts from where you join, and the jog runs long enough for you to finish it. Each blow gives a burst of speed to catch back up (`lc_jog_boost`, 1.5 times, for `lc_jog_boost_secs`, 4). It's over when everyone's in.
- **The drill.** Place an exercise yard ("Point: exercise yard": stand where the rows of citizens will be and aim at the spot where the leader stands). Everyone gets a place in rows of five in front of the leader (a ring on the ground) and copies the leader's moves: crouch, jump, turn right around, a step left, right, forward or back, each within `lc_drill_window` seconds (2.5). The leader is a player **Clinic Medic or Records Clerk** at the yard if there is one: their own moves are called out to everyone (one every few seconds; if they stand about, the moves are called for them). Otherwise a Union fitness instructor calls them (`lc_drill_pace`, 3.5 seconds). Halfway through comes **skipping** (`lc_drill_rope`): a rope turned to a beat, jump on every one. A wrong move, a slow one, a missed beat or wandering off your place is a mistake; `lc_drill_mistakes` (3) of them and a unit at the edge of the yard steps in with the stick, then again every two more. `lc_drill_cues` (14) moves in all.
- **The parade.** Plot a parade route ("Point: parade route stop", in order, the first is where the column forms up). A column of soldiers (Civil Protection if there are none) marches it (`lc_parade_units`, 6), with units posted along the way. Citizens line the route and cheer: `/cheer` (the most), `/salute`, `/wave`, chanting in chat ("Glory to the Union!": the right words count more, yelling them more still, the same line twice less), and jumping up and down. Your enthusiasm is a bar on screen that drains while the column is near; below `lc_parade_min` (35) as it passes and a unit sees to it (after which, funnily enough, you cheer).

The stick never takes you below 15 health (`lc_exercise_beat_dmg`, 8 a blow). Units who see someone skipping it send them there, then use the stick; skipping it entirely costs 10 Civil Standing, and doing it well earns up to 10 (less if you were late). Afterwards **breakfast** goes to citizens of good standing only (`lc_exercise_breakfast`): Trusted get a meal, Exemplary a hot one; everyone else eats at the usual distribution. A few NPC citizens take part too (`lc_exercise_npcs`, 6). `lc_exercise <jog|drill|parade>` starts one now, `lc_exercise next` says what the next morning holds, `lc_exercise stop` ends it, and `lc_event jog|drill|parade` works too. `lc_morning_exercise 0` turns it all off.

**Block raids** (`lc_event raid`): a team of units of its own (arriving from just out of sight nearby, and leaving the same way when it's done; the city's patrols carry on as they were) run into one housing block and go through it systematically, a string of stops spread across the block. At each stop they frisk anyone there and confiscate contraband lying around: contraband items, loose weapons, TVs and radios. Whoever dropped or spawned each item is traced, and **each owner becomes wanted for contraband, for longer the more was found under their name** (five or more is a weapons cache, and gets the street units after them). Residents' hidden stashes may be found too, and owners who are offline are wanted when they next log in. If no units can get there, there's no raid and no announcement of one. `lc_raid_units`, `lc_raid_stash_chance`.

**Prison:** (there's a margin of `lc_prison_buffer` units, 150 by default, around the prison area and 250 around your own cell, so a step over the edge isn't an escape) security NPCs arrest instead of kill, CP players arrest with the Arrest Baton, escaping makes you wanted, and sentences survive death and disconnects. **Marched to the cells:** arrested by a unit NPC (beaten down, or giving yourself up to one), your hands are tied and you're walked there: the unit who took you leads, holding you by the arm, and the nearest other unit follows behind. They walk the city's paths to a free cell, unlocking the Civil Protection and prison doors on the way (the perimeter's and the cell block's: tag them **cp** or, better, **prison**, which stays locked in a blackout) and locking them behind them, and lock you in. Where the paths don't reach a cell, they walk you as close as they can get (the nearest spot on the paths outside the prison zone) and take you through the doors from there. The Resistance hears about it on Freq 7, and anyone can try to get you out: put the leading unit down and, if there's another unit about (the one behind, or one nearby), he takes your arm instead; only with nobody left to take you are you free (still tied: F1 > Work at the cuffs, or have someone cut them) and wanted for escaping custody. A unit walking a prisoner alone is soon joined by another. Doors they open on the way stay open three times as long, and don't shut while the prisoner is near them; while he's busy fighting he lets go of you, and getting far enough from him is slipping away. The Resistance's man inside can let a prisoner slip on the way (docs/features/resistance-insider.md, Let them slip). No walkable route, or a walk longer than `lc_march_max` (240 seconds): you're struck from behind, everything goes black, and you come to in a cell. **Cuffed only once you're subdued:** a unit who catches up with you beats you down first (`lc_subdue_hits`, 3 blows of the baton, each leaving you dazed, slow for a couple of seconds), or you're shot or hurt down under 20 health, or down, or you stand still for them (a second or more). Running past a unit isn't enough to be cuffed, and someone in noclip is never laid hands on. **Losing them:** while you're wanted the units only know where you are while the hunt is hot: a unit sees you, or one did in the last `lc_wanted_lost` seconds (30). Out of sight that long (lying low in rebel territory, slipping away), nobody knows where you've gone: step out and only a unit who actually sees you comes for you (an Overwatch squad hunting you, alert 3 and up, doesn't give up). `lc_march 0` turns it off (editor panel, Prison and conduct). CP players' Arrest Baton still sends people straight to a cell (tie them and drag them along to walk them in yourself). **No cells on the map yet?** Then an arrest happens on the street. You're cuffed and made to kneel for 20 seconds (`lc_street_detention`), and longer the longer you were wanted: `lc_street_detention_per_minute` (10) seconds more for each minute, at most `lc_street_detention_max` (60) (the owner, 2 November); searched, fined 25 credits (`lc_street_fine`), and let go with a warning. (Arrests need prison **cell points** placed with the editor; a prison zone around them is optional.)

**Don't run in front of the cops.** A metrocop who sees you sprinting within `lc_run_range` units (500) shouts "STOP RUNNING!", in chat with his name and across the middle of your screen. Stop within `lc_run_react` seconds (2) and that's the end of it. Keep running, or start running again in front of a unit within `lc_run_memory` seconds (30), and you're wanted for fleeing Civil Protection (`lc_run_wanted`, 60 seconds) with the units nearby chasing you. Walking off from a stop-and-search gets the same shout. Running means holding sprint while you move, or moving faster than halfway between your walking and sprinting speeds, so it works with the gamemode's slow pace too. Units, anyone in a convincing uniform, rebel territory and the curfew grace period (when you're meant to hurry home) are exempt. `lc_running_cops 0` turns it off. If a unit never shouts, the debug report has a "running check" line for each player: their speed, whether they count as sprinting, whether they're exempt and why, and whether a unit can see them.

**Flying a scanner (Combine consoles).** Prison is long, so put a Combine console in a cell (editor: "Fixture: Combine console"), or anywhere else. A prisoner or a unit (or an admin) presses E on it and links up with one of the city's scanners: the nearest one is taken over, or a new one goes up over the street. Your view goes with the scanner, through red lenses (the Civil Protection mask's view, in red; a masked unit's own lenses come off while they fly), and your body stays standing at the console where everyone can see it. WASD flies, Space and Ctrl go up and down, Shift is faster, the mouse looks, left click is the camera flash, R (or E, or `!unlink`) disconnects. The scanner always appears somewhere with room for it, never inside a wall, and if it gets wedged while you're trying to fly it's shaken loose. The scanner can't go further than `lc_scanner_range` (6000 units) from its console. The link drops if you're hurt, if the scanner is shot down (it takes 60 damage), if the console goes, and when your sentence is up: you come out of the scanner and walk out of the release spot as usual.

- **The flash** reaches exactly as far as a photograph (about ten feet, `lc_scanner_photo_range`), so anyone it dazzles knows they've been photographed. It blinds anyone in front of it for a moment and stuns the city's people (units too) for three seconds. A prisoner who flashes a unit gets `lc_scanner_penalty` seconds (60) more on their sentence, and is told so on the spot, with the time left.
- **Snitching.** Every flash is also a photograph, but only of what's up close: within `lc_scanner_photo_range` (128 units, about ten feet), as close as a scanner's camera works, so photos from the top of the map don't count. The pilot's view brackets the nearest suspect in front of the camera with how far it is, and says MOVE CLOSER, IN RANGE or ALREADY REPORTED (and REBEL GROUND IN FRAME when you're in it or that close to it). Rebels (hunted fighters, Father Grigori, free vortigaunts, anyone in Resistance gear), the wanted, BOLO subjects, anticitizens, anyone standing on rebel ground, zombies, and rebel ground itself all go to Civil Protection as a **scanner report**. Every unit on duty gets it in chat, and whoever it caught is **outlined in red for `lc_scanner_spotted` seconds (60), through walls**, with what they were reported for and how far away they are. Rebel ground is marked on units' screens, and the city's own units nearby go after the hunted and the wanted. The person photographed is told, and it goes on their record. The pilot earns Civil Standing for it (Grigori 15, rebel ground 15, a free vortigaunt 12, a rebel fighter or rebel NPC 12, the wanted, a BOLO or someone on rebel ground 8, an anticitizen 6, a zombie 1 or 2) but **never time off**: prison is still a punishment, you just get the option to serve the Combine while you're in. It can't be farmed: the same person can't be reported again for `lc_scanner_spot_cooldown` seconds (300, rebel ground twice that), only four zombies count per photo, and a pilot earns at most `lc_scanner_snitch_cap` (100) standing from reports every ten minutes. **Rebels shoot back.** A rebel who sees a flown scanner (in front of him, in plain sight, or right beside him) fires on it after about a second, every second or so. Their aim is poor: it gets worse with distance and with how fast you're flying, and each hit only chips it (8 of its 60), so a close charge across open ground can still get a photo before it's shot down, while coming up behind a rebel who's looking the other way gets you the picture without a shot (`lc_scanner_rebel_fire 0` turns it off). **Broken feet show in the camera:** a crippled pilot's scanner is slower and lurches and rolls with every step. **Smoke blinds the camera:** nobody standing in a smoke grenade's cloud, or behind one, can be flashed or photographed, and a scanner flying into the smoke sees nothing at all, so smoke is the Resistance's answer to a snitch overhead (the pilot's view says SMOKE IN FRAME). Units can fly scanners too; their reports go out the same way, without the standing. `lc_scanner_link 0` turns consoles off.

**Giving yourself up:** while you're wanted, walk up to a unit and press E (or type `!surrender` near one). You get half the usual sentence, the search is called off, and serving it pays back more of your Civil Standing.

**Supply drops.** A city event (now and then by itself, or `lc_event supply`): Union supplies come in at a **loading dock** (the editor's "Point: loading dock"; with none, somewhere on the streets away from rebel ground and the infestation). With open sky over it, a **dropship** flies in with a cargo container slung underneath, sets it down and climbs away, and comes back to lift it out when it's over; under a roof, an **APC** is parked there instead. Beside it: a stack of crates and the open crate of Union supplies, for `lc_supply_minutes` (6). An Overwatch soldier stands guard (`lc_supply_guards`, 1), turning to look about now and then; anyone hanging about the crates where he can see them is warned off twice ("Step away from the crates"), then wanted. Two metrocops unload it the whole time (`lc_supply_porters`, 2): each walks to the stack, picks up a box in both hands, carries it off to a spot down the street, sets it down on a growing pile and comes back; between them, and behind the soldier's back, is when to sneak in. Units are told where and get a **Guard the supply drop** directive; the Resistance get a radio tip and a **Raid the supply drop** operation (and their fighters go for it themselves half the time, `lc_rebel_react`); citizens are told to keep clear. Anyone who isn't a unit can press E on the crate to take a load (eight seconds, keep still): gun parts, ammunition, chemicals, rations, bandages, now and then a smoke grenade, sometimes two things at once. It's done quietly; but a unit who sees you at it, at any moment of the eight seconds, stops you there and then and makes you wanted. The crate holds `lc_supply_loads` loads (5). If any are left when the convoy moves on, every unit within reach is paid `lc_supply_pay` credits (40) and some XP; if it's emptied, the whole city hears that a supply drop was looted.

**Stealing the Combine's data.** Put a **Combine data terminal** (editor fixture) in the nexus or a Combine-only zone; any Combine console prop the map already has works too (the scanner console and the commissary don't). Anyone with a multitool (a Resistance Hacker, or whoever bought one) aims it at one and fires (primary fire; E works too), and stays close for `lc_heist_seconds` (30) while it downloads. Halfway through it trips the alarm: every unit gets DATA BREACH with where, it's marked on their screens, the city's units nearby turn hostile and come running, a response squad comes through the door, and units get a **Data breach** directive. Being seen at it makes you wanted. The terminal then has nothing new for `lc_heist_cooldown` minutes (20). **One terminal only holds half the picture:** what you get is a **data fragment**; download a second, different terminal's and the two fragments make a whole **Combine data chip**. The Resistance get **Steal the Combine's data** on their Operations board (two terminals, 60 XP) whenever two are ready.

**The master terminal** is the motherlode: the editor's **Combine master terminal** fixture, or a `masterinterface` prop the map already has. A download takes `lc_heist_master_seconds` (60), every alarm goes off a third of the way in (MASTER TERMINAL BREACH), and the squad that comes has two more soldiers. What comes out is a **master data core**: the dealer pays 400 for it, the lab's decoding gives 150 XP and 150 credits and marks every unit for `lc_heist_master_intel_seconds` (600, ten minutes), and plugged into a terminal it wipes the record of **every Resistance member in the city**. It's dry for `lc_heist_master_cooldown` minutes (60) after. The Resistance get **The master terminal** on their Operations board when it's ready (120 XP).

The **Combine data chip** is contraband, and has three uses:
- **Sell it** to the black market dealer: 130 credits. (The quartermaster doesn't buy them.)
- **Take it to the Resistance lab** (only there: the quartermaster won't touch it): E on the scientist, and **Decode it** is at the top of his list (or Use it from your pockets beside him; `!decode` works too): 50 XP and 50 credits for whoever brought it, and for `lc_heist_intel_seconds` (300, five minutes) every Resistance member sees every unit in the city outlined through walls, NPC and player, with a countdown on screen. The scanner report, turned around.
- **Plug it into a terminal yourself** (Use, looking at one): your record is wiped clean, with no violations, no BOLO and no wanted status. (A Fighter's face is still known: hunted ranks stay hunted.)

**Pacing (the owner, 7 November: "slow down the pacing so events are more sparse").** The city's own happenings are about twice as far apart: automatic events every `lc_event_interval` 24 minutes (was 12), skirmishes `lc_skirmish_interval` 50 (was 25), trains `lc_train_minutes` 24 (was 12), rebel territory growing `lc_rebel_spread_minutes` 40 (was 20), Overwatch drops `lc_drop_cooldown` 60 (was 30), the Resistance's own operations `lc_rebel_ops_minutes` 60 (was 30), and the Administrator's commendation of an exemplary citizen `lc_loyalty_praise_minutes` 40 (was 20). A server still on an old default moves to the new one when it starts; a value someone chose is kept. See `docs/dev/pacing-audit.md`.

# The city's people

Every citizen and Civil Protection unit is a **named person with a profile that never changes**: a name, a sex and voice, a model, a Union job, and a housing block. You'll see the same faces again. Look at one to see their name and what they do. They spend part of their day at their workplace (if the map has one for their job) and go to **their own block** at curfew. About one in eight citizens (`lc_rebel_pct`) is secretly a **Resistance sympathizer**: Resistance players see "one of ours" on them, and the Resistance's contacts and supply runs are drawn from them.

`lc_citizens` and `lc_cops` still set how many are out on the streets at once; the roster (`lc_roster_size` citizens, `lc_roster_cops` units) is everybody who lives in the city, and they take turns, the person who's been away longest coming out next. The roster is saved per map in `garrysmod/data/livingcity/<map>_people.json`, so it's the same people next session and next week. **A person who is killed stays dead**, and the city fills the gap with a new arrival. `lc_people [list]` summarizes, `lc_resetpeople` starts over.

### Trains

With a **train platform** on the map (an editor point; failing that, the Intake Processor's workplace), a train comes in every `lc_train_minutes` (12, give or take a quarter): a horn at the platform, a notice for everyone ("The train from City 14 has arrived..."), and two to four **new arrivals** step off, suitcase in hand (hanging upright from the hand, swinging as they walk), and walk to their housing blocks before getting on with life in the city. Intake Processors are told a train is in. For a minute and a half after, **anyone leaving the city** walks to the platform with a suitcase and boards, instead of going out through a doorway; then the train pulls out. Not during a curfew or lockdown. `lc_trains 0` turns them off; `lc_train` (admin) brings one in now.

### The power grid, and forcefields

**Transformers** (an editor fixture: the Combine's own generator, bolted down so it can't topple, humming with its lamp lit while it has power, dark and silent when it's down; move it with the physgun in build mode and it's stood upright and bolted down where you drop it) power the district around them, out to `lc_grid_radius` (1800 units): every streetlight, loudspeaker, city screen and TV, forcefield, ration dispenser, water vending machine and commissary terminal there, every one of the **map's own switchable lights** (the lamps, spotlights and glows the mapper named so they could be turned off), and the locks on its Combine doors, run off the nearest one. Anything with no transformer near it is on the city's mains and never goes out. **Sabotage** one (a multitool, twelve seconds; or enough damage: explosives, a lot of bullets) and its district goes dark: lights out, loudspeakers silent (nobody there hears the PA or a broadcast), screens and TVs dead, dispensers, vending machines and the commissary dead (no rations there until it's back, and NPCs don't queue at a dead dispenser), forcefields down, and **Combine doors unlocked**. **Prison doors stay locked.** A unit who sees you do it makes you wanted for sabotage; the Resistance gets XP. Units hear about it at once, the Resistance hears their doors are open, and Maintenance Technicians are told to fix it: E on it, ten seconds, paid. The city's own maintenance crews see to it after a while, and emergency crews after `lc_blackout_minutes` (8).

**The whole city goes dark** when half the grid is down at once (`lc_blackout_share`, 0.5 of the transformers; 0 turns it off; on a map with one transformer, that one). Then everything above goes out everywhere, the city's mains included, and so does **the map's own lighting**: everyone's going by flashlight (F). Prison doors still hold. Restore enough transformers and the lights come back. Admins can black out the city for an event from the admin tools ("The city": Black out the city, Lights on, Follow the grid) or with `lc_blackout on|off|auto`. `lc_blackout_light` sets how dark (a, pitch black, to m, normal; b by default), and night stays as dark as night if that's darker. Two things Source decides, not us: the sun's light is baked into the same lighting as the lamps, so outdoors goes as dark as indoors (like a moonless night; the sky itself stays bright); and players, NPCs and props are lit separately from the walls, so they may not darken as much as the room around them.

**Combine forcefields** (an editor fixture: place it facing you, across a doorway or a street, about eight feet wide) let units (and admins in build mode) walk through; nobody else, unless they **swipe a Combine keycard** (E on it, or the actions menu: a few seconds, and a unit who sees a citizen do it makes them wanted). A multitool **shorts one out** for a minute, and it drops with its district's power. Who may pass is worked out the same way on your machine and the server's, so walking into one is smooth. NPCs aren't stopped by them, and nobody gets trapped: if a field comes back up (power restored, a short or a swipe running out) around someone it wouldn't let through, they can step out of it, on whichever side they like.

### Checkpoints, turrets, and holding the infestation

A checkpoint is built round a **Combine power bank**: a glowing wall unit, placed in the editor ("Fixture: Combine power bank") or put up by a Civil Protection officer (actions menu: Civil Protection > Put up a power bank on this wall; Division Leader and up, one each). **Turrets and forcefields** within `lc_checkpoint_range` (700 units) of it run off it, on a cable you can see; a turret with no bank near it can run off a forcefield instead. The bank draws from its district's transformer, so a sabotaged transformer, a city-wide blackout, or a **multitool on the bank itself** (aim it at the bank and primary fire: eight seconds, dead for two minutes) kills its glow and everything on its cables. Units E on a bank to read what it's powering.

**Turrets** are Half-Life 2's Combine floor turrets. They shoot the infected, the wanted and known Resistance fighters, and leave ordinary citizens, people in custody and units alone. Place them in the editor ("Fixture: Combine turret", facing you), or **any unit deploys one** where they're looking (actions menu: Civil Protection > Deploy a turret here), near a power bank or forcefield: `lc_turrets_per_unit` (2) each, `lc_turrets_per_post` (4) on one bank or forcefield. A unit packs one up with E. Nobody else can pick one up or move it. Without power, a turret folds up, goes quiet and is held where it stands (so it doesn't topple on its own); powered, it stands on its legs and **can be knocked over** again, which is how you beat one. The editor's turrets come back (out of sight) if they're knocked over or blown up. **Combine barricades** (low, medium, tall) wall off the rest.

**Holding the infestation.** Forcefields stop the infected as well as people. **The infestation can't spread over or past a checkpoint that has power** (`lc_containment`). What the turrets kill holds it where it is without pushing it back (units still have to go in for that), and quietly: the radio doesn't report every kill. But kill a checkpoint's power within reach of the infestation (a blackout, a sabotaged transformer, a shorted power bank) and it's a **containment breach**: the whole city hears it, the infestation surges over the checkpoint, and `lc_breach_infected` (4) of the infected come pouring out. One breach in a place every five minutes at most.

### Talking to people

**Press E on a citizen** (when no job of yours needs that press for something else) and a small window opens: their name, their job and what they're doing, how they feel about you, and what you can do:

- **Chat:** whatever they've heard. The city's rumours (what's been happening: rebel ground taken, the infested spreading, arrests) come out here now, not in chat. A sympathizer who likes you will tell you where the Resistance gathers.
  - **They notice what you need, and show you the way (the owner, 10 November: no place names, since the gamemode moves between maps; "Here, let me show you" and walk over there, or a vague statement and a waypoint and trail).** If you need something (registering, work, a room, food, water, your block, a job, a shift: your next step), Chat now and then gets advice instead of a rumour, at most once a minute. The words never name a place: "You look half-starved. Try the bins." Where to go comes from what the editor placed on this map. A stranger points: the marker and the trail on the ground go to the nearest one. A citizen who likes you (warm) says "Come on, I'll show you", the window closes and they walk you there, waiting if you fall behind ("Keep up!") and saying one line when you arrive. If they can't get there, are stopped or pulled away, or you wander off, they point instead. With nothing of the kind on the map: "Can't help you there, friend." Food or drink already in your pockets: they tell you to have it. Settings: `lc_citizen_advice` (1; 0 is Chat as before), `lc_citizen_lead` (1; 0 and everyone only points), `lc_citizen_lead_range` (3000 units: further, even a friend points). Recordings go in `sound/livingcity/citizens/`, a man's and a woman's take of each line (`advice_bins_male1.wav`, `advice_bins_female1.wav`); the list is `VOICE_LINES.md` section 5, and `lc_voice_missing` lists what's missing. Without one they speak with Half-Life 2's own citizen voice. The code is `sv_livingcity_advice.lua`.
- **Give something...** (was "Share some food"; the owner, 10 November: "a 'Share' or 'Give' button that opens a menu of the many things that can be shared/given"): a menu of everything in your pockets a citizen could be handed, food first (your papers and Combine-issued kit never show):
  - **Food or water:** taken as sharing food always was (they remember it, +2).
  - **A drink or a smoke:** someone fond of one (the vice trait, `docs/features/city-minds.md`) takes it (+2, +3 if very fond) and lights up or drinks there and then; someone sober says no and you keep it.
  - **Medicine:** taken gratefully (+3).
  - **Papers:** the Civil Orientation pamphlet anyone takes (+1; the disloyal shrug, 0). The **Resistance pamphlet** is handed over as its own feature decides (`sv_livingcity_rebelpamphlet.lua`: a sympathizer takes it, anyone else may be won over, tuck it away or push it back, an informant reports you; the same as F1 > This person > Hand them a Resistance pamphlet). Any other item marked `leaflet = "resistance"` a sympathizer or a disloyal citizen keeps (+2); someone loyal pushes it back (-3).
  - **Arms (a gun or a grenade from your pockets, not one in your hand):** only a sympathizer takes it, under their coat (+3); anyone else is frightened and refuses (-1). A Civil Protection player's menu never lists arms (the owner, 10 November).
- **Pick their pocket:** a Guile check (d20 + Guile against `lc_pickpocket_dc`, 12). A few credits, now and then a ration, water, or a dead man's papers. Once per person per ten minutes. Fail and they shout "Thief!" and back away, they won't forget it, and if a unit saw it you're wanted for theft.
- **Inform on them** (not the Resistance): if they really are a sympathizer, they're picked up within the minute and you're paid (+15 standing); if not, you've wasted a unit's time (-5).
- **Ask them to keep watch** (the Resistance, a sympathizer): a lookout, below.

Units are units: Civil Protection players still stop citizens with E.

**They remember you.** Each person on the roster remembers how each of your characters has treated them (another character of yours is a stranger to them), saved with the roster: hurt them (-4), get caught robbing them (-3), report them (-4), and at -3 they're **afraid of you**: they won't talk, and when you come near they back away. Feed them (+2), heal them (+2), and at +3 they're **warm**: they greet you in the street and always tell you what they've heard. Memory runs from -10 to +10, and each person keeps their strongest twenty. `lc_talk 0` turns the window off.

### Resistance lookouts

**Spotting a sympathizer:** to the Resistance (and nobody else), the ordinary-looking citizens who are secretly with them carry a small orange lambda over their heads, when you're close and can see them; look at one and their name tag says "one of ours: a sympathizer". A Resistance Member (rank 2 and up) can ask a sympathizer to **keep watch** (E on them, then "Ask them to keep watch for us" in the talk window). They stay where they're standing for `lc_lookout_minutes` (15), and when a unit or Overwatch comes into sight (`lc_lookout_range`, 1100 units, and not through smoke) they call out "Combine!" and Freq 7 hears where, at most once every 45 seconds. Two each. The one who posted them always hears, radio or not. A lookout is still a citizen: a unit can stop them, and then the Resistance hears the lookout's been stopped (talking to your own lookout doesn't count). Resistance players see "one of ours: keeping watch" on them.

**Lookout posts** are the armed version, placed by an admin: the editor's "Point: rebel lookout post" keeps a Resistance fighter (SMG, pulse rifle or shotgun) standing guard there, at the entrance to rebel territory, say. Place it from where the Combine would come: he faces that way. When a unit, Overwatch, someone in a convincing uniform or one of the city's own units comes into sight within `lc_lookout_post_warn` (1400 units) he shouts and warns Freq 7 where; when they come within `lc_lookout_post_fire` (650) or onto rebel ground, he opens fire (after a moment's shouting). He holds his post, fights from it, and goes back to it after; killed, someone takes the post after `lc_lookout_post_respawn` seconds (120), out of sight.

**Changes from the 7 November playtest.**
- **Under fire:** when a gun goes off nearby (`lc_gunfire_fear`, 700 units; 0 off), citizens run from the shooter, and about a third crouch and cover their heads for a few seconds first ("head down").
- **Rebels spread out:** doorways and narrow halls are never picked for spawning, wandering or patrols; a zone's room counts only its open floor (never less than a quarter of its area), and every 30 seconds an over-full zone loses fighters nobody can see (never ones following a player, raiding, on a post, fighting, or the quartermaster).
- **"Pick up that can"** only happens with a bin within reach, never in the infestation zone or while you're on a job; pick the can up and the bin is marked for you.

**Citizen Information (the owner, 10 November: "an NPC that is city helpdesk or Citizen Information ... behind a counter that answers questions on topics").** Editor: "Fixture: Citizen Information desk", placed behind a counter: a clerk who looks up at whoever comes near. Press E for the topics: rations and ration cards, work, housing, papers and registration, the law, curfew, Civil Standing, joining Civil Protection, and **Where is...?** (Civil Intake, Housing Allocation, Labor Assignment, the Commissary, the ration dispenser, your block: the route is marked, with the trail on the ground; a place this map lacks, they say so). Always the official version: never a word about bins, the black market or the Resistance. Your Civil Standing sets their manner (polite at 50 and up, curt below zero); someone wanted gets no answers. Each answer is learning it: no tip card for it. Recordings in `sound/livingcity/clerk/` (`VOICE_LINES.md` section 6). `lc_infodesk 0` and the clerk only says the desk is closed. (`sv_livingcity_infodesk.lua`, `entities/lc_infoclerk.lua`)

**The old-timer (the owner, 10 November: "an old man NPC that gives advice and players can find them and talk to them").** One of the city's citizens (a man, if there's one out) is an old man who has seen it all, kept with the roster so he's the same old man after a restart (if he dies, someone else grows old). He goes about his day like anyone: you have to find him (his job shows as "Old-timer"). E on him opens his own window. He opens up as he knows you: each visit (once every 15 minutes) and sharing food warm him to you. A stranger gets the simple advice (keep your head down, curfew, scanners, don't run); friendly (3), where people really find food (the bins, marked), water, fishing and cooking; glad to see you (6), the dealer (marked), what a lockpick is for, and where the Resistance gathers. **His face** (the owner, 10 November: "make his face the old man from Lost Coast, but his body just a standard citizen in citizen clothes"): Lost Coast's old fisherman's head on his own citizen's body, on each player's screen (the fisherman laid over him bone for bone, everything but his head and neck shrunk away, the citizen's own head hidden beneath). A player whose game doesn't have the model sees the citizen's own face, never an error. `lc_oldtimer_face` picks the model (empty: his own face). (`cl_livingcity_oldtimerface.lua`) What he tells you is learned: no tip cards for it. **His troubles, an unmarked quest line (the owner, 10 November: "little hints in his dialogue that you can help remedy and then he likes you more and gives you better advice").** Each visit, whatever is troubling him slips out at the end of his greeting, one at a time, never marked anywhere: he's too old to scavenge for food; the tap on his floor is dry (water); his twenty-year cough, and the Commissary's prices (Union medicine: antiparasitic tablets won't do, the owner, 10 November); someone stole his ration card (a ration card: you worked for it); he hasn't had a smoke in years (a Union or hand-rolled cigarette; the radio trouble was dropped, the owner: "I never added music"). "Offer him something from your pockets" gives him what helps with his current trouble: he warms to you (+2), thanks you, and the next one comes out. Nothing he needs: he says so. A meal once his troubles are past still warms him a little, at most once every 10 minutes. Help with all five and he's glad to see you, and "Sit with him a while" has him give you his old Lockpick set (once). What each character has done for him is kept with the roster. Afraid of you, he's like anyone else. Units still stop him with E. Recordings in `sound/livingcity/oldtimer/` (`VOICE_LINES.md` section 7). `lc_oldtimer 0` and he's just another citizen. (`sv_livingcity_oldtimer.lua`)

**The Colonel (the owner, 10 November: the weary Resistance fighter at camp; "make his model Odessa Cubbage"; on whether he can die: "unless he is client-side for a player and actually just a hallucination, which is actually an awesome story idea").** An old fighter standing by the quartermaster (or in the middle of rebel ground, with no quartermaster), Odessa Cubbage's face, called the Colonel. **Only the Resistance can see him, because he isn't there:** he died when the Combine burned the first camp, and the cell still sees him. He's drawn on each Resistance player's own screen and nowhere else, faintly see-through and cold-tinted, slowly fading in and out with the odd flicker (the owner, 10 November: "slightly transparent ... so he looks sort of ghostly"): no shadow, nothing to bump into, bullets pass through him, nobody can arrest him, and whenever the Combine are in the camp (a sweep, a unit or soldier within 600 units) he simply isn't there, back once they've gone. Anyone else near someone talking to him sees "* ... is talking quietly to an empty corner." He lets it slip slowly, and says it outright once you've done all he asks. Looking at him shows "The Colonel, [E] talk". His window:
- **What's the word?** Live intel, marked with the trail: a sweep coming into rebel ground first, then a prisoner being marched to the cells, a supply drop, or the nearest sympathizer (a lookout waiting to be asked); a quiet day, he says so.
- **His jobs, unmarked** (what he needs is said at the end of his greeting): three Union medicine for the wounded (40 XP); his brother's dog tags, lying somewhere in Combine ground (a Combine-only zone, else the prison, else by the Combine's Commissary; a new item, `dog_tags`; 60 XP and the brother's .357 with 12 rounds); then the unit who lit the first camp, a living Civil Protection unit named by designation only, dead by your hand (75 XP). "Give him something from your pockets" hands over what he needs.
- **Debrief me** (after a Resistance job): clean (no warrant, not downed, not taken in while on it) is `lc_colonel_debrief_xp` (25) XP; messy (done, but with a warrant or worse) or failed, a dressing-down. Within 45 minutes of the job.
- **Heard anything strange?** (once you've brought the medicine): ten whispers, each once, pointing crookedly at the ways the city itself can set off its secret endings (a dozen dead in minutes; a perfect morning drill; the lights kept dead; six dancing in a ring; fifteen fires; the master core cracked at the lab; the dead piling up; the weeds left alone; the priest and the core; the city red for 45 minutes).
- What he knows about you is kept per character (PData `lc_colonel_<character>`). Recordings in `sound/livingcity/colonel/` (`VOICE_LINES.md` section 9), heard only by the player he's talking to. `lc_colonel 0` and he's gone. (`sv_livingcity_colonel.lua`, `cl_livingcity_colonel.lua`)

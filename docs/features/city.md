# The city and the Resistance

Citizens wander, sit, lean, work at workplaces, chat, queue at ration lines, and arrive and leave through doorways. They don't know who Gordon Freeman is. Cops walk beats, hold checkpoints, walk right up to citizens to inspect them (with the trainstation harassment animations, where the model has them), chatter on the radio, and occasionally ask you to pick up a can. Crowd a cop or keep pestering him and you get "move along", then a warning, a shove, a crack with the stick, and then you're wanted for obstructing Civil Protection. Hostile units actually come after you. Walking up to a unit to report to him (a loyalty check, say) is never treated as crowding: he acknowledges you instead of warning or shoving you. Wandering NPCs stay on their own level and within a few streets of where they are, rather than setting off across the map.

**Ration lines:** people nearby join the line and take their places in the order they arrive. The person at the front is served from the ration dispenser if one is placed at the front of the line (it plays its dispensing animation), or by an unseen clerk if not. Players can stand at the front of the line too. After a few seconds you're handed a ration, and the NPCs behind you wait their turn. The usual allocation rules apply: one per allocation, and none for anticitizens. Scanners follow citizens, change altitude now and then, and work themselves free when they snag on walkways. They never photograph units (or anyone in a convincing CP uniform). Birds that wander into a doorway are shooed out of it, and anyone opening a door scares off the birds in its way. Overwatch soldiers are optional. Zombies live in infestation zones; birds and the odd headcrab roam the streets.

**The Resistance** lives in rebel territory: a few armed fighters (`lc_rebels`, 4, spread across the rebel zones) mill about and keep watch. Resistance players are among friends there and ordinary citizens are left alone, but anyone who looks like the Combine (a unit, Overwatch, the Administrator, or someone in a convincing CP uniform) who walks in gets a shout ("Combine!") and then, after `lc_rebel_warning` seconds (2), gunfire. Their shots do `lc_rebel_damage` (0.5) of normal damage, because dying here is permanent. Shoot at one and the fighters nearby shoot back, whoever you are; the Combine doesn't care who shoots rebels, and units earn a little XP for it. Quests that send you into rebel territory (the Missing Unit's Retaliation, the Overwatch incursion) make sure there's someone home.

**Territory spreads, and can be pushed back.** The rebel and infestation zones you place are where it starts. Left alone, each slowly spreads: every `lc_rebel_spread_minutes` (40) or `lc_infest_spread_minutes` (10) minutes on average, as long as someone holds it (a fighter, or something infested, inside it) and nobody has fought over it for `lc_territory_calm` minutes. **It grows along the path nodes you lay in the editor:** each growth takes two or three of the links out of the territory at random (`lc_territory_links`, 3: this many or one fewer), from a node inside it (or within about 13 feet outside the edge of a zone you placed, on its floor) to the next node out, and the ground along each link becomes territory, `lc_territory_width` (160) units wide and eight feet (96 units) tall, on the floor between the two nodes. In the open it spreads wide: a gap the new ground closes in on one floor (up to 16 cells, about 680 by 680 units; never a whole block ringed by streets) is filled, and so is the ground between a node and its neighbours once they're all taken. A link whose ends are further apart in height than stairs or a ramp (more than 160 units, or steeper than about 37 degrees) is never taken, so it never goes through a floor or a ceiling; nor does it run into housing blocks, the prison, Combine areas, no-go zones, checkpoints, ration lines, cells, spawn doorways, dispensers, terminals, or the other side's ground. One territory's grown ground is one zone however big it gets: square cells (128 units) on a grid, one floor deep, looked up by cell (asking "is this rebel ground" costs the same at any size), kept and sent as the few rectangles they merge into, at most `lc_territory_cells` (600) cells. The editor shows it as those rectangles. Everything else that asks about grown ground goes by those cells too, not by a circle round where it last grew to: where its barrels, crates and bones go, where fighters and the infested appear and patrol (each cell's own floor is dry ground, not a ditch), the things in it rebels use, a fighter or zombie straying from it and heading back, a team clearing it keeping new fighters off it, sweeps arriving and raids staging, graffiti near it, guards for a cleanup shift near an infestation, walking out of an infestation in a story, and growth keeping clear of the other side. A territory with no path nodes in it or near it grows the old way: a round patch out from its edge in a random direction, onto open floor people walk on. Due to grow but unable to (nobody holding it, nowhere to go), it tries again a minute later, not a whole interval later; the PA, the radio and the rumours only hear about it when ground really was taken, and the radio says where. Someone moves in, and each growth brings one more rebel or zombie to the population. It stops at `lc_territory_max` growths (6) for the Resistance and `lc_infest_max` (30) for an infestation, and at `lc_territory_city_max` (45) of each kind across the whole city. Each growth of infestation brings one more of the infested, up to `lc_zombies_max` (50) alive at once. Zones of one kind that you drew touching or overlapping count as one territory: they grow, and are reported, as one place, and growth close together comes over the radio as one message. Fighting back pushes it in: every rebel or zombie killed there (by a player or a unit, not by their own side) counts, and every `lc_territory_hits` kills (3), or clearing all of it out, take back one growth's worth of ground, the cells furthest from the zone you placed (walking through the territory) first (a round patch an older version grew goes whole). The zones you placed are never taken. The CP net and the Resistance radio hear about ground gained and lost. Grown ground is kept in `<map>_territory.json`, not your layout, and comes back after a restart. `lc_territory` shows each territory's state and why it last didn't grow; `lc_territory_grow rebel|infest` grows one now (admins), and superadmins have the same in C > Help > Admin testing tools > The city ("Grow rebel ground now", "Grow the infestation now", with each territory's state); `lc_territory_reset` takes it all back.

**Containment pay.** A unit who puts down the infested is paid for it once the shooting stops (or the patch is contained): `lc_containment_pay` credits a kill (3, at most 60 at a time), and rounds for the weapon they used (a few per kill; `lc_containment_ammo 0` turns that off). When a patch is contained or ground retaken, the whole city hears which unit did it.

**Containment** (the CP infestation job) sends you to where the infestation has spread to, if it has. A few extra infected crawl out for it on top of the usual number; nothing replaces them once they're down. From the moment you reach it, **nothing new spawns in the area** while the job is on, so whatever is there when you arrive is all there is to clear. The job isn't done until **every infested thing in the area is down** (the tracker counts how many are left). Then that patch is contained, and nothing infested spawns or spreads near it for `lc_infest_quiet_minutes` (10). Poison zombies are rare: about one in a hundred.

**You can see it, and fight over it.** Crossing into rebel territory or the infestation puts a line on everyone's screen. Taken ground gets dressed to match (`lc_dressing`): barrels, crates, pallets, old furniture, a table with chairs round it and a burning barrel for the rebels; rubbish, bones and blood for the infestation. Pieces are spaced out (never inside each other, a wall or a doorway), small junk lies where it falls, and zones drawn overlapping get a piece or two each rather than a full set (60 pieces a kind at most, city-wide). It appears out of sight as ground is taken, and is cleared away once ground is lost. Everything stands on the zone's own floor with solid ground all round it: a rebel camp on a rooftop is dressed on the roof, never hanging off its edge, and territory only spreads where someone could walk to it: across the same floor, or up or down a stairwell, ramp or ladder an NPC can use (by the map's nodegraph), never out over the street into thin air. A patch that spreads is one floor deep, so a patch on a roof isn't also the street under it, and it's shrunk to fit the floor it's on. Patches an older version grew over thin air are dropped, and ones reaching past an edge shrunk, on the next load. **The infestation looks and sounds wrong:** thick putrid yellow stains and scorch marks all over its ground, a heavy, dark, sickly yellow haze (fog, a yellow cast over everything and motes drifting in the air) that starts faintly a few metres before its edge and thickens steadily the deeper in you go, thickest in its middle, and a geiger counter clicking faster and faster, gently at the edge (40% calmer than it was, the owner asked) (`lc_infest_ambience 0` turns it off for you; the stains are painted by each player's own game, once, as ground is taken). CP and Overwatch get a **Retake the block** job (clear a grown rebel patch, then hold it 30 seconds and it's gone); the Resistance gets **Take ground** (hold a spot by its territory for a minute and it becomes theirs; dispatch sends units to look).

**Who holds the city.** The share of the city's walkable ground that's Combine, Resistance and infested is shown at the top of the F4 screen, and by `!city`. It changes the city (`lc_city_effects`): **unrest** (the Resistance holds `lc_unrest_share` of it, 12%, or 4+ grown patches) means more inspections and raids, rations every 1.5 times as long, a harsher PA, and a black market 15% cheaper; **full compliance** (nothing has spread) means skirmishes half as often and a black market 25% dearer. An infestation that's grown as far as it can **breaks out** (`lc_outbreaks`, at most every 30 minutes): six infected loose in the nearby streets and a lockdown. `lc_outbreak` (admin) starts one.

**One camp, however many zones.** Rebel zones you placed that touch, or nearly (`lc_rebel_merge`, 128 units apart or less), are one territory: a room that isn't a perfect box takes several zones to cover, and it's still one camp, with one quartermaster, one supply crate, one fire and one table, the scavenging runs leaving from it one at a time, and the zones after the first just a few more beds and crates. A quartermaster left over from a zone that was deleted, or a second one in the same territory, goes. **The quartermaster.** Each rebel territory you placed has one (named on your crosshair). He sells the black market's stock to the Resistance, and nothing to anyone else; if he's killed, someone takes his place five minutes later. **Fighters follow you:** a Resistance Member or above can press E on a rebel to bring them along (three at most), each with his own spot just behind you. A quartermaster or a post guard never joins a squad, and nobody stays in a squad from an earlier session: squads end when you leave or rejoin. **Following you comes first:** they never stop to chase anyone, take cover somewhere else or wander off after a unit. They run when you run, climb ladders after you (they wait at the foot while you climb, then come up the ladder after you), and one who falls far behind catches up out of sight. **They hold their fire until it's on,** so you can slip past a patrol with them: then they shoot at anything Combine that's shooting at you or at them, whatever you shoot at, units hunting the Resistance, and (on rebel ground) the usual. They shoot as they go, and stand and fire whenever they're already at your side. E again sends them home, and they go home if you die. To his own he charges what the lab does (`lc_lab_price` of the dealer's price), for everything he sells. A forged ration card costs 10. He hands out food free from the camp's supply crate, so he buys food and drink back for next to nothing (`lc_qm_food_price`, at most 2 credits; 0: he won't buy it).

**A Resistance squad** (E on fighters to bring them along) takes orders from the C menu (Resistance) or chat: **act casual** (`!casual`: guns away, plain clothes, and units take them for citizens; fired on, or the leader in a fight, they pull their guns at once), **guns out** (`!gunsout`), and **meet me there** (`!meet`: they go on ahead, acting casual, to your job's marker and wait for you). **Raids:** the Resistance's fighters plan a raid on the Combine a minute and a half ahead (`lc_raid_warning`, 90): the Resistance hear where, and "Back up the raid" goes up on their board (get there, take down two of the units they're fighting). **The other way round:** Civil Protection's sweeps into rebel territory are planned a minute and a half ahead too (`lc_sweep_warning`, 90): Civil Protection and Overwatch players hear where on their net and in a notice, and "Join the sweep" goes up on their board (get to the edge of rebel territory, take down two armed anticitizens); and the moment the Resistance hit the Combine, "Repel the attack" goes up for them (get there, take down two of the attackers). The Resistance hear nothing of a sweep until it moves in.

**A camp of your own.** A rebel zone gets as many fighters as it has room for (`lc_rebel_room`, 350: one for every 350 by 350 units of it); extras slip away out of sight, but not on ground a team is retaking, where the fighters holding it stay to be cleared. Nothing the city puts down there blocks a passage (it keeps `lc_walkway_clear`, 100 units, to walk by) or sits in the water; fighters and their things go on dry ground first. Sleepers lie on their mattresses, eyes shut. **Hold E** (half a second) on anything the city put in a camp (mattresses, couches, crates, the table and chairs, the fire, the supply crate, the pots) to pick it up: it goes where you look, along the floor; let go of E to set it down, R turns it. Looking down at your feet keeps it a step in front of you, and it's always set down on the floor, never in it, so it can be picked up again (a mattress, a model on its side, is lifted by how it lies). Where everything was left is kept for the map (`data/livingcity/<map>_camp.json`). **Spawning on top of someone** (put back where you left off, and an NPC standing there): you're moved to the nearest clear spot beside them, and anyone stuck inside a person for two seconds is moved out the same way, so nobody needs noclip.

**Units helping units.** `!backup` sends the three nearest NPC units running to you, armed; they go after anyone wanted, or any rebel, they find there (45-second cooldown). It also puts out a 10-20. `!10-20` (or `!mark`) marks where you are on every unit player's screen for 90 seconds; units who get there earn XP, 20 if they're quick, at least 3. Bind keys with `lc_backup_call` and `lc_1020`.

**Talk on the street.** What's happened lately (ground taken or lost, raids, skirmishes, outbreaks, arrests, deaths) gets around. Press E on a citizen and now and then they'll tell you something you haven't heard. Units mostly get nervous silence.

**Skirmishes**, now and then (`lc_skirmishes`, on; one every 20 to 30 minutes (`lc_skirmish_interval`, 25, give or take a fifth; a server still on the old default of 9 is moved to 25); never two at once, never during curfew or lockdown): either a few fighters slip out of rebel territory and **raid** the nearest checkpoint or patrol beat, and the units nearby draw pistols and fight them, or Civil Protection sends an armed **sweep** team into rebel territory (from a spot out of sight with a way in, along the city's own routes) and the fighters there fight back; the sweep's two minutes start when the team gets there. The Resistance radio and the CP net both hear about it. `lc_skirmish raid`, `lc_skirmish sweep`, `lc_skirmish end` (admin) start or stop one.

**Raids and operations are real, and you can find them (the owner, 7 November: "I have never actually seen rebel NPCs doing anything").** What was wrong is in `docs/dev/rebel-activity-audit.md`. A raid or a Resistance operation (a data heist, a prison break, a screen, a supply drop, an ambush) takes the idle fighters at home first, as many as it needs (never the quartermaster, a lookout on his post, one out scavenging, or anyone a player is with), and they walk out to the target along the city's routes (one stuck behind something gets past it where nobody can see). Still short, the room left under `lc_npc_budget` is used, then a spare NPC nobody can see (an idle fighter elsewhere, then a citizen) is taken out and a fighter comes in at the raid's hidden spot instead, so the city's NPC count doesn't go up. The Combine side does the same: a raid that reaches its target with fewer than two units near gets two or three: an idle unit within reach is sent as he is, or one is swapped in out of sight near the fight; a sweep team is swapped in the same way. Everyone a raid or sweep brought counts against `lc_npc_budget` until they're home or gone. Only when nothing can be moved or swapped may `lc_event_npc_allowance` (0: off) more come in above the cap. The raid's own two and a half minutes (four for an operation) start when the fighters get there; one that hasn't got there in six minutes is called off ("Our fighters couldn't get through", and no fight is claimed); a planned raid that can't go tells the Resistance so. The Resistance's players see **Our fighters** on their screens, following the party, from the moment it sets out; Civil Protection and Overwatch players see **10-20: ANTICITIZEN ATTACK** once the fight starts. What says a fight happened (rations docked "near a Resistance attack", the PA's `skirmish_raid` line, the rumours, the raid tip) waits until the fighters are really at the target. **The Commissary's restrictions** add to the parties: one more fighter from 3 restrictions, two from 5, then one more for each past that (at most `lc_market_raid_max`, 6), and the restrictions' fighters rebel territory has no room for (or the cap keeps out) go into the parties too. How often raids, sweeps and operations come is unchanged.

**Manpower: the dead aren't replaced at once (the owner, 7 November: "there needs to be a real hit to the resistance that is actually visible and tangible").** Each side has a reserve: the Resistance's fighters (`lc_manpower_rebel_max`, 20), Civil Protection's units (`lc_manpower_cp_max`, 20) and Overwatch's soldiers, their own small pool (`lc_manpower_ota_max`, 12). `lc_npc_budget` still caps how many are out at once. Every fighter, unit or soldier the city brings out takes one from that side's reserve: rebel territory's top-up, `LC.ReinforceRebels`, raid parties and the swaps for them, lookout posts, waves, quest allies; patrols, posts, sweep teams, responders, backup, cleanup guards, garrisons, convoys, drops. A death is a loss; one who goes home or is taken away alive goes back in the reserve. An empty reserve means nobody comes until a recruit does: fewer fighters at home, fewer patrols, smaller raid parties and sweep teams. Not counted: the quartermaster, a quest's own people, the Administrator's guard, the interrogator, the stalker technician (dreams, endings and stories make their own NPCs). The Resistance recruit one every `lc_manpower_rebel_minutes` (8), faster for each of the Commissary's restrictions (`lc_manpower_restrict_pct`, 15% each), for citizens' sympathy and for each patch of rebel territory past the first, and slower after heavy losses (6 or more in an hour). The restrictions' extra fighters at home (`LC.MarketRebelBonus`) still raise how many the Resistance keep there, but now come from the reserve, which the restrictions also fill faster. Civil Protection get one every `lc_manpower_cp_minutes` (5), Overwatch one every `lc_manpower_ota_minutes` (20), and an Overwatch drop brings its soldiers as a batch. While a side has fewer than `lc_manpower_hold` (3) in reserve, its raids (or sweeps) are postponed, and its own are told why (radio and a notice). After a fight that cost a side three or more, its radio says so ("We lost four fighters near Block 2. We're stretched thin: 3 left in reserve.") and the other side hears it as good news. The Resistance see their numbers on F2 > Resistance and in the C menu's Resistance menu ("Fighters: 7 in reserve (of 20), 3 lost today", "Next recruit in about 12 minutes"); units on the unit tools (F2) and the records terminal's Manpower tab; the Administrator on F2 > Combine manpower and her tablet's office (Combine manpower), and on the records terminal too (the owner, 7 November). Kept per map in `data/livingcity/<map>_manpower.json` (whoever is out when the server stops counts as home). `lc_manpower 0` turns it off (sv_livingcity_manpower.lua).

**The Combine grows with the Resistance's players** (the owner, 9 November: "if there were 40 rebel players, they could wipe out the CP units in a firefight or two"; `LC.ManpowerMax`, `LC.ManpowerInterval`): for every Resistance player online past 4, Civil Protection's reserve holds 2 more units and Overwatch's 1 more soldier, and both refill 5% faster. With 40 Resistance players that's about 92 Civil Protection and 48 Overwatch in reserve, refilling nearly three times as fast. On a small server nothing changes. (`lc_npc_budget` still caps how many NPCs are out at once.)

**Help for the outnumbered side** (the owner, 9 November: "if 15 resistance players and 5 cp/overwatch, then the cp/overwatch side gets an addition/excess temporary NPC budget of 20 (10 player difference x2) ... make sure all the excess NPCs don't end up standing only in combine HQ ... a sort of 'reserve' combine soldier group that is ready to deploy near (... like a 15 ish second response time, not instant psychic appearance ...)"; `sv_livingcity_imbalance.lua`). When one side's players are at least double the other's (and at least 4), the outnumbered side gets 2 NPCs for every player of difference (at most 40), on top of the NPC cap. For the Combine: half walk the streets as extra Civil Protection patrols, spawned out of sight like the city's own; the other half is a reaction force that isn't on the map until it's needed. When a Resistance player gets violent (a unit killed, or Alert 2 and up), a rebel raid arrives, or the Resistance attacks a checkpoint or the Nexus (a unit killed within 600 units of a checkpoint or in a Combine-only zone, by a Resistance player or a rebel NPC; a checkpoint's power bank shorted by a Resistance player; a forcefield broken), a squad of Overwatch soldiers is sent: 4 soldiers; or 5 and a Combine Elite leading them (the owner: "if sociostability is red and there is lots of rebel fighting ... 5 soldiers and an elite leading them") when sociostability is red, when 3 or more units have died in the last five minutes, or for an attack on a checkpoint or the Nexus. With open sky 500 to 1400 units from the trouble, they come in by Combine dropship (the owner: "yes please"), the Elite first out of the door, and run the rest of the way; otherwise they appear out of everyone's sight about 2800 units away, roughly fifteen seconds' run. One squad to a place every 45 seconds; they stay five minutes and slip away when nobody's looking. The reaction force always has room for at least one heavy squad. They come out of the Combine's reserves like any unit. For the Resistance outnumbered: more fighters in rebel territory, where there's room. The Combine manpower readout says so.

**The NPC cap grows with the server** (the owner, 9 November: "can we have this scale with the max player count? 100 NPCs seems to be ok on our 32 man server"; then: "on a 32 slot server, max NPC count should be 100, on a 64: 175 NPCs, and 120-128: 250 NPCs"; `LC.NPCBudget`, `LC.NPCBudgetSteps`): `lc_npc_budget` is the cap for a 32-slot server, 1.75 times that at 64 slots and 2.5 times at 120 and up (in a straight line between), never less than the setting on a smaller server, and never more than 250 (past that the engine's AI starts to drag). With the default 100: 100, 175, 250. The outnumbered side's extra is on top.

**Tougher Combine units** (the owner, 9 November: "rather than flood the server with 150 Combine NPCs ... just make all CP/Combine/Overwatch units have more health ... they need to be relatively consistent"; `LC.ToughenUnit`): every Civil Protection NPC has `lc_cp_health` (70; Half-Life 2's own is 40), every Overwatch soldier `lc_soldier_health` (90; Half-Life 2's 50) and every Elite `lc_elite_health` (150; 120 before). Always the same, never by player count. A Nova Prospekt guard on a garrison still has half again a soldier's (135). The three are sliders in the editor's Population section; changed there, the units already out change too, just as hurt as they were.

**The desperate Combine** (the owner, 9 November: "let's make their actions reflect their desperation ... the more outnumbered, the more SMGs, grenades, and combine energy balls are used"; `sv_livingcity_desperation.lua`, `LC.Desperation`): when the Resistance's players outnumber the Combine's, the Combine's NPCs fight dirtier, more so the more outnumbered they are. How desperate (0 to 1) comes from the ratio of Resistance to Combine players, once there are at least 4 Resistance players: nothing below 1.5 to 1, 0.2 at 2 to 1, 0.6 at 3 to 1, all of it at 4 to 1 and more. It moves a small step every 5 seconds (about a minute and a half from none to full), so nothing jumps when someone joins or leaves. Everyone gets a notice when it starts and when it ends, and the Combine readout says how desperate they are. At that level:
- Metrocops given a gun are issued an SMG instead of a pistol, as big a share of them as the desperation (a fifth at 0.2, all at 1); cops already carrying a pistol swap it as it rises.
- Soldiers carry frags (up to 3, thrown by Half-Life 2's own AI) and also throw grenades themselves at Resistance fighters (players and rebel NPCs) they can see 250 to 1100 units away: half frags, a quarter the Combine's poison gas, a quarter flashbangs. One soldier throws every 45 seconds when barely desperate, down to every 12 at full, and no one person gets more than one every 12 to 3 seconds. Never a frag or gas where a unit, a Combine player or a citizen would be caught (gas only matters if an Insider fouled their masks); their own flashbangs don't blind their own.
- Elites carry up to twice the energy balls (2 to 4), topped up every 30 seconds, and fire extra ones at Resistance fighters they can see 300 to 1600 units away (every 30 seconds down to every 8), never with one of their own in the way.
- Every Combine NPC does up to 10% more damage to players.
`lc_desperation 0` (an editor tick box) turns it all off.

**Resistance fighters** (the owner, 9 November: "I would expect them to be weaker, they aren't wearing armor and aren't military trained, but they can't be too weak ... slightly more resistance NPCs than combine"): every fighter has `lc_rebel_health` (50; Half-Life 2's own is 40), at home or in a player's squad alike (`lc_rebel_home_hurt` 1: they used to take double damage at home). They carry SMGs (70%), shotguns (20%) or crossbows (10%), never pulse rifles; a lookout post's are as before (three SMGs to a shotgun). There are never fewer than `lc_rebel_per_combine` (1.25) fighters for each Combine NPC on the streets (`LC.CombineStreetCount`: those posted inside the Combine's own buildings don't count), within the room in the rebel zones, the Resistance's reserve and the NPC cap. Both are editor sliders (Population). A soldier (90) is about twice as tough as a fighter, a metrocop (70) 1.4 times.

**Testing the desperate Combine alone** (the owner, 9 November: "it's just me, there are no other human players, so I need to be able to test how it feels"; `LC.DesperationTestSet`, `LC.DesperationTestSquad`): the editor's section "Desperate Combine: test it" has a button for each level (0%, 20%, 40%, 60%, 80%, 100%), each with a line on what player counts cause it and what it brings. A level is pinned at once (no creeping up) for 30 minutes, and while it is the tester is the grenades' and energy balls' target whatever their side. Three buttons send units at the tester from out of sight: 4 soldiers, an Elite and 3 soldiers, or 3 armed metrocops (pistols or SMGs as desperate as they are); they leave after 5 minutes. "Back to normal" unpins it and removes the test units. Admin extras: `lc_desperation_test <percent | off>`, `lc_desperation_squad <soldiers | elite | cops>`.

**Guerrillas, not an army.** The fighters don't open up on the Combine the moment it's in sight. A unit (or a convincing uniform) has to come onto rebel ground and within about 1100 units to be shouted at, and then within 1500 (or 900 unseen) to be shot at; the city's own units on rebel ground, within 1000 if they've come for a fight, and 650 if they've only wandered in. Out of their territory they keep their heads down, because **the city's units notice an armed rebel in the street** within `lc_units_spot_rebels` units (600, in front of them and in plain sight) and open fire (dispatch hears about it): a fighter out on his own walks rather than runs, and backs off out of sight rather than walk past one.

**Rebels at home** (`lc_rebel_life`) keep busy instead of standing about: someone always walks the edge of the zone, stopping to look out, once two or three are home; others work at the lab bench, a workbench or the camp table (reaching, tinkering, cleaning a gun), go through the crates, tend the garden pots, carry a box from one crate to another, sit round the burning barrel, sit on a chair, a couch or a bench, lean on a wall, or sleep on a mattress, a bed or a bunk (more of them sleep at night). What each one's doing shows under their name. It's all put down the moment they're needed: a shout of "Combine!", a fight in the zone, a lookout's warning (on their feet, guns up, facing the trouble; the shouting wakes the sleepers), someone asking them along, or a raid.

**Scavenging runs** (`lc_rebel_errands`): now and then (`lc_rebel_errand_minutes`, 12 on average (it was 4 until 7 November, the owner: food scarcity), for each rebel zone you placed with three or more fighters at home, while anyone's on the server) one or two slip out to scavenge at a bin, a junk pile or a salvage point within reach, or just somewhere in the streets. They walk, keep out of sight of units (backing off and waiting it out; three times and it's too hot out there, so home), search for a while, and carry what they found home, into the zone's supply crate: a meal or two for the Resistance. A unit who catches one in the open opens fire, and they fight their way home. `lc_rebel_errand` (admin) sends one out of the nearest rebel zone now.

**The Resistance plans its own operations (the owner, 10 November).** A Resistance Lieutenant or Commander (a Fighter when neither is online) opens **F2 > Resistance > Operations: plan or join** and picks a target the city offers right now: a data terminal to raid, a city screen to hijack, one of ours to break out of the prison, or a supply drop or Overwatch landing while one is on. Then when: in 2, 5 or 10 minutes. The cell hears it on Freq 7, and any Resistance player can join (or back out) on the same page; the planner can call it off. When the time comes the fighters go (the same operation the city's own rebels run), everyone who joined gets a waypoint to the target, and if it works, everyone who joined and was within reach earns 25 experience and 15 credits, and the planner 10 more experience. With another fight going on, it waits up to five minutes, then it's called off. One plan at a time, at least `lc_rebel_plan_minutes` (15) apart. Someone always talks: `lc_rebel_plan_leak` (15%) of plans reach Civil Protection beforehand, the target and roughly when. `lc_rebel_plans` 0 turns it off.

**Bounties (the Combine's answer).** The City Administrator, or a unit of Division Leader rank or higher, can put a price on a wanted person's head: **F2 > Me > Bounties on the wanted**, 50 or 100 credits (one every two minutes). Every unit hears of it, and so does the person. A unit player who brings them in collects it and 10 experience; taken in by the city's own units, or no longer wanted, the bounty lapses. `lc_bounties` 0 turns it off.

## The state of the city, day to day (the owner, 10 November; phase one)

**Five numbers**, 0 to 100 (50 to start), kept with the map in `data/livingcity/<map>_city.json` and rolling on from one city day (`lc_day_minutes`) to the next:
- **Supply** (the city's stores): a supply drop that comes in +8; looting it -2; the Administrator's extra rations -15; every day eats 4. Under 20, morale sinks each day.
- **Morale** (the people's mood): unrest wears it down a little every five minutes, calm lifts it; speeches and amnesties +6; crackdowns -6. Under 30, the Resistance grows each day.
- **Civil Protection's strength**: arrests of Resistance members +1, raids +1, each unit killed by a player -1, rebel operations that work -2. Above 50 more units walk the streets, below fewer (up to 4 either way).
- **The Resistance's strength**: operations that work +4, arrests of their members -1, raids that find things -2. Above 50 more fighters in the camps, below fewer (up to 4).
- **The Administrator's authority**: speeches +3, crackdowns +5, amnesties -8, rebel operations -2. It sets how many of the city's citizens are secret informants (half as many at 0, half again at 100).
Every day each number drifts 2 back toward 50. While the server is empty the city goes on: the days it missed (up to a week) pass quietly when it starts again. `lc_ledger_effects` 0 keeps the numbers but leaves the streets alone.

**Districts with an owner.** Each housing block is a district: the Combine's, contested, or the Resistance's. A rebel operation that works pushes the district it hit toward the Resistance; a block raid pushes it back; each day a district drifts back toward contested. A **crackdown** marks a district as the Combine's for two days; a district **liberated** by the collapse is the Resistance's for three, then the Combine come back for it (a block raid, on the next morning someone is here to see it).

**The collapse leaves a mark.** The Administrator killed: the district nearest rebel ground is liberated, the Resistance +15, morale +10, authority -25. The uprising crushed: a crackdown week in every district, authority +15, the Resistance -15, morale -10. She steps down or ends it: authority starts again at 50.

**The news comes the city's way** (the owner: no new windows): each city morning a **City Bulletin** on the city screens and in chat, what changed and why ("Civil Protection raided Block 4: 3 things confiscated. The Resistance struck in Block 2. Supply low. Morale steady."). Anyone can read the state of the city on **F2 > Me > The state of the city** (the numbers in words, every district and who holds it, this morning's bulletin).

**The Administrator's levers** (F2 > Me > The city's levers), each with its cost:
- **Go to the speech stand** (editor: "Point: speech stand"; she's taken there, facing the way it faces), then **Give a speech**: her words on the screens and in chat; morale +6, authority +3, and every citizen within earshot +2 standing. Once a city day. With no stand placed she speaks from where she is.
- **Declare an amnesty**: every wanted citizen forgiven. Morale +6, authority -8, the Resistance +3. Once a city day.
- **Order a crackdown on a district**: a block raid there now and the district marked for two days. Authority +5, morale -6, the Resistance +4.
- **Order extra rations**: a ration day now. Supply -15, morale +8; not with the stores under 20.

**On the streets (phase two):**
- **A district under a crackdown has a checkpoint:** a Civil Protection unit posted in the middle of it, stopping and searching whoever passes, until the crackdown ends.
- **The Resistance's districts are dangerous for patrols:** a Combine unit walking through one may be ambushed (at most every two minutes per district, a one in four chance): two fighters from out of sight, who melt away a couple of minutes later. Units hear it on the CP net.
- **The black market is cheapest on Resistance ground:** a quarter off for anyone buying inside a Resistance-held district (never under what the dealer pays back).
- **Supply sets the Commissary's prices:** dearer as the stores run down (up to 40% more with nothing left), cheaper when they're full (up to 15% off).
- **Acts:** every `lc_ledger_act_days` (14) real days the act ends. The side holding more districts (and the stronger) wins it: the bulletin says so and every player of that side online earns 50 experience. Then the city starts over: the five numbers back to 50 and every district contested. Characters, records and legacy are untouched. 0 turns acts off.

`lc_ledger` 0 turns all of it off; `lc_ledger_effects` 0 keeps the numbers and the districts but leaves the streets (units, fighters, informants, checkpoints, ambushes, prices) alone.

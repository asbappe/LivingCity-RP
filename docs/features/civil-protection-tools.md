# Civil Protection's tools

**The unit's quick menu** (F2; the owner, 7 November, moved it off F1, which now has what everyone has) puts what a unit does most on the number keys (voice lines first, they need the fastest answer): 1 Voice lines, 2 Unit tools, 3 Kick, 4 Call for backup, 5 Issue a BOLO, 6 Send my 10-20, and on 7 and 8 search, cuff, escort or take a microphone for whoever you're looking at (or walk a prisoner to the cells), else My directives (F4) and the records terminal. Dragging and uncuffing someone cuffed are on F1, as for anyone. The Administrator's F2: Sociostability and a broadcast.

**Supplies: the Civil Protection Commissary** (editor: "Fixture: Civil Protection Commissary"). Units don't make anything, so F3 has no crafting for them: it says to visit a Civil Protection Commissary, with a button that marks the nearest one on your screen (gone when you get there). Press E there and you're issued what your rank allows: grenades, ammunition for the issued weapons you carry (back to a full load), armour (50; officers 75, Overwatch 100), up to two bandages, and your ration once every `lc_cpsupply_ration` (600) seconds. Once every `lc_cpsupply_cooldown` (120) seconds; then the unit counter opens for anything more. Whatever the wait (7 November): each **ration voucher** you carry is exchanged for a ration packet; once every city day (`lc_day_minutes`) you get **today's kit**, a health vial (on the floor in front of the Commissary) and a bandage, on top of the issue; and **standing at it charges your vest**, `lc_cpsupply_recharge` (5) a second, up to your issue (50, officers 75, Overwatch 100), not with its power out. **The unit counter is the only place units buy** ammunition, grenades, cuffs and suit batteries: the Union Commissary terminals sell citizens' things and nothing else. Citizens are turned away. A city with none placed: the Union Commissary terminal issues units their supplies, and sells them those, instead.

**Unit tools** (C > Unit tools, at the top of the full menu for units; or bind a key to `lc_unittools`) puts every one of these in one window, each button with a line saying what it does: **Dispatch** (call for backup, send your 10-20, issue a BOLO), **Records** (the terminal), **Your mask** (voice lines), **In front of you** (search, cuff, cut ties, drag, escort, take a microphone, for whoever you were looking at when you opened it), **Checkpoints** (a turret, a power bank), **Escorts** (workers waiting for one, and handing yours back), **Post office** (reading the mail), **Sociostability** (officers) and **Your work** (your directives, F4). The full menu's Civil Protection section has the same, as a list. The chat commands below still work as shortcuts.

- **The records terminal** (`!records`, or bind a key to `lc_records`; units only, and a stolen uniform doesn't get you in). Every citizen has a record kept by CID: violations, which the city writes in itself as they happen (wanted, custody, contraband found, escape), notes units add, and their **loyalty points** (Civil Standing). Units can log a violation, add a note, and award or deduct loyalty points: 10 at a time, 25 for officers, once every two minutes per citizen. Records survive restarts (`garrysmod/data/livingcity/records.json`), and people who aren't on can be searched by name or CID. Someone living on **forged papers** has a record under that number instead, and it starts clean. A **Manpower** tab (the owner, 7 November) shows Civil Protection's and Overwatch's reserves, the same lines as the unit tools: in reserve, lost today, and when the next unit or soldier comes (not there while `lc_manpower` is 0).
- **Scanner reports.** When a scanner flown from a Combine console photographs a rebel, a free vortigaunt, the wanted, a BOLO subject, an anticitizen, a zombie or rebel ground, every unit gets the report and the people it caught are outlined in red through walls for a minute (see Flying a scanner, under The city). A unit can fly a scanner from a console too.
- **BOLOs** ("be on the lookout"): issued from a record, or on a description alone. Every unit hears it, and a citizen with one out on them shows it on their nametag to units. They expire after `lc_bolo_minutes` (30). One unit can issue one every 30 seconds (staff any time).
- **Cuffs and walking people along.** `!cuff` (or `!tie`), looking at someone up close, cuffs them in three seconds, if their hands are up (`!handsup`), they're down, a unit has stopped them, or you're behind them. Units carry their own; anyone else needs cuffs (Commissary for units, the black market for everyone else). Tied: no weapons, nothing in your pockets, walking pace. `!drag` takes them by the arm and walks them along with you (`!drag` again lets go); the baton's arrest takes them in, and their things go with them. `!uncuff` (or `!untie`) (instant for a unit, four seconds for anyone else). Left alone and unseen, a tied person can `!struggle` free in 30 seconds (`lc_struggle_seconds`).
- **Sociostability**, city-wide: green (nominal), yellow (marginal: half as many stops again), red (judgment waiver: twice as many, and every metrocop has his pistol out instead of his stick until it's lifted). The city sets it from what's happening (curfew, sweeps, raids, unrest, a skirmish, anyone at alert 2: yellow; a lockdown, an outbreak's infected loose in the streets (only while they're out of the infestation, at most five minutes), anyone at alert 3: red), with the PA and a line in chat when it changes, which says why ("Sociostability: Marginal. (curfew)"). Headcrabs and zombies inside an infestation don't touch it. Only the Administrator sets it (the owner, 7 November: "CP Recruits should not be able to change the sociostability; only the Administrator"; before, any officer, Division Leader and up, and any admin could, which is why a recruit who was a server admin saw it), for a while: F2 > Sociostability, C > The city > Set sociostability, the buttons on the records terminal, or `!socio <green|yellow|red> [minutes]`, `!socio auto`. Anyone else is refused ("Only the Administrator sets sociostability"), and the controls aren't shown to them. Everyone sees it at the top of the screen when it isn't green. **Units murdered by NPCs** (the owner, 9 November: "if NPC rebels kill units then it's a 50% effect: 2 murdered CP units by NPCs causes yellow and 4 causes red"): a Civil Protection or Overwatch unit killed by an NPC citizen, rebel or vortigaunt counts for ten minutes; two make the city yellow, four red, and more are needed on a full server (past 16 players: 32, half again; 64, two and a half times; 128, four and a half; `LC.SocioScale`). A player's murder is as before (it makes them wanted).
- **The mask.** Units (and anyone in a CP uniform) see the city through the mask's lenses: darker edges, a cold tint, and a readout with their designation, sociostability and the number of BOLOs out. It stays on in build mode, and goes when you change faction. `lc_mask_overlay 0` turns it off for you.
- **Duty allocation.** Units don't queue at the ration line. Every ten minutes (its own timer: the slower dispenser, `lc_ration_interval`, is for citizens) a unit on duty is issued a **ration voucher** and their ration coupons as credits: `lc_cp_coupons` (8) plus one per rank. No packet out of thin air (the owner, 7 November): the voucher is a ration packet at a Civil Protection Commissary or a ration dispenser (E on either). It's the same allocation a dispenser gives, so a unit with no voucher waits for the next. Overwatch don't eat, and get the coupons only.
- **`/request`.** A citizen with a **Civil Protection call box** (was "call box"; Union Commissary, 10 credits) calls Civil Protection to where they are: `/request <what's wrong>`. Units get the message and a marker on screen for two minutes, and it's listed on the terminal; with no units on, the nearest Civil Protection NPC walks over. Once a minute (`lc_request_cooldown`). A false request is a violation a unit can log.
- **Voice lines.** Units speak in the mask's voice: `!vo` (or bind a key to `lc_vo_menu`) for a menu of about fifty lines, `/vo <word>`, or say a line's exact words in chat ("Move along."). Overwatch have their own. The list is in `gamemodes/livingcity/gamemode/modules/shared/sh_livingcity_cp.lua`.

**A unit's search is a pat-down** (the owner, 7 November; `sv_livingcity_patsearch.lua`, `cl_livingcity_patsearch.lua`). Searching a player or an NPC citizen (baton reload, `!search`, F2, C > Unit tools > In front of you) opens a quick menu, like F1 and F2 (number keys, 0 puts it away, which ends the search; since 13 November, the owner: "they should close other opened F1-F4 menus and popup, giving the player a quick and easy way to respond"; `lc_situation_windows 1` on a player's own client brings back the old window with the body to click): one number for each spot, **Jacket, Waist, Legs, Boots**. Whatever today's search would find on them (contraband and illegal guns, each with its usual chance to slip past) is spread over the four at random when the search starts; an NPC citizen carries something with the same odds as before, in one spot. Click a spot: a second's check fills it, then it says what was there ("Nothing." if empty), and that is taken. **Done**, closing the window, or walking more than about 140 units apart ends it, and everything found is confiscated exactly as a search always was (the messages, standing, wanted for possession, the unit's XP and credits, the records, the evidence stock). A spot never checked keeps what's in it, so the skill is checking them all. The person searched sees a small panel: the four spots with their own things in each, which one is being checked, "Stand still.", and **R**: once a spot has been checked, they may try once to slip one thing from an unchecked spot into a checked one (60%: it's never found; otherwise the unit's window flashes "They tried to move something: ..." and it's found there and then). Walking off is evading a search, as before (wanted, "Evading a search"). Nothing a spot holds is sent to the unit before it's checked, and the server checks every click (the unit searching, close enough, one spot at a time, at least 0.8 seconds apart). With `lc_jobgames` 0, or if the unit's window doesn't open within the three seconds a search has always taken, it is the old search: three seconds of standing still, then everything is found at once.

`lc_cp_tools 0` turns all of this off.

**Changes from the 7 November playtest.**
- **Running isn't prison:** caught running the first or second time in an hour, a unit cuffs you, gives you three blows of the baton, laughs and says "All right, you can go." No cell and no standing lost. The third time in an hour is prison as before. Giving yourself up for running goes the same way.
- **No shove for your own:** a unit never shoves the player it is escorting or who called it for backup, and nobody is told to stop running or move back in the infestation zone.
- **Turrets and power banks are the Overwatch's** (and admins'): ordinary units and recruits no longer deploy them, and the buttons are hidden from them.
- **No Scanner Operator role:** any unit flies a scanner from a terminal (F2 > Unit tools).
- **"Freeze"** plays "Don't move!" (the clip it pointed at isn't in the metrocop voice set).
- **Backup joins your squad** for 5 minutes (was 2): chat says as each unit arrives, and they attack whoever you hit or whoever hurts you.
- **Killing a rebel** gives a unit +5 standing, +2 credits and a box of rounds for the gun in hand (with the AR2, which has no ammo box, 30 AR2 rounds straight in: the owner, 7 November), with a notice, and 5 XP (`LCF.XP.rebel_kill`; the owner, 8 November: "5 xp per kill"), a player rebel the same as an NPC one (before, a player rebel gave no XP).
- **The night shift** (the owner, 8 November; `sv_livingcity_bedtime.lua`). Units are due in bed at the good night like everyone (lights out, see sleep in `survival-and-credit.md`), unless they take the **Night shift** directive, on the board (F4) first, from dusk on most nights (`lc_nightshift_chance`, 0.85): walk the patrol stops through the night (reward 30 credits plus 5 a stop). On it, or once it's done that night, there's no lights out for them, and a CP Commissary issues one free stimulant shot (once a night; the one-shot rule still applies to taking it). The city's own units patrol either way.
- **Enforcers in the dreams**: a unit or Administration member asleep for a Nightmare or a propaganda dream is an Enforcer in it: untouchable, free to wake, and with a baton for the dreamers who misbehave (1 XP each, paid on waking). See `endings.md`, Enforcers.
- **Raiding rebel ground at night**: anyone in a rebel camp attacked by a unit (shot, batoned or otherwise) or arrested wakes everyone asleep there (the rebels' own shots never do), players out of their dreams too, groggy for a minute (`survival-and-credit.md`, Woken in your sleep).
- **No implant signal**: a rebel who had the chip pulled at the lab is noticed by a unit checking papers or searching them 15% of the time (`lc_chip_notice`): a crime, and the papers fail (`endings.md`, The foil hat and the chip).
- **Vests at a power bank:** hold E on a live bank to add 15 charge a press. Anyone who isn't a unit commits "Tampering" if a unit sees it; up close, the bank says which applies to you.
- **Prison guards** keep out of cells, the release spot and the prison doors, so a door is never held open by a guard standing in it.

**Arrests in the open** (the owner's playtest, 7 November; `sv_livingcity_arrest.lua`). The Arrest Baton's right click no longer sends anyone straight to a cell.
- **Who:** players (not Civil Protection), citizens, and now **rebels** (NPC rebels used to be refused with "only citizens can be arrested").
- **A struggle first.** A player always gets the chance (not with their hands up, down, or beaten down by all `lc_subdue_hits` blows); a citizen NPC sometimes (`lc_arrest_struggle_chance`, 25 percent, twice that for a sympathizer); a rebel NPC always. For `lc_arrest_struggle_time` seconds (4) both sides press E as fast as they can (the mouse is left alone; E is a press, not a use, while it lasts). **Who is winning is felt, not shown on a bar** (the owner, 12 November; `cl_livingcity_arrest.lua`): every press jolts the view with a scuffle, the edges of the screen close in as you lose and open as you win, a line says what your body feels ("Their grip is slipping!"), and a heartbeat comes in for the last seconds. `lc_struggle_bar 1` (each player's own client) brings the old bar back. The one resisting needs `lc_arrest_struggle_ratio` (1.25) times the officer's presses, plus two, to break free; a tie goes to the officer. Against an NPC the officer plays alone (a rebel fights harder than a citizen). `lc_arrest_struggle 0` turns struggles off.
- **Broken free:** they shove the officer back (the unit's shove, and a moment dazed) and run. A player is wanted for "Resisting arrest"; an NPC runs flat out, straight away from the officer and on to a spot well off on the far side, and the nearest free unit goes after them (not a job's suspect: that one is left for the player to catch). They can't be grabbed again for a couple of seconds.
- **The officer gone:** if the officer dies, leaves, or walks more than 220 units off during the struggle, it just ends: the player is let go with "The officer is gone: the struggle is over." (an NPC goes back to what it was doing). That is not a win: nobody is wanted for it and nobody broke free (the owner, 7 November).
- **Held:** cuffed where they stand. The officer can walk them in himself (a player: F1 > Drag them along; an NPC: F2 > Walk them to the cells / let go) and at the cell they're locked in; or leave it, and the nearest free unit walks over and walks them to the cells (a player the way a unit walks in anyone it arrests, still counted as the officer's arrest). With no unit free, an NPC prisoner follows the officer. Nobody to come for `lc_arrest_wait` seconds (60), no way to walk there, or a walk taking longer than `lc_march_max`: taken in, as before. `lc_march 0` puts everyone straight in a cell, as before.
- **Units arresting a citizen at an inspection** walk them in too. A rebel in custody has no gun (given back on release). Arrests a job or a story makes off screen (a neighbour picked up by evening, a prisoner to break out) still put them straight in a cell.
- **The Apprehend job:** a failed Authority check, and the suspect shoves you back and runs, the whole way, at a run.

**Changes from the 7 November playtest (part 2).**
- **Backup comes armed to a gunfight:** call for backup with a gun in your hand, shot at in the last 30 seconds, or on a violent job, and the units come with pistols and SMGs (half and half), and keep them in hand while they're with you (calming down after a fight, or Half-Life 2's own weapon choice, used to put the stunstick back in their hand). Dispatch says so: "Weapons free: they're coming with pistols and SMGs." Units already with a player for a job aren't pulled away by someone else's call.
- **My career** (F4 > Career, or C > "My career: rank, XP and the way to the Overwatch", which opens that tab): your rank and the XP to the next one, every rank and what it takes (which need the conditioning), and the way up in plain words: where the Overwatch Candidate story stands for you (waiting on a few more jobs, on your board now, a failed part coming back and when), the Man Inside if it's yours, and that nothing you fail locks you out. Every faction with ranks has the page; the way to the Overwatch is Civil Protection's.

## A unit's shift: the quota, papers duty, inspections, the review (the owner, 10 November)

Something for Civil Protection to do when no rebels are about. Every Civil Protection player on duty works shifts of `lc_cp_shift_minutes` (30 again since 12 November, the owner: "just make the shift back to 30 minutes then"; it was 15 from 10 November), each with a quota. **F2 > Me > My shift** shows the time left and the quota so far, and holds the work:

- **Searches** (`lc_cp_quota_searches`, 3): anyone searched counts, a player (the pat-down) or one of the city's people.
- **Checkpoint papers duty** (`lc_cp_quota_papers`, 1): F2 > Me > My shift > Checkpoint papers duty. Travelers walk up one at a time; E on each shows their papers beside today's rules (expiry, the photo, a banned district, the stamps); approve, deny or call it in. It's the Intake Processor's papers check, done by a unit. Seeing most of them through (3 of 5) counts.
- **A residence inspection** (`lc_cp_quota_inspections`, 1): F2 > Me > My shift > Get an inspection order names a resident (a housed citizen or rebel who's online) and their block. Go to the block and F2 > Me > My shift > Inspect the residence (8 seconds). Their stash is gone through: each contraband item is found 4 times in 5, confiscated, and the resident is charged exactly as a block raid charges (standing lost, wanted); they're told their residence was inspected, clean or not. The unit gets 5 experience for each thing found.
  An Insider also sees **Report it clean** there (see `resistance-insider.md`).
- **Arrests** aren't in the quota, but count toward the review.
- **The performance review** at the end of the shift: a grade from the quota met (A all of it, B two thirds, C a third, D less), arrests adding a little, and experience for it (A 30, B 20, C 10), which is what promotes a unit. The last five reviews are kept: F2 > Me > My shift > My last reviews.
- **No waiting out the clock** (the owner, 10 November: "adjust it so CP units do not have to take breaks between shifts if they so choose"): with the checklist done, F2 > Me > My shift > "Report in now" gives the review at once and starts the next shift; otherwise the next shift starts the moment one ends.
- **A break, if they want one:** My shift > "Go on a break": no shift and no review until F2 > Me > "Back on duty", which starts a fresh shift (the one they were on is dropped).
- `lc_cp_duty` 0 turns it off.

## Jobs that protect something on its way (11 November)

The jobs to secure a landing zone (`cp_securelz`) and to guard a supply drop (`cp_guardsupply`) are on the board for 60 seconds (`LC.ProtectBoardSecs`). If a player takes one, what it protects (the dropship, the supply drop) arrives 60 seconds after that (`LC.ProtectTakenSecs`); if nobody does, it arrives one second after the job leaves the board, and the job is gone. Everyone on the job sees "The dropship arrives in 0:42" in the job tracker, top right. With no unit online to take the job, a dropship comes after six seconds and a supply drop at once, as before. The Resistance's answer to a landing is counted from the landing. A landing party counts as lost only once somebody had landed and all of them are dead. (`LC.ProtectWait` in sv_livingcity_quests.lua.)

## The housing block inspection (11 November)

The trail leads to a door of the block itself (the one nearest its middle), not the block's middle. The door is shut and locked until the breaching charge (E on it) or a kick (G, or F2 > Kick) opens it; any door of the block within 900 units will do. The stash furniture is set down on the room's floor, out from the middle only as far as the walls allow.

## Searching what they carry, and papers on the street (11 November)

- **A fifth spot.** When the person searched carries a suitcase, a parcel, a box or a crate, the search window has a fifth spot named for it ("Suitcase", "Parcel"), beside the legs. For an NPC, half the time what they hide is in it. A clipboard is not searched.
- **Papers.** "Done searching: check their papers" in the search window (a citizen NPC), or F2 > "Check their papers" with a citizen in front of you (within 160 units): the papers window from the station job, against today's rules (the same for every unit, for an hour). "In order: wave them on", "Out of order: cite them", "Forged: take them in". About one in five is out of order and about one in sixteen forged (`LC.StreetPapersWrong` 0.2, `LC.StreetPapersForged` 0.06). Out of order (or forged) and cited: 1 credit and +5 Civil Standing (3 credits for a forgery taken in). In order and cited: -2 Civil Standing. Waved on: nothing, and nobody tells you what you missed. The same citizen can be asked again after five minutes.

## Company on a patrol (11 November)

On the night shift (`cp_nightshift`) or a patrol job (`cp_patrol`), 40 seconds in and then at most once in two and a half minutes (a one in two chance each half minute), a unit meets somebody (`sv_livingcity_patrolcompany.lua`, `lc_patrol_company` 1):

- **A loiterer:** standing about out of sight 500 to 1600 units off, near where the patrol is going. A notice says so and it is marked on the unit's screen. Seen from within 350 units, half of them bolt and half stay with an excuse.
- **A tail:** walks after the unit, 280 to 480 units behind. After 25 seconds: "You have the feeling of being followed." Looked at for a moment (within 700 units), or walked up to (200), they run.
- **Running:** get within 110 units and they give up (3 experience); 2200 units away or 45 seconds on, they are lost.
- **Caught, or stayed:** a citizen to stop, search or ask for papers. They carry something 85% of the time, and their papers are out of order 60% of the time (forged 20%). Searching them or seeing their papers is 2 more experience, and they go on their way. One lasts five minutes at most.

## Fighters that fight (11 November)

`sv_livingcity_combat.lua`, `lc_combat_ai` (1; 0 is as before). For Civil Protection, Overwatch and Resistance NPCs:

- **Stopping to fight.** One being walked somewhere by the city who can see an enemy within 2200 units, with a gun in hand, is no longer marched on: it fights, for up to a minute a journey, then goes on. (Being given the next step of its way again and again is why they fired a round or two at each other and walked past; against a player they had been left alone to fight.) One with a stick still closes in.
- **A line of fire.** One that knows of an enemy within 3000 units but can't see them goes for a line of fire or closes in, every four seconds, instead of standing facing a wall.
- **Cover.** Hurt by an enemy, it takes cover from them three times in ten (seven in ten below half health), at most once in seven seconds.
- **Better shots.** Each is given very good weapon handling once, may duck, and fires on the move.

## Flashbangs that hold (11 November)

Blinded NPCs now have their weapons taken out of their hands for as long as they are blind, and given back (the one they held, in hand) when it wears off. Before, only what they were allowed to do and whom they remembered was cleared, which the tests accepted but the real game did not: they saw their target again a moment later and fired. Anyone within 300 units of the bang is caught whatever is in the way (it was 150); further off, up to 560, they need a line of sight to it. Whoever threw it gets a chat line: "[Flashbang] 3 blinded: they can't shoot until it wears off." or "[Flashbang] Nobody was caught in it."

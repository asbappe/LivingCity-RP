# The editor: placing things, path mapping

### Placing things

**The editor's panel** lists everything you can place in sections that fold away (points, fixtures, street furniture, workplaces, paths and ladders, zones, doors, tools), each line with how many are on this map; the section headings show their totals. Click a line to use it. **Find** marks every one of that thing on your screen, through walls, numbered, with how far away it is (handy for "where did I put the other dealer?"); **Go** takes you to the next one (in noclip). At the top: the layout at a glance (points, fixtures, zones, tagged doors, path nodes, city NPCs about) and what the city would like that the map hasn't got yet.

**Path mapping** (editor panel, "Paths and doors": *Path mapping*, or `lc_pathmap` / `!pathmap`; superadmins and the listen-server host only): turn it on and **walk the places NPCs should be able to go**. A path node is laid every `lc_pathmap_spacing` units (130) of walking, linked to the one before it (only if a person could really walk between them) and to any other node within about 230 units you could walk to, so loops close by themselves; walking over an existing node joins it instead of stacking another. A green line at the top of the screen says it's on. It pauses in **noclip**, in the air, on a ladder or in a vehicle, and a jump of more than 400 units (a teleport) starts a fresh chain rather than drawing a link across it. Everything is saved to the map's layout file a few seconds after you stop (and the moment you turn it off), shows in the editor overlay, and can still be deleted or linked by hand. `lc_pathmap_undo` removes what this session added. These are the same path nodes NPC routing already uses first, so a patch you've walked is a patch pursuing units can path through.

- **Points:** loiter, sit, bench, lean, ration line slots (front first; the person at the front walks up to the line's dispenser for their ration, then steps away), patrol beats (in order), checkpoints, **stalker terminals** (a Combine zone's stalker works the map's terminal here: see Zones), spawn/exit doorways (citizens), prison cells, release spot, **loading docks** (supply trucks and crates for the kitchen and factory shifts), **graffiti walls** (sanitation scrubs them), **rubble piles** (reclamation crews and citizens' work orders clear them), **leaky pipes** (citizens' work orders patch them), **broadcast studio** (where the Administrator speaks, filmed live, when no player is Director of Propaganda), **train platform** (where trains stop), **arrival** (where new people step into the city before Civil Intake; place it from the way they should face), **infestation spawns** (zombies and infested players) and **resistance spawns** (hunted players). Place the three kinds of spawn far apart so citizens don't walk into the infestation. Citizens only ever appear at their spawn doorways (or, on a map with none, at clean spots), never on Combine metal or glass, in a Combine-only zone, or near an infestation spawn.
- **Fixtures:** water vending machine, ration dispenser, commissary terminal, **Citizen Information desk** (a clerk; place behind a counter: `city-people.md`), **Civil notice poster** (on a wall, facing you; its words fit what's nearest: `talking-and-tips.md`), **bed**, **toilet** and **bathtub** (for maps without enough of their own: the map's own beds, mattresses, toilets, tubs and showers all work too; see Toilets and Sleep below), **Civil Intake** and **Housing Allocation** (new arrivals register and ask for a room; see Civil Intake above), stove, burning barrel, black market dealer, **Combine console** (put one in a cell: see Flying a scanner below), **Resistance lab** (a lab bench and a scientist who makes the Resistance's guns: one in every rebel base; see The Resistance lab below), **Civil Protection Commissary** (units are issued their supplies there; see Civil Protection's tools), **Union reclamation point** (hand in scrap for credits), **incinerator** (where the dead are hauled; see Mortician under Union jobs; nothing ever spawns one by itself), **transformer** and **Combine forcefield** (the power grid, below), **Combine power bank**, **Combine turret** and **Combine barricades** (low, medium, tall: checkpoints, below). **Bin points** and **salvage piles** are points, not props: they make a spot searchable, or salvageable with a crowbar, on maps short of bins and junk. They appear in the world and come back with the layout. Place them at the foot of a wall and they'll face you. **Move one with the physgun** (build mode) and it's saved where you drop it.
- **Persistent props:** build mode, spawn any prop, put it where you want it, and zap it with **Prop: make persistent** (left click). It's frozen, saved in the layout with everything else, and there every time the map loads: model, position, angle, skin, colour, material, scale and bodygroups. Left click it again to save it as it is now (after recolouring it, say); move it with the physgun and it's saved where you drop it; right click and it's no longer persistent (it stays until the map changes); the remover tool takes it out of the layout as well. One that gets broken or blown up comes back a minute later, out of sight. The editor labels them PERSISTENT PROP, and the layout browser lists them. City screens are the Combine's own wall monitors (the big one is the giant Breencast screen); TVs are an apartment's little set. Floor-standing ones sit on the ground. The ration dispenser is wall-mounted at chest height, and a **ration dispenser placed at the front of a ration line** serves that line. Vending machines drop the can out of their slot near the floor.
- **Workplaces:** the editor's "Workplaces" section has a line for each Union job (Sanitation depot, Ration plant, Clinic, Factory, Post office, Maintenance yard, Train station, Civil records office, Nexus laundry, City morgue, Reclamation yard, Radio repair shop). Put one or a few where that work is done and its shifts happen there. Workplaces placed the old way ("workplace" with a job number) still count.
- Any of these spots the map hasn't got, the shifts that use them find a street instead, as before.
- **Overwatch landing zones:** "Point: Overwatch landing zone" is where Overwatch drops reinforcements when the city is in unrest (otherwise a street). Put it under open sky: a dropship comes in over the rooftops and its soldiers jump down out of its container there.
- **Rebel lookout posts:** "Point: rebel lookout post" keeps an armed Resistance fighter on guard there (see Resistance lookouts below). Place it at the way into rebel ground, from the side the Combine would come.
- **Bunks:** "Point: bunk" is a bed a rebel sleeps on now and then (see Rebels at home below). Aim at the middle of the bed from its foot or its head, so it lies along the bed. You only need one for a bed the game can't tell is a bed: mattresses, beds, couches, chairs, benches, tables, workbenches and crates inside rebel territory (your persistent props, the zone's dressing, and the map's own) are found by themselves. The sit, bench and lean points work for rebels too, inside rebel territory.
- **Loading docks:** "Point: loading dock" is where supply drops come in (and supply trucks for the Union's shifts). Under open sky a dropship brings the supplies; under a roof, an APC.
- **Flying a scanner far from the console:** the city around the scanner stays as awake as around anyone standing there (no NPCs asleep and hopping about), and the camera looks where you look, not where the scanner's body tips when it bumps something.
- **Scanner launch spots:** "Point: scanner launch spot" is where scanners flown from a console go up (the one nearest the console). Until there is one, they go up out in the open air near the console, somewhere with sky above it, never inside the map's geometry. `lc_scanner_range` is measured from wherever it went up.
- **Ladders:** NPCs can't climb, so mark a ladder for them: "Path: ladder end" at its foot and again at its top, both with the same number. Routes then go up and down it, and an NPC who reaches one end on the way to the other is put at the other end (a step and they're there; it looks better than gliding up a ladder and sticking halfway). Usable ladders the map already has are found by themselves where the map allows it. **Ladders are also learned from players:** most maps' ladders are only a ladder texture on a wall, but when anyone climbs one, the spot they got on and the spot they got off are saved as a ladder (the same "Path: ladder end" points, numbered for you, so you can see them and remove one), and NPCs use it from then on (`lc_learn_ladders`). Someone you're escorting who sees you go up or down one goes to its foot (or top) and follows you, and so do the fighters following you. One who falls far behind (a door, a jump they couldn't make) catches up when nobody's looking.
- **Paths:** nodes and links for places the map's own navigation can't handle. Send a **test walker** to see if a route works. Where you haven't drawn any, NPCs route over the map's own AI nodegraph: the trip is planned node to node and walked as short legs (the engine is good at a few hundred units and bad at crossing a multi-level map in one go), places that aren't connected to where someone is standing are never picked, and a place people keep failing to reach is skipped for ten minutes. Someone stuck a step from where they're going is quietly moved on when nobody's looking; someone stuck far from it gives the trip up instead of popping across the map. When the engine can't walk a leg of a planned trip (a locked or CP-only door the nodegraph doesn't know about, a gate, a crowd), the walker tries it again in single node-to-node steps, and if one link still won't walk it's remembered for 15 minutes and the trip is re-planned around it: once, it counts as a long detour; twice, nobody is routed along it. The debug report's NPCs section says how often each happens (the "route repairs" line).
- **Rebel territory** ("Zone: rebel territory") is the Resistance's ground: their fighters live there, and street units don't patrol it or chase anyone into it (the editor's "Street units chase people into rebel territory" setting, `lc_rebel_pursuit`, lets them). It used to say "(no security)", meaning exactly that; no rebel zone has security of its own. Raids and Overwatch still come for it.
- **Zones** are boxes or circles (the "Zone shape" choice in the editor panel). A **box** takes two clicks, one corner of the room or area and then the opposite corner, and stands "Box zone height" tall (192 units) from the floor you clicked; it fits square rooms and streets without the gaps a circle leaves in the corners. A **circle** is one click at the radius on the slider. Every zone has `lc_zone_mercy` (96 units, about eight feet) of leeway at its edge: your housing block and the prison reach that far past the line, so a step into a corner the shape missed isn't breaking curfew or escaping; Combine-only zones start that far inside it, so brushing the edge isn't trespassing. **Rebel ground has no mercy**: its rooms are often small, and eight feet either side of a small room is more than the room. Instead, anyone within that eight feet of it sees REBEL TERRITORY AHEAD, and REBEL TERRITORY once they're in (not while they stand inside a Combine-only zone: on a map where the two are stacked one above the other, the nexus isn't rebel ground; the banner on crossing in doesn't show there either); a unit inside the line is shot at (rebels ignore anyone in build mode, so an admin can build there). Right click cancels a box you've started. Kinds: no-go, rebel territory (no security), housing blocks (numbered), prison area, infestation, and **Combine only** (the nexus, offices, armory, prison). Citizens, NPC or player, stay out: NPCs never wander or route through it, and a player gets `lc_trespass_grace` seconds (10) to leave before they're wanted for trespassing. Units, admins, people in custody and anyone in a convincing uniform may be inside. **Every Combine-only zone is garrisoned:** mostly Overwatch soldiers with a metrocop or two (metrocops on their beat walk the streets and don't wander in), spread through it, some standing post and some walking from one end of it to the other, on top of the city's own numbers (editor panel, Events: the garrison sliders; `lc_garrison`, most per zone, 6; a small zone gets fewer, about one per 15 x 15 metres of floor; `lc_garrison_cops`, out of every ten, 3; `lc_garrison_respawn`, seconds before a lost one is replaced out of sight, 90). **Near the prison they're Nova Prospekt's own:** any soldier posted in a Combine-only zone within `lc_prisonguard_range` (3000 units) of a prison zone or cell wears the prison guard's uniform (some carry shotguns); `lc_prisonguard_range 0` turns that off. **Overwatch soldiers stay in the Combine-only zones** (the owner, 7 November): one with nothing to do walks from spot to spot inside the nearest one or stands post there, instead of walking the streets, and the city's own soldiers (`lc_soldiers`) spawn inside one, out of sight. They come out only when deployed: a job or quest (an escort, a cleanup crew's guards, a story), backup a unit called, a response squad, a raid, a skirmish with the Resistance, a lockdown, or sociostability at its worst (red). With no Combine-only zone placed, soldiers patrol as before. **No citizen NPC goes in** except prisoners (and anyone being taken to a cell or let out) and someone a job or story is about: no walk of theirs may end inside, their Union work in there is left alone, and one found inside is walked straight out (put outside out of sight after 20 seconds if they can't find the way). **Each Combine-only zone keeps its own stalkers** (the owner, 7 November: "the stalker is just the worker/laborer/janitor/maintenance/technician for the combine only zones"): `lc_zone_stalkers` of them (1; editor panel, Events: "Stalkers per Combine-only zone"; 0: none, and the work in there is left undone). They appear out of sight, and one killed is replaced out of sight two minutes later. With nothing to do, a stalker stands at a Combine terminal working it, looping the stalker's own console animation: at a **Point: stalker terminal** in its zone (stand where the stalker should stand, facing the map's terminal, and aim at the floor at your feet; the map's terminals are usually part of the map, which the city can't see, hence the point), else in front of a Combine console prop in the zone (combine_interface, combine_intmonitor, combine_smallmonitor and the like), else wherever it is. Which animation that is depends on the stalker model installed: a few likely names, then the model's own list searched for one that sounds like console work; the one used is logged and shown in the debug report (People). When there's work in its zone it goes and does it, then back to its terminal: litter or a dead headcrab picked up and carried off out of sight, a body dragged off out of sight (gone, once nobody can see), a mess or graffiti scrubbed off, a **broken streetlight or loudspeaker repaired** (after the same wait for a player as outside, `lc_npc_work_grace`).
- **Doors:** CP only, **prison door**, always locked, always open, NPCs ignore, force-treat-as-door, plus a tester and inspector. **Doors beside a prison cell or release spot, or inside a prison zone, are prison doors by themselves; doors inside a Combine-only zone are CP only by themselves.** Both are physically locked, opened only by a unit's E (for a few seconds), a keycard, a bypass charge, an escorted prisoner being walked through, or a Union worker's Civil Protection escort. The difference: a **CP-only** door's lock goes dead when its district loses power, but a **prison door stays locked through any blackout**, and if anything else opens it (a button on the map, a trigger, the map's own logic) it shuts and locks itself again at once. A unit pressing one of the map's buttons gets ten seconds to open the cells that way. A prisoner can't open any door or press any button, admin or not. Prisoners keep their fists. A tag of your own overrides all that: if a cell door isn't locking, tag it **Door: prison or cell**. **Any tagged door counts as a door, whatever its shape** (a wide Combine shutter, a brush that slides; sliding `func_movelinear` doors can be tagged too), and the map's navigation is joined through it: a map's nodegraph is built with its doors shut, so the nexus behind a Combine door is often cut off from the rest of the city. A Civil Protection or prison door joins the two sides for **units only**, so cops and soldiers walk through it (opening it as they get there) and citizens don't try; an always-open or force-tagged door joins them for everyone. An **always open** door stays open: the door's own timer that would swing it shut is turned off, it's locked in the open position (E does nothing), and if anything shuts it anyway (the map's own logic) it opens again a moment later.

**Changes from the 7 November playtest.** Every jogging route stop gets a path node under it (or reuses one within 48 units), linked to the next stop and, if not yet joined, to the nearest walkable node within 450 units: when a stop is placed and once when the layout loads (saved once; the file format is unchanged).

**Point: prison work spot** (new, 10 November): where a prisoner's work detail picks up trash (`docs/features/city-events.md`, "The work detail"). Optional: without one, the prison zone, else the corridor between the cells.

**Point: speech stand** (new, 10 November): where the City Administrator gives her speeches (F2 > Me > The city's levers > Go to the speech stand takes her there, facing the way the point faces). Optional.

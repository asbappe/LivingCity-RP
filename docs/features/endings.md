# Secret endings

Joke "end of the city" events, mostly about the motherlode (the master data core). **Nobody really dies and nothing is lost:** characters, Civil Standing and credits carry on (the usual inheritance caveat applies), nobody can be hurt while one runs, and afterwards the map reloads with its Living City layout. (With a dream point placed on the map, they are shared dreams for everyone asleep instead, and the map doesn't reload: see Shared dreams below.)

**Set aside for now (the owner, 7 November: "Can we set aside these Gman interactions (visiting in sleep, talking to administrator, and sealed directives) for a future endings config/setup?").** `lc_directives` is 0 by default (moved from 1 on a server still at 1): no sealed directives, no G-Man in anyone's sleep, no glimpses of him, no talks with the Administrator. Everything below is kept as it was, for a future endings setup; `lc_directives 1` brings it all back.

**How they start: sealed directives** (the owner, 2 November; the design is ENDING_TRIGGERS_DRAFT.md). Every ending is a sealed directive on the City Administrator's device (right click: the office, **Sealed directives**): an envelope with a wax seal, a rhyming riddle, and how warm it is (cold, warm, hot). One unlocks when the Administrator runs the city the strange way its riddle hints at (the office's events, rations, prices, bonuses, commendations, censures, pardons, and its new **Orders**: shut down the grid, restore the power, stand down the morticians, ban weedkiller, a Day of Cleansing, permit Father Grigori's flock to worship, an amnesty that opens every cell), or when the city does it by itself (a bloodbath, a struggling server, a perfect morning drill, sociostability red for 45 minutes, a long blackout, six people dancing, fifteen fires, forty deaths in ten minutes or the infestation everywhere, the weeds at their maximum). Then it's decided by, in order: an Administrator playing now (not AFK), within `lc_directive_sign_minutes` (20), Sign it or Refuse it (a bar at the top of their screen says one's waiting, how long is left and how to open it; with `lc_directive_last_call_minutes`, 5, left, the G-Man appears with the envelope beside him, "Come back. Come back here, now." (`lc_gman_line_last_call`), and the bar turns red; closing the envelopes without answering brings him once a directive: "Get back here..." (`lc_gman_line_walk_away`)); a superadmin online (a popup: start it now, put it to the G-Man's vote, let it pass; `lc_directive_staff_minutes`, 5); an admin online (the same); nobody, the G-Man's vote. When the last of them leaves, `lc_directive_grace_minutes` (10) before it goes down the list, in case they're rejoining. **Refused**, the G-Man appears to the Administrator over their screen ("Not for long"; later, harsher, "We'll see about that"), `lc_gman_rumour_share` (60) percent of players start dreaming that the Administrator is keeping something from them, and he forces their hand: unmarked crates about the city (`lc_gman_crates`, 6: an SMG or a shotgun, ammunition, a powered vest and the cells to charge it full), the infestation spreading, then the grid failing and the infected in the streets. Two minutes after each refusal he also visits a rival: someone who could run for the office (Half-Life: Alyx's "A previous hire has been unable, or unwilling..."), who then has `lc_gman_rival_minutes` (30) to call a **recall vote** (F1, or C > Me: "Call a vote to remove the City Administrator", or /recall; a box at the side of their screen says so in plain words, with the time left, until they call it or the time runs out): Civil Protection and the Administration vote (not citizens); more yes than no and the Administrator is out and the rival is in. Each player, the first time they look at one of his crates, hears "The right man in the wrong place can make all the difference in the world." A new Administrator (elected, or by recall) gets a visit: "I've received some interesting offers for your services... These are extraordinary times." As a directive warms (and while he's forcing someone's hand), players now and then **glimpse him** a way off about the city, gone when they look straight at him or get close (`lc_gman_glimpses` 1, at most every `lc_gman_glimpse_minutes`, 8). Every clip, its file and when it plays: GMAN_SOUNDS.md. He comes back with the same directive after `lc_gman_return_minutes` (30, then 10, then 7); a fourth refusal goes to everyone's vote. Signed, the ending starts and his hand comes off the city. Asleep, `lc_gman_dream_chance` (30) percent of the time, the G-Man appears in your dream reciting the warmest riddle. A dream comes on slowly (the owner, 7 November): dark first, then he fades in over about three seconds (drawn solid, lit up out of the black: drawn see-through, the inside of his mouth showed through his lips as a black square), his words follow, and the whole dream lasts 26 seconds, long enough to read the riddle (his other visits last 12 seconds, a summons 14). As he appears you hear him: a recording from `garrysmod/sound/livingcity/gman/` whose name starts with `dream` (`dream1.wav`, `dream_listen.mp3`; one at random, sent to players like the announcements), or with none there the sound in `lc_gman_line_dream` (his Episode One inhale, `vo/episode_1/npc/gman/gman_inhale.wav`; empty for silence). He's drawn the way Half-Life 2's intro draws him: in his own dark space over your screen, whatever you're doing. A vote that says no: "Not this time." His three Episode One lines are only in the pre-release Episode One files, which the server may not have: each is a setting (`lc_gman_line_refused`, `lc_gman_line_refused_again`, `lc_gman_line_vote_no`); put the file under `garrysmod/sound/` (and sync FastDL) and point the setting at it, or at any other line. A missing one is skipped (the words still show on screen); one that's there is sent to players when they join. His vote line is `vo/citadel/gman_exit02.wav` only now (`exit01` says "Doctor Freeman"; a server still on both moves once). `lc_directives 0` turns it all off (admins can still start an ending with `lc_ending`).

**Room for everyone.** The endings that gather everyone into rings at the landing zone (Harpies, Bloom, Marrow, Hard) give each person their own spot: when a ring is full, the rest stand on the next ring out, so a city with a hundred NPCs or more doesn't end up standing inside itself (the owner's Marrow, 1 November: units piled into one small ring, and the engine pushed some through the floor). The city's people are also never spawned on top of someone else, and any NPC that falls out of the map is put back where it last stood within two seconds (or taken away if there's nowhere to put it).

**How every one goes.** Something triggers it. A few seconds later (`lc_ending_pause`, 4) **the G-Man stops time**: everyone freezes, every NPC stops, the colour drains out of the world, the G-Man is standing right in front of each person (on their own screen, whichever way they were looking: one each, only they see theirs; the view turns to him and stays on him for the vote; he idles, straightens his tie now and then, and his mouth moves as he speaks), the city's announcements stop (and stay quiet until it's over), his end-of-Half-Life-2 line plays for everyone (`lc_ending_gman_lines`, the game's own `vo/citadel/gman_exit02.wav` by default), and a vote comes up on every screen: **1** to let it happen, **2** to skip it (`lc_ending_vote_secs`, 30). More skips than yeses and time starts again; anything else, **including nobody voting at all**, is the apocalypse. It plays out with its song until everyone has been "taken" (or `lc_ending_max`, 180 seconds), the screen goes white, and the map reloads. One ending, or one skipped, every `lc_ending_cooldown` minutes (180: three hours), remembered across the reload. The city's events wait while one is on.

**Photosensitivity.** The endings with strobes or screen flashes (`bus`, `bloom`, `marrow`, `base`, `hard`, `water`, `plane`) warn on the G-Man's vote screen and again with a popup as they start: Reduce flashing, or I'm fine (the default: doing nothing for 15 seconds keeps it). `lc_ending_calm 1` (each player's own, remembered) turns the strobes into a steady glow and leaves out the screen flashes, the lightning and the static; the music, the dancing and everything else stay.

**Shared dreams (with a dream point placed)** (the owner, 7 November: "take the endings and turn them into 'dreams' that all sleeping players can experience together at a set time at night"; `sv_livingcity_dreams.lua`). **With no dream point on the map, nothing here applies and the endings are exactly as described on this page.** Once the owner places one (the Living City Editor, **Point: dream**, somewhere out of the map nobody can walk to; optionally **Zone: dream** round it, as its edge), the City Administrator can choose a dream for tonight (below). **Endings set off in play** (Father Grigori's `/eatchip`, an admin's `lc_ending`) **are the whole city's, as always**: dreams are a bonus, chosen, never set off (the owner, 7 November). One dream waits at a time, and one dream a night at most. About `lc_dream_delay` (60) seconds after the Administrator says good night (the `night` announcement; if no recording plays, from the night coming), **everyone asleep in a bed who hasn't opted out** dreams it together: they are warned about 30 seconds before (with the way to opt out), and taken out of their beds to the dream point, where the ending plays for them alone (no G-Man and no vote: he's kept for the endings). **Everyone awake is not touched at all:** no time stop, no freeze, no vote, no G-Man, no song, no lights out, no storm, no doors locked open, the city's events and announcer carry on, and there is no map reload afterwards. Inside a dream, the ending's people, props, rings, bombs and flying all stay inside the dream zone (or within `lc_dream_radius`, 1500, of the point), the ending brings its own Civil Protection units (`lc_dream_combine`, 6) instead of the city's, its own citizens instead of the city's people, and its own Father Grigori; its NPCs are held still one by one (the way `ai_disabled` holds the whole city in an ending). Dreamers who wander off its edge are put back in the middle. When it ends, everything it made is removed (whatever appeared inside the dream while it ran), and everyone wakes. **Everything comes back:** before anyone is moved, everything about them is written down, in memory and on disk (`data/livingcity/dreams/<steamid64>.json`): where they lay and their bed, health, armour, pockets, every weapon with the rounds in it and the ammunition carried, survival (hunger, thirst, tiredness and the rest), credits, standing, XP, rank, faction, job and shift, clothes, model, skin and bodygroups, everything saved for them and everything shown about them. In the dream they carry only their hands, and nothing counts: no picking up, dropping, buying or selling (the menus, chat commands and console commands wait till they wake, except the vote and waking up), no credits or standing gained or lost, no crimes, nobody really hurt and no character dies. On waking it is all put back, then checked part by part twice, putting back again anything that differs; they are back in their bed, still asleep (the dream counts as sleep; Jump gets them up), and the file is deleted only once everything matches. If something still differs, the file is kept and admins are told. **A crash, a disconnect or a map change in the middle of a dream:** the next time they join, everything is put back from the file (in their bed's spot, on the same map). A snapshot already put back once is never put back again by itself (newer things aren't undone); an admin can. **Opting out:** C > Settings > Shared dreams (on, or opt out; saved with the player), or `/dream off` and `/dream on`; in a dream, C > Settings > Shared dreams: wake up (or `/dream wake`) wakes them at once with everything back. **Admins:** C > Help > Dreams (also the Dreams button in the Endings menu): whether dreams can happen, what waits for tonight and when, dream one now (with the admin in it too, awake), wake everyone, forget tonight's, and **Put back** anyone's things from their snapshot; `lc_dream_restore <name>` and `lc_dream_now [ending] [me]` do the same in the console. The admin's own `lc_ending` (and the Endings menu's Play it now) stays the whole city's, as before, dream point or not. `lc_dreams 0` switches dreams off (the endings are as before); `lc_dream_every_night 1` dreams a random ending on a night with nothing waiting (when the cooldown allows). **What a dream can't do the way the city does:** the lights going out and the storm are the whole map's, so a dream leaves them out (the client's own colours, strobes and darkness still show for the dreamers); the flying endings (Bus, Plane, Base, Fire) fly inside the dream's edge, so they want a tall, open dream area (a dream zone box sets how high they may go); the Flood's water fills the dream zone only (its box, or the square round the dream point), drawn and swum the gamemode's own way; a map's own rising water (a `func_water_analog` named `lc_flood`) sits where the mapper built it in the city, so a dream never raises it (the owner, 7 November: "we want the flood water to be used but restricted to the dream zone").

**The Administrator chooses tonight's dream** (the owner, 7 November: "the administrator can choose a good, happy dream if everyone has been obedient and compliant, but can choose a nightmare ... labelled as 'Happy' 'Neutral' or 'Nightmare'"). Every ending has a mood, set by `lc_dream_moods` (`id=happy|neutral|nightmare`, comma-separated; not listed is Neutral). By default: Happy: The Bus, Plane, Hard, Shalom; Neutral: Bloom, Fire, Base; Nightmare: They Return (the mingebags), Marrow, The Flood, Standup. On the Administrator's tablet (right click: the office), **Tonight's dream** shows what waits and when (or why the city can't dream: no dream point, dreams off), and lists the endings by their titles under Happy (a reward), Neutral and Nightmare (a punishment), each one a pick, and **No dream tonight**. Only the City Administrator can pick. The pick waits for tonight's dream (a minute after the good night) and can be changed until it begins. The G-Man has no part in dreams. The Administrator's pick ignores the endings' cooldown and doesn't start it over (so it never holds an ending back), but there is still one dream a night: picked after tonight's was dreamt, it waits for tomorrow night. The sleepers feel the mood in the warning before it (a warm line for a happy dream, a strange one for a neutral one, a cold, uneasy one for a nightmare), and the next morning the whole city reads in chat how it slept. A random dream from `lc_dream_every_night` gives way to the Administrator's pick.

**Out of it in a dream.** A dreamer the ending takes out (drowned in the flood, caught by the mingebags, gone up in sparkles, taken off the bus) doesn't go dark: they watch the rest of the dream. Clicks cycle through the other dreamers still in it (never anyone awake, never the city), and Jump flies free, but only inside the dream zone's edge and under its top (without a zone, within `lc_dream_radius` of the point). When the dream ends they wake in their bed with everything back, like everyone else.

**Opting out, and nightmares** (the owner, 7 November, part 3: "they can opt out if it's a neutral or positive dream, not out of a nightmare, and they have to play a nightmare unless they take a stimulant to keep themselves awake"). Opting out (C > Settings > Shared dreams, /dream off) covers Happy and Neutral dreams only. A Nightmare (an ending or a dream you play) takes everyone asleep outside a Combine-only zone, opted out or not, and its warning says there is no opting out (the owner, 8 November: anyone asleep inside a Combine-only zone is spared, and so is anyone Unbroken from beating the last one). Nobody wakes themselves from a Nightmare while still in it ("Wake up" says so); once out of it (out of lives in a dream you play, or taken out of an ending) they may. A stimulant shot keeps you from sleeping for 15 minutes, so a Nightmare cannot have you. Admins can still wake everyone, and anyone's things can still be put back.

**Nightmare dreams you play** (the owner, 7 November: nightmare dreams such as a maze where giant metro cops chase everyone, a climb with explosive barrels that explode if touched, "and any other similar ideas"; `sv_livingcity_dreamgames.lua`, `cl_livingcity_dreamgames.lua`). Four more dreams on the Administrator's tablet under Nightmare, next to the endings (their mood can be changed in `lc_dream_moods` like any ending: `maze`, `barrels`, `crumble`, `freeze`). Each builds its own arena at the dream point from plain props (Garry's Mod's building plates, Half-Life 2's officers, soldier, scanner and barrels), all of it removed when the dream ends; nothing of the map is used. Everything else is as for any dream: sleepers only, everything written down first, nothing counts, nobody really hurt, and they wake in their bed with everything they had. The screen shows the dream's name, what to do, lives and the time left. **The Patrol Maze** (5 minutes, 3 lives): a maze (its size fits the dream zone) with a glowing token in every corridor; two to four giant Civil Protection officers (1.7 times life size, taller than the walls) hunt the dreamers along the corridors; caught, a life goes and you're back at the start; the four golden tokens in the corners make them run for 8 seconds, and touching one then sends him back to the middle for 5; every token picked up and everyone still in it wins. **The Barrel Tower** (4 minutes, 3 lives): ramps side by side climbing up (2 to 5, as the headroom allows) to a red platform where a giant Overwatch soldier rolls explosive barrels down, more often as it goes on; touch one and it blows (anyone close by is caught too): a life gone, back to the bottom; jump over them; reach the top and win. **The Crumbling Floor** (3 minutes, 1 life): a floor of tiles (raised over a drop where there's room); a tile you stand on cracks (yellow, then red) and falls a second later, and a scanner overhead marks more, faster as it goes on; fall and you wake; the last one standing wins (alone: last 90 seconds). **Freeze, Citizen** (3 minutes, 3 strikes): cross a yard to a giant officer (3.2 times life size); move only while his back is turned; when he turns and shouts, anyone still moving after a moment's grace is seen and sent back; reach the red line by him and win. Out of lives (fallen, in The Crumbling Floor), or won: a window asks whether to wake now or watch the rest (flying about inside the arena, never past its edge) until it is over; watchers still see the time and the tokens; with nobody left in it, it ends and everyone wakes. When the time's up, whoever is still in it wakes as the nightmare fades. Waking from one (the owner, 8 November): won, Unbroken and a second wind for a day (plus a Vortigaunt's whisper for a citizen, a Vortigaunt's gift for the Resistance; never credits, standing or experience); still in it at the end, nothing either way; lost every life, 30 off your health until your next full night's sleep; see survival-and-credit.md. Going over a wall or off the edge puts you back at the start. The random dream (`lc_dream_every_night`) still picks only from the endings, and the endings' cooldown doesn't apply to these.

**The Endings menu (superadmins): tune them live.** C > Help > Endings menu, the button at the top of the Living City Editor tool, or `lc_endings_menu`. Pick an ending from the list (the one playing is picked for you), press **Play it now**, and tune it while it plays. **The dance** tab: pick one of the ending's moves (Bloom's dance; Marrow's club and rave dances; sat on the Bus or in Base's ZIGs; party hard; the hora, rushing in and up on the chair; numa numa; the crowd at the rope, on the rope and limp; Father Grigori headbanging and dancing), click a bone on the body map, and turn it with three sliders (each says what it does to that bone: on an arm, pitch takes it out to the side and yaw raises it forward). The bone lights up on everyone doing that move (yellow, with its axes: red forward, green right, blue up; its other side in blue), and a close-up at the top follows them (drag it to turn round, wheel to zoom; "watch someone else" changes who). Left and right mirror each other unless you untick it. No ending playing? Tick the **stand-in**: someone does the move in front of you, to a beat you set, only on your screen. A turn is added to what the dance already does, so it keeps moving; "back to how the code has it" undoes one bone or the whole move. **Settings**: every one of the ending's settings (and every ending's own: cooldown, vote, pause...) as sliders and boxes, each with what it does and its default a click away, and the song's time as it plays for lining up the sections. **Just for you**: your own volume, calm mode and Base's arcade blocks. **Changes**: everything that's been changed, to copy and send on so it can be built in as the new normal (a setting only counts as changed when its value really is different: 0.360000 is 0.36). Once some tuning has been built into the code, the saved copy of it is dropped by itself (Bloom's hands, 20 October), so it's never turned twice. **The Combine's units** have bones that sit differently from people's (their own models, and a Combine pack's): a move can be tuned for them on their own ("The dance, on the Combine's units only": a metrocop stands in), added on top of the move's own tuning. The dances' tuning and the few settings each player's game keeps for itself (Bloom's palms, the headbang, the kippah, the chair) are kept on the server in `data/livingcity/ending_tuning.json` and sent to every player, so everyone sees the same dance; the endings' other settings are saved like any other setting.

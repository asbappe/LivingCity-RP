# Secret endings

Joke "end of the city" events, mostly about the motherlode (the master data core). **Nobody really dies and nothing is lost:** characters, Civil Standing and credits carry on (the usual inheritance caveat applies), nobody can be hurt while one runs, and afterwards the map reloads with its Living City layout.

**How they start: sealed directives** (the owner, 2 November; the design is ENDING_TRIGGERS_DRAFT.md). Every ending is a sealed directive on the City Administrator's device (right click: the office, **Sealed directives**): an envelope with a wax seal, a rhyming riddle, and how warm it is (cold, warm, hot). One unlocks when the Administrator runs the city the strange way its riddle hints at (the office's events, rations, prices, bonuses, commendations, censures, pardons, and its new **Orders**: shut down the grid, restore the power, stand down the morticians, ban weedkiller, a Day of Cleansing, permit Father Grigori's flock to worship, an amnesty that opens every cell), or when the city does it by itself (a bloodbath, a struggling server, a perfect morning drill, sociostability red for 45 minutes, a long blackout, six people dancing, fifteen fires, forty deaths in ten minutes or the infestation everywhere, the weeds at their maximum). Then it's decided by, in order: an Administrator playing now (not AFK), within `lc_directive_sign_minutes` (20), Sign it or Refuse it (a bar at the top of their screen says one's waiting, how long is left and how to open it; with `lc_directive_last_call_minutes`, 5, left, the G-Man appears with the envelope beside him, "Come back. Come back here, now." (`lc_gman_line_last_call`), and the bar turns red; closing the envelopes without answering brings him once a directive: "Get back here..." (`lc_gman_line_walk_away`)); a superadmin online (a popup: start it now, put it to the G-Man's vote, let it pass; `lc_directive_staff_minutes`, 5); an admin online (the same); nobody, the G-Man's vote. When the last of them leaves, `lc_directive_grace_minutes` (10) before it goes down the list, in case they're rejoining. **Refused**, the G-Man appears to the Administrator over their screen ("Not for long"; later, harsher, "We'll see about that"), `lc_gman_rumour_share` (60) percent of players start dreaming that the Administrator is keeping something from them, and he forces their hand: unmarked crates about the city (`lc_gman_crates`, 6: an SMG or a shotgun, ammunition, a powered vest and the cells to charge it full), the infestation spreading, then the grid failing and the infected in the streets. Two minutes after each refusal he also visits a rival: someone who could run for the office (Half-Life: Alyx's "A previous hire has been unable, or unwilling..."), who then has `lc_gman_rival_minutes` (30) to call a **recall vote** (F1, or C > Me: "Call a vote to remove the City Administrator", or /recall; a box at the side of their screen says so in plain words, with the time left, until they call it or the time runs out): Civil Protection and the Administration vote (not citizens); more yes than no and the Administrator is out and the rival is in. Each player, the first time they look at one of his crates, hears "The right man in the wrong place can make all the difference in the world." A new Administrator (elected, or by recall) gets a visit: "I've received some interesting offers for your services... These are extraordinary times." As a directive warms (and while he's forcing someone's hand), players now and then **glimpse him** a way off about the city, gone when they look straight at him or get close (`lc_gman_glimpses` 1, at most every `lc_gman_glimpse_minutes`, 8). Every clip, its file and when it plays: GMAN_SOUNDS.md. He comes back with the same directive after `lc_gman_return_minutes` (30, then 10, then 7); a fourth refusal goes to everyone's vote. Signed, the ending starts and his hand comes off the city. Asleep, `lc_gman_dream_chance` (30) percent of the time, the G-Man appears in your dream reciting the warmest riddle. He's drawn the way Half-Life 2's intro draws him: in his own dark space over your screen, whatever you're doing. A vote that says no: "Not this time." His three Episode One lines are only in the pre-release Episode One files, which the server may not have: each is a setting (`lc_gman_line_refused`, `lc_gman_line_refused_again`, `lc_gman_line_vote_no`); put the file under `garrysmod/sound/` (and sync FastDL) and point the setting at it, or at any other line. A missing one is skipped (the words still show on screen); one that's there is sent to players when they join. His vote line is `vo/citadel/gman_exit02.wav` only now (`exit01` says "Doctor Freeman"; a server still on both moves once). `lc_directives 0` turns it all off (admins can still start an ending with `lc_ending`).

**Room for everyone.** The endings that gather everyone into rings at the landing zone (Harpies, Bloom, Marrow, Hard) give each person their own spot: when a ring is full, the rest stand on the next ring out, so a city with a hundred NPCs or more doesn't end up standing inside itself (the owner's Marrow, 1 November: units piled into one small ring, and the engine pushed some through the floor). The city's people are also never spawned on top of someone else, and any NPC that falls out of the map is put back where it last stood within two seconds (or taken away if there's nowhere to put it).

**How every one goes.** Something triggers it. A few seconds later (`lc_ending_pause`, 4) **the G-Man stops time**: everyone freezes, every NPC stops, the colour drains out of the world, the G-Man is standing right in front of each person (on their own screen, whichever way they were looking: one each, only they see theirs; the view turns to him and stays on him for the vote; he idles, straightens his tie now and then, and his mouth moves as he speaks), the city's announcements stop (and stay quiet until it's over), his end-of-Half-Life-2 line plays for everyone (`lc_ending_gman_lines`, the game's own `vo/citadel/gman_exit02.wav` by default), and a vote comes up on every screen: **1** to let it happen, **2** to skip it (`lc_ending_vote_secs`, 30). More skips than yeses and time starts again; anything else, **including nobody voting at all**, is the apocalypse. It plays out with its song until everyone has been "taken" (or `lc_ending_max`, 180 seconds), the screen goes white, and the map reloads. One ending, or one skipped, every `lc_ending_cooldown` minutes (180: three hours), remembered across the reload. The city's events wait while one is on.

**Photosensitivity.** The endings with strobes or screen flashes (`bus`, `bloom`, `marrow`, `base`, `hard`, `water`, `plane`) warn on the G-Man's vote screen and again with a popup as they start: Reduce flashing, or I'm fine (the default: doing nothing for 15 seconds keeps it). `lc_ending_calm 1` (each player's own, remembered) turns the strobes into a steady glow and leaves out the screen flashes, the lightning and the static; the music, the dancing and everything else stay.

Songs go straight in the server's **`garrysmod/sound/livingcity/endings/`** (the official place), named for the ending's codeword so the name gives nothing away: `harpies.mp3`, `bus.mp3`, `plane.mp3`, `shalom.mp3`, `fire.mp3` and so on (`bus2.mp3` for another; one is picked each time). `.mp3` or `.ogg` (a song as `.wav` is ten times the download, and an MP3 renamed `.wav` is silent). The older layout, a folder per ending, still works. `lc_endings` points out a song whose name matches no ending, and a superadmin gets a "No song" notice when an ending starts without one. After adding one, press the host's FastDL sync, change the map, and have players reconnect, or their games can't download it. Admins: `lc_endings` (what's found, and the cooldown), `lc_ending <ending> [now]` (trigger one; `now` skips the G-Man and the vote), `lc_ending_cancel` (stop it where it stands, no reload), `lc_ending_reset_cooldown`, `lc_endings_on 0` (off). Hooks: `LivingCityEndingTriggered(id, ply)`, `LivingCityEndingVote(id, yes, skip)`, `LivingCityEndingStarted(id)`, `LivingCityEndingTook(ply, id)`, `LivingCityEndingReset(id)`.

**The Endings menu (superadmins): tune them live.** C > Help > Endings menu, the button at the top of the Living City Editor tool, or `lc_endings_menu`. Pick an ending from the list (the one playing is picked for you), press **Play it now**, and tune it while it plays. **The dance** tab: pick one of the ending's moves (Bloom's dance; Marrow's club and rave dances; sat on the Bus or in Base's ZIGs; party hard; the hora, rushing in and up on the chair; numa numa; the crowd at the rope, on the rope and limp; Father Grigori headbanging and dancing), click a bone on the body map, and turn it with three sliders (each says what it does to that bone: on an arm, pitch takes it out to the side and yaw raises it forward). The bone lights up on everyone doing that move (yellow, with its axes: red forward, green right, blue up; its other side in blue), and a close-up at the top follows them (drag it to turn round, wheel to zoom; "watch someone else" changes who). Left and right mirror each other unless you untick it. No ending playing? Tick the **stand-in**: someone does the move in front of you, to a beat you set, only on your screen. A turn is added to what the dance already does, so it keeps moving; "back to how the code has it" undoes one bone or the whole move. **Settings**: every one of the ending's settings (and every ending's own: cooldown, vote, pause...) as sliders and boxes, each with what it does and its default a click away, and the song's time as it plays for lining up the sections. **Just for you**: your own volume, calm mode and Base's arcade blocks. **Changes**: everything that's been changed, to copy and send on so it can be built in as the new normal (a setting only counts as changed when its value really is different: 0.360000 is 0.36). Once some tuning has been built into the code, the saved copy of it is dropped by itself (Bloom's hands, 20 October), so it's never turned twice. **The Combine's units** have bones that sit differently from people's (their own models, and a Combine pack's): a move can be tuned for them on their own ("The dance, on the Combine's units only": a metrocop stands in), added on top of the move's own tuning. The dances' tuning and the few settings each player's game keeps for itself (Bloom's palms, the headbang, the kippah, the chair) are kept on the server in `data/livingcity/ending_tuning.json` and sent to every player, so everyone sees the same dance; the endings' other settings are saved like any other setting.

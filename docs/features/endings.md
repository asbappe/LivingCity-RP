# Secret endings

Joke "end of the city" events, mostly about the motherlode (the master data core). **Nobody really dies and nothing is lost:** characters, Civil Standing and credits carry on (the usual inheritance caveat applies), nobody can be hurt while one runs, and afterwards the map reloads with its Living City layout. (With a dream point placed on the map, they are shared dreams for everyone asleep instead, and the map doesn't reload: see Shared dreams below.)

**Set aside for now (the owner, 7 November: "Can we set aside these Gman interactions (visiting in sleep, talking to administrator, and sealed directives) for a future endings config/setup?").** `lc_directives` is 0 by default (moved from 1 on a server still at 1): no sealed directives, no G-Man in anyone's sleep, no glimpses of him, no talks with the Administrator. Everything below is kept as it was, for a future endings setup; `lc_directives 1` brings it all back.

**How they start: sealed directives** (the owner, 2 November; the design is ENDING_TRIGGERS_DRAFT.md). Every ending is a sealed directive on the City Administrator's device (right click: the office, **Sealed directives**): an envelope with a wax seal, a rhyming riddle, and how warm it is (cold, warm, hot). One unlocks when the Administrator runs the city the strange way its riddle hints at (the office's events, rations, prices, bonuses, commendations, censures, pardons, and its new **Orders**: shut down the grid, restore the power, stand down the morticians, ban weedkiller, a Day of Cleansing, permit Father Grigori's flock to worship, an amnesty that opens every cell), or when the city does it by itself (a bloodbath, a struggling server, a perfect morning drill, sociostability red for 45 minutes, a long blackout, six people dancing, fifteen fires, the master data core decoded by the Resistance's lab scientist in the last half hour (All your base's own way in, built 7 November), forty deaths in ten minutes or the infestation everywhere, the weeds at their maximum). Then it's decided by, in order: an Administrator playing now (not AFK), within `lc_directive_sign_minutes` (20), Sign it or Refuse it (a bar at the top of their screen says one's waiting, how long is left and how to open it; with `lc_directive_last_call_minutes`, 5, left, the G-Man appears with the envelope beside him, "Come back. Come back here, now." (`lc_gman_line_last_call`), and the bar turns red; closing the envelopes without answering brings him once a directive: "Get back here..." (`lc_gman_line_walk_away`)); a superadmin online (a popup: start it now, put it to the G-Man's vote, let it pass; `lc_directive_staff_minutes`, 5); an admin online (the same); nobody, the G-Man's vote. When the last of them leaves, `lc_directive_grace_minutes` (10) before it goes down the list, in case they're rejoining. **Refused**, the G-Man appears to the Administrator over their screen ("Not for long"; later, harsher, "We'll see about that"), `lc_gman_rumour_share` (60) percent of players start dreaming that the Administrator is keeping something from them, and he forces their hand: unmarked crates about the city (`lc_gman_crates`, 6: an SMG or a shotgun, ammunition, a powered vest and the cells to charge it full), the infestation spreading, then the grid failing and the infected in the streets. Two minutes after each refusal he also visits a rival: someone who could run for the office (Half-Life: Alyx's "A previous hire has been unable, or unwilling..."), who then has `lc_gman_rival_minutes` (30) to call a **recall vote** (F1, or C > Me: "Call a vote to remove the City Administrator", or /recall; a box at the side of their screen says so in plain words, with the time left, until they call it or the time runs out): Civil Protection and the Administration vote (not citizens); more yes than no and the Administrator is out and the rival is in. Each player, the first time they look at one of his crates, hears "The right man in the wrong place can make all the difference in the world." A new Administrator (elected, or by recall) gets a visit: "I've received some interesting offers for your services... These are extraordinary times." As a directive warms (and while he's forcing someone's hand), players now and then **glimpse him** a way off about the city, gone when they look straight at him or get close (`lc_gman_glimpses` 1, at most every `lc_gman_glimpse_minutes`, 8). Every clip, its file and when it plays: VOICE_LINES.md. He comes back with the same directive after `lc_gman_return_minutes` (30, then 10, then 7); a fourth refusal goes to everyone's vote. Signed, the ending starts and his hand comes off the city. Asleep, `lc_gman_dream_chance` (30) percent of the time, the G-Man appears in your dream reciting the warmest riddle. A dream comes on slowly (the owner, 7 November): dark first, then he fades in over about three seconds (drawn solid, lit up out of the black: drawn see-through, the inside of his mouth showed through his lips as a black square), his words follow, and the whole dream lasts 26 seconds, long enough to read the riddle (his other visits last 12 seconds, a summons 14). As he appears you hear him: a recording from `garrysmod/sound/livingcity/gman/` whose name starts with `dream` (`dream1.wav`, `dream_listen.mp3`; one at random, sent to players like the announcements), or with none there the sound in `lc_gman_line_dream` (his Episode One inhale, `vo/episode_1/npc/gman/gman_inhale.wav`; empty for silence). He's drawn the way Half-Life 2's intro draws him: in his own dark space over your screen, whatever you're doing. A vote that says no: "Not this time." His three Episode One lines are only in the pre-release Episode One files, which the server may not have: each is a setting (`lc_gman_line_refused`, `lc_gman_line_refused_again`, `lc_gman_line_vote_no`); put the file under `garrysmod/sound/` (and sync FastDL) and point the setting at it, or at any other line. A missing one is skipped (the words still show on screen); one that's there is sent to players when they join. His vote line is `vo/citadel/gman_exit02.wav` only now (`exit01` says "Doctor Freeman"; a server still on both moves once). `lc_directives 0` turns it all off (admins can still start an ending with `lc_ending`).

**Room for everyone.** The endings that gather everyone into rings at the landing zone (Harpies, Bloom, Marrow, Hard) give each person their own spot: when a ring is full, the rest stand on the next ring out, so a city with a hundred NPCs or more doesn't end up standing inside itself (the owner's Marrow, 1 November: units piled into one small ring, and the engine pushed some through the floor). The city's people are also never spawned on top of someone else, and any NPC that falls out of the map is put back where it last stood within two seconds (or taken away if there's nowhere to put it).

**How every one goes.** Something triggers it. A few seconds later (`lc_ending_pause`, 4) **the G-Man stops time**: everyone freezes, every NPC stops, the colour drains out of the world, the G-Man is standing right in front of each person (on their own screen, whichever way they were looking: one each, only they see theirs; the view turns to him and stays on him for the vote; he idles, straightens his tie now and then, and his mouth moves as he speaks), the city's announcements stop (and stay quiet until it's over), his end-of-Half-Life-2 line plays for everyone (`lc_ending_gman_lines`, the game's own `vo/citadel/gman_exit02.wav` by default), and a vote comes up on every screen: **1** to let it happen, **2** to skip it (`lc_ending_vote_secs`, 30). More skips than yeses and time starts again; anything else, **including nobody voting at all**, is the apocalypse. It plays out with its song until everyone has been "taken" (or `lc_ending_max`, 180 seconds), the screen goes white, and the map reloads. One ending, or one skipped, every `lc_ending_cooldown` minutes (180: three hours), remembered across the reload. The city's events wait while one is on.

**Photosensitivity.** The endings with strobes or screen flashes (`bus`, `bloom`, `marrow`, `base`, `hard`, `water`, `plane`) warn on the G-Man's vote screen and again with a popup as they start: Reduce flashing, or I'm fine (the default: doing nothing for 15 seconds keeps it). `lc_ending_calm 1` (each player's own, remembered) turns the strobes into a steady glow and leaves out the screen flashes, the lightning and the static; the music, the dancing and everything else stay.

**Shared dreams (with a dream point placed)** (the owner, 7 November: "take the endings and turn them into 'dreams' that all sleeping players can experience together at a set time at night"; `sv_livingcity_dreams.lua`). **With no dream point on the map, nothing here applies and the endings are exactly as described on this page.** Once the owner places one (the Miscount Editor, **Point: dream**, somewhere out of the map nobody can walk to; optionally **Zone: dream** round it, as its edge), the City Administrator can choose a dream for tonight (below). **Endings set off in play** (Father Grigori's `/eatchip`, an admin's `lc_ending`) **are the whole city's, as always**: dreams are a bonus, chosen, never set off (the owner, 7 November). One dream waits at a time, and one dream a night at most. About `lc_dream_delay` (60) seconds after the Administrator says good night (the `night` announcement; if no recording plays, from the night coming), **everyone asleep in a bed** dreams it together: they are warned about 30 seconds before (with the way to opt out), and taken out of their beds to the dream point, where the ending plays for them alone (no G-Man and no vote: he's kept for the endings). **Everyone awake is not touched at all:** no time stop, no freeze, no vote, no G-Man, no song, no lights out, no storm, no doors locked open, the city's events and announcer carry on, and there is no map reload afterwards. Inside a dream, the ending's people, props, rings, bombs and flying all stay inside the dream zone (or within `lc_dream_radius`, 1500, of the point), the ending brings its own Civil Protection units (`lc_dream_combine`, 6) instead of the city's, its own citizens instead of the city's people, and its own Father Grigori; its NPCs are held still one by one (the way `ai_disabled` holds the whole city in an ending). Dreamers who wander off its edge are put back in the middle. When it ends, everything it made is removed (whatever appeared inside the dream while it ran), and everyone wakes. **Everything comes back:** before anyone is moved, everything about them is written down, in memory and on disk (`data/livingcity/dreams/<steamid64>.json`): where they lay and their bed, health, armour, pockets, every weapon with the rounds in it and the ammunition carried, survival (hunger, thirst, tiredness and the rest), credits, standing, XP, rank, faction, job and shift, clothes, model, skin and bodygroups, everything saved for them and everything shown about them. In the dream they carry only their hands, and nothing counts: no picking up, dropping, buying or selling (the menus, chat commands and console commands wait till they wake, except the vote and waking up), no credits or standing gained or lost, no crimes, nobody really hurt and no character dies. On waking it is all put back, then checked part by part twice, putting back again anything that differs; they are back in their bed, still asleep (the dream counts as sleep; Jump gets them up), and the file is deleted only once everything matches. If something still differs, the file is kept and admins are told. **A crash, a disconnect or a map change in the middle of a dream:** the next time they join, everything is put back from the file (in their bed's spot, on the same map). A snapshot already put back once is never put back again by itself (newer things aren't undone); an admin can. **Opting out:** C > Settings > Shared dreams (on, or opt out; saved with the player), or `/dream off` and `/dream on`; in a dream, C > Settings > Shared dreams: wake up (or `/dream wake`) wakes them at once with everything back. **Admins:** C > Help > Dreams (also the Dreams button in the Endings menu): whether dreams can happen, what waits for tonight and when, dream one now (with the admin in it too, awake), wake everyone, forget tonight's, and **Put back** anyone's things from their snapshot; `lc_dream_restore <name>` and `lc_dream_now [ending] [me]` do the same in the console. The admin's own `lc_ending` (and the Endings menu's Play it now) stays the whole city's, as before, dream point or not. `lc_dreams 0` switches dreams off (the endings are as before); `lc_dream_every_night 1` dreams a random ending on a night with nothing waiting (when the cooldown allows). **What a dream can't do the way the city does:** the lights going out and the storm are the whole map's, so a dream leaves them out (the client's own colours, strobes and darkness still show for the dreamers); the flying endings (Bus, Plane, Base, Fire) fly inside the dream's edge, so they want a tall, open dream area (a dream zone box sets how high they may go); the Flood's water fills the dream zone only (its box, or the square round the dream point), drawn and swum the gamemode's own way; a map's own rising water (a `func_water_analog` named `lc_flood`) sits where the mapper built it in the city, so a dream never raises it (the owner, 7 November: "we want the flood water to be used but restricted to the dream zone").

**The Administrator chooses tonight's dream** (the owner, 7 November: "the administrator can choose a good, happy dream if everyone has been obedient and compliant, but can choose a nightmare ... labelled as 'Happy' 'Neutral' or 'Nightmare'"). Every ending has a mood, set by `lc_dream_moods` (`id=happy|neutral|nightmare`, comma-separated; not listed is Neutral). By default: Happy: The Bus, Plane, Hard, Shalom; Neutral: Bloom, Fire, Base; Nightmare: They Return (the mingebags), Marrow, The Flood, Standup. On the Administrator's tablet (right click: the office), **Tonight's dream** shows what waits and when (or why the city can't dream: no dream point, dreams off), and lists the endings by their titles under Happy (a reward), Neutral and Nightmare (a punishment), each one a pick, and **No dream tonight**. Only the City Administrator can pick. The pick waits for tonight's dream (a minute after the good night) and can be changed until it begins. The G-Man has no part in dreams. The Administrator's pick ignores the endings' cooldown and doesn't start it over (so it never holds an ending back), but there is still one dream a night: picked after tonight's was dreamt, it waits for tomorrow night. The sleepers feel the mood in the warning before it (a warm line for a happy dream, a strange one for a neutral one, a cold, uneasy one for a nightmare), and the next morning the whole city reads in chat how it slept. A random dream from `lc_dream_every_night` gives way to the Administrator's pick.

**What tonight's dream may be** (the owner, 8 November: "the administrator (or NPC / automated administrator if no player) cannot select nightmares or propaganda only; preferably nightmares on bad sociostatus days, and a healthy mix of propaganda and neutral on other days, and happy dreams if no socio negative events happen"; `sv_livingcity_dreamrules.lua`). The day, from dawn to the dream, is watched: how low sociostability fell, and anything bad (sociostability below green, a curfew, a contraband sweep, a block raid, a lockdown, an outbreak, a rebel attack, a sweep of rebel ground). (`lc_dream_rules` 0 turns these rules off: she picks anything, as before.) A **Nightmare** only after a bad day (sociostability fell to `lc_dream_bad_socio`, red, at some point); a **Happy** dream (not propaganda) only after a clean day, nothing bad at all; a **propaganda** dream any night but never two nights running; a **Neutral** dream any night. Her tablet (and F2's Tonight's dream) says what the day was and greys out what isn't allowed tonight, with why; a pick the day no longer allows by dream time gives way to the city's own pick, and she's told. **With nobody playing the Administrator** (`lc_dream_auto` 1) the city picks a dream every night by the same rules: a Nightmare after a bad day, a Happy dream after a clean one, otherwise propaganda and Neutral by turns. A player Administrator who picks "No dream tonight" is obeyed.

**Who chooses tonight's dream, from 9 November** (the owner, 9 November: "the administrator (human player) can't pick which dream it is, they can only pick nightmare or propaganda as a category, and they can only pick once every few nights ... there have to be a neutral or happy dream in between propaganda and nightmare dreams ... the administrator ONLY has to show up/in/watch dreams that they picked the category for ... a round robin ... for the gamemode's automated AI/NPC administrator ... base it mostly around sociostability"; `sv_livingcity_dreamplan.lua`). This replaces the choosing above (the moods and the dreams stay as they are; `lc_dream_rules 0` lifts the waits below):
- **A player Administrator** chooses only a kind on her tablet's Tonight's dream: "A Nightmare tonight" or "A propaganda dream tonight", never which dream, and never Happy or Neutral or "no dream". One choice every 3 nights (`LC.DreamPickNights`: choose tonight, the next two nights after that), and never the night straight after a Nightmare or propaganda dream; the tablet says when she can next choose. She can take tonight's choice back until it's dreamt (nothing is spent until then). Every night she doesn't choose, the city dreams a Happy dream (after a calm day) or a Neutral one.
- **She only has to be in the dreams she chose.** On those nights lights out sends her to bed like everyone, and she's in it (starring in propaganda, an Enforcer in a Nightmare). On every other night lights out leaves her alone, and asleep she sleeps through the dream, unless she turns "Dreams I didn't choose" to join them on her tablet (it starts as skip); in one she joins, she can wake whenever she likes. She's also asked as she lies down (the owner, 9 November: "add the opt-in for when she actually goes to sleep"): in the evening or at night, with a dream still to come that she didn't choose, a small window asks "Dream it with the city, or sleep through it?" (her usual choice marked), with "Remember this" to make it her tablet's setting too; her answer holds for that night (`LC.DreamAdminAsk`, `LC.DreamAdminTonight`).
- **Nobody playing her** (`lc_dream_auto` 1): the city chooses every night by how the day went. A Civil Protection or Overwatch unit murdered by a citizen, rebel or vortigaunt: a Nightmare (a player's murder counts whole, an NPC's half, so it takes two of theirs, more on a full server as for sociostability). Sociostability at red (`lc_dream_bad_socio`) for at least half the day (`LC.DreamRedShare`): a Nightmare or propaganda, a coin toss. Always green, or just one dip to yellow: Happy. Anything else: Neutral. A murder makes the night Neutral at best, never Happy. After every Nightmare or propaganda night comes a Happy or Neutral one first, whoever chooses.
- **Round robin:** each kind (Happy, Neutral, Nightmare, propaganda) plays its dreams in turn, the next one each time, so nothing repeats while others wait. Remembered across restarts in `data/livingcity/dream_plan.json`.
- **As it comes:** about seven seconds before the dream, everyone about to dream it sees the screen fade to black and a line fade in about the day and what's coming, inferred, never a formula, chosen by why (`LC.DreamMoodTexts`): "Today the city was calm. You slip off into a happy dream." / "Today the city felt a bit unsettled. Your dreams may reflect that." / "The streets were filled with murder and crime. You expect a nightmare." / "Sirens wailed all day and the city never settled. Something dark waits behind your eyelids." / "The Administrator feels the citizens need motivation to better themselves and the city, and is keen to instruct you even in your sleep." / "The Administrator has decided the city needs reminding what disobedience costs." and more, several for each. Then it fades into the dream.

**Out of it in a dream.** A dreamer the ending takes out (drowned in the flood, caught by the mingebags, gone up in sparkles, taken off the bus) doesn't go dark: they watch the rest of the dream. Clicks cycle through the other dreamers still in it (never anyone awake, never the city), and Jump flies free, but only inside the dream zone's edge and under its top (without a zone, within `lc_dream_radius` of the point). When the dream ends they wake in their bed with everything back, like everyone else.

**No opting out** (the owner, 8 November: "make it so no one can opt out of dreams"; before, on 7 November, Happy and Neutral dreams could be opted out of). Everyone asleep dreams the night's dream, whatever its mood (outside a Combine-only zone, for a Nightmare; anyone Unbroken from beating the last Nightmare is spared that and propaganda). Nobody wakes themselves from any dream until they're out of it ("Wake up", C > Settings > Shared dreams, says so); once out of it (out of lives in a dream you play, taken out of an ending, or won) they may. Enforcers may wake any time. The only ways to be kept out of one: a stimulant shot (one at a time), the lab's foil hat, or a pulled chip. An old opt-out a player had set no longer counts, and `/dream off` just says there's no opting out. Admins can still wake everyone, and anyone's things can still be put back.

**Never a thin dream: stand-ins** (the owner, 8 November: "every dream needs a minimum of several human players, which are filled in by NPCs if human players are not available ... let's make it 6 for now"; `sv_livingcity_dreamstandins.lua`). Every dream has at least `lc_dream_min` (6) dreamers: when fewer real players are asleep for it, the city's own named people (made-up names if there aren't enough) dream it with them. Their names go up in chat as it begins ("Also dreaming: ..."), and they say something now and then. In a dream you play they run about near the start, now and then lose a life (a red flash, back to where they began), and most drop out before the end (a puff and a line in chat); they never take tokens, block anyone or take a win from a real player. The Loyalty Quiz seats its own, up to the same number (they answer in chat, and the rebel is dragged off). In an ending they stand about in the dream with the dreamers. A dream with nobody real asleep doesn't happen, as before. `lc_dream_min 0` turns stand-ins off.

**Enforcers: Civil Protection and the Administration** (the owner, 8 November: "the metrocops can be the enforcers and in dream they can punish bad dreamers ... not be dinged like citizens and rebels"; "happy dreams or neutral dreams don't need enforcers"; "1 [XP] per correct punishment"; `sv_livingcity_dreamenforcers.lua`, `cl_livingcity_dreamenforce.lua`). In a Happy or Neutral dream a unit or Administration member asleep dreams it like anyone. In a dream nobody can opt out of (a Nightmare, or a propaganda dream) they are **Enforcers**: nothing in it touches them (no lives, nothing lost or won, no loyalty points, no health off), they can wake whenever they like, and they never count towards `lc_dream_min` (a dream with only Enforcers asleep doesn't happen). In an ending (The Flood, They Return, Marrow, Standup) they watch it, free to fly about inside it. In a dream you play they walk the arena with a stun baton, and see its lines and music. Dreamers who misbehave glow red for them for 20 seconds: an offence in a propaganda dream (the ones it already counts), something disloyal said in a nightmare, or standing about for 20 seconds in The Patrol Maze, The Barrel Tower or The Crumbling Floor (not in the dreams where standing still is the point). The dreamer is told an Enforcer can see them. The baton (attack, within reach) on a glowing dreamer costs them a life, as being caught does (and the usual loyalty points in a propaganda dream), clears the glow, and earns the Enforcer `lc_dream_enforcer_xp` (1) XP, paid when they wake. On anyone not glowing nothing happens. The Administrator still stars in a propaganda dream herself (her tablet's "Star in propaganda dreams yourself"); with that off she's an Enforcer too. The warning before a forced dream tells an Enforcer what they'll be.

**Everyone in bed for it, and stimulants** (the owner, 8 November: "all human players get sleepy/exhausted at the same time and all need to sleep; the only way to avoid this is a stimulant, which cannot be used two days/dreams/nights in a row"; "if they buy a stimulant to stay awake, then they miss the dream until the next night"; `sv_livingcity_bedtime.lua`). Lights out (`survival-and-credit.md`, Sleep) puts every player in bed at the good night, the Administration included, and keeps them there until tonight's dream has gone by. A stimulant shot keeps you up while it lasts and lights out waits for it to wear off; by the time you've slept, the dream has gone by without you, and that's the end of it (there's no catching up on a missed dream). Only one shot, though: a second is refused until you've slept and dreamt properly (a dream dreamt through, to its end or until you were out of it; or a night's sleep on a night it didn't keep you up through). A unit on the night shift is the other exception. **The safeguard** (the owner, 8 November: "ALL dreams will end for all players after 5 minutes ... in case they get bugged/glitched"): any dream still going `lc_dream_max_minutes` (5) after it began is ended, everything it made removed, and everyone in it woken with everything put back. (The Patrol Maze runs exactly five minutes; an ending runs as long as its song, so a song over five minutes is cut short.)

**The foil hat and the chip** (the owner, 8 November: "the lab can make some sort of tin foil hat or maybe they can surgically remove the chip ... I want the rebels to have to endure dreams at least at the start ... an opt-out eventually by means of a roleplaying mechanic"; "500 for the chip surgery ... special one-time tools and codes"; "a 15% chance that a metrocop notices"). Both come from the Resistance lab, and only once you've lived through `lc_chip_dreams` (3) of the city's dreams (as a dreamer, not an Enforcer; counted per character). **The foil hat** (`foil_hat`, contraband; the lab makes it for scrap and cloth, or about 30 credits): put it on from your pockets and sleep in it, and it keeps the night's dream out only `lc_foil_chance` (a third) of the time (the owner, 8 November: "only stop dreaming 1/3 the time ... it's a hecking tin foil hat"; its description says the scientist mutters about field harmonics and thermodynamics, and that it is a tin foil hat). Whether it works is decided as it goes on and isn't shown. It's spent come the dream either way ("a static, a distant power that can't quite break in" when it works; "a distant power, an electric vibration, enter your mind and overpower your senses" when it doesn't, the owner's wording), or after an in-game day with no dream. **Pulling the chip** (`lc_chip_price`, 500 credits, thirty seconds under): no dreams ever again; you still go to bed at lights out, but a night's sleep takes at most `lc_chipless_sleep` (90) seconds and you get up on your own; but a unit who checks your papers or searches you notices "no implant signal" `lc_chip_notice` (15%) of the time, once a stop: a crime ("No implant signal (chip removed)"), and the papers check fails. A new character has a new chip.

**Nightmare dreams you play** (the owner, 7 November: nightmare dreams such as a maze where giant metro cops chase everyone, a climb with explosive barrels that explode if touched, "and any other similar ideas"; `sv_livingcity_dreamgames.lua`, `cl_livingcity_dreamgames.lua`). Four more dreams on the Administrator's tablet under Nightmare, next to the endings (their mood can be changed in `lc_dream_moods` like any ending: `maze`, `barrels`, `crumble`, `freeze`). Each builds its own arena at the dream point from plain props (Garry's Mod's building plates, Half-Life 2's officers, soldier, scanner and barrels), all of it removed when the dream ends; nothing of the map is used. Everything else is as for any dream: sleepers only, everything written down first, nothing counts, nobody really hurt, and they wake in their bed with everything they had. The screen shows the dream's name, what to do, lives and the time left. **The Patrol Maze** (5 minutes, 3 lives): a maze (its size fits the dream zone) with a glowing token in every corridor; two to four giant Civil Protection officers (1.7 times life size, taller than the walls) hunt the dreamers along the corridors; caught, a life goes and you're back at the start; the four golden tokens in the corners make them run for 8 seconds, and touching one then sends him back to the middle for 5; every token picked up and everyone still in it wins. **The Barrel Tower** (4 minutes, 3 lives): ramps side by side climbing up (2 to 5, as the headroom allows) to a red platform where a giant Overwatch soldier rolls explosive barrels down, more often as it goes on; touch one and it blows (anyone close by is caught too): a life gone, back to the bottom; jump over them; reach the top and win. **The Crumbling Floor** (3 minutes, 1 life): a floor of tiles (raised over a drop where there's room); a tile you stand on cracks (yellow, then red) and falls a second later, and a scanner overhead marks more, faster as it goes on; fall and you wake; the last one standing wins (alone: last 90 seconds). **Freeze, Citizen** (3 minutes, 3 strikes): cross a yard to a giant officer (3.2 times life size); move only while his back is turned; when he turns and shouts, anyone still moving after a moment's grace is seen and sent back; reach the red line by him and win. Out of lives (fallen, in The Crumbling Floor), or won: a window asks whether to wake now or watch the rest (flying about inside the arena, never past its edge) until it is over; watchers still see the time and the tokens; with nobody left in it, it ends and everyone wakes. When the time's up, whoever is still in it wakes as the nightmare fades. Waking from one (the owner, 8 November): won, Unbroken and a second wind for a day (plus a Vortigaunt's whisper for a citizen, a Vortigaunt's gift for the Resistance; never credits, standing or experience); still in it at the end, nothing either way; lost every life, 30 off your health until your next full night's sleep; see survival-and-credit.md. ## Sitting out a kind of dream, once earned (the owner, 10 November)

Nobody can opt out of the city's dreams until they've earned it, kind by kind:

- **Happy dreams:** after living through 2 Happy dreams (`lc_dream_skip_after`), anyone may sit them out.
- **Neutral dreams:** the same, after 2 Neutral dreams.
- **Nightmares and propaganda dreams:** only a citizen with Exemplary standing (Civil Standing `lc_dream_skip_standing`, 400), the city's favoured. Never the Resistance. Fall below it and they come again.
- **Choosing:** F2 > Me (More) > My dreams (also C > Settings > Shared dreams) lists each kind they've earned ("Happy: dreaming them (sit them out)"), or `/dream skip happy` (`neutral`, `nightmare`, `propaganda`; the same again to dream them again). Sitting a dream out, they simply sleep through it.
- **The note:** someone who has earned a kind but hasn't chosen to sit it out sees a small line under the day's mood as the screen goes black ("You've earned the right to sit out happy dreams: F2 > Me (More) > My dreams."), and the same in the "A dream is coming" notice.
- **How far along:** My dreams also says how many Happy and Neutral dreams they've lived through of the number needed ("Happy dreams lived through: 1 of 2."), for the kinds not earned yet.
- Dreams lived through are counted per kind for each character (not as an Enforcer, not a test from the editor); a new person starts again from none. `lc_dream_skip` 0: nobody sits anything out, as before.

## The stand-ins act (10 November; the owner: "enhancements, making custom animations, improving the ai or scripting of the scene")

The city's people who fill a dream up to `lc_dream_min` (`sv_livingcity_dreamstandins.lua`) used to jog at random near the start and flash red. Who they are, how many, when they lose a life and when they drop out are all as before, and they still never touch the game (no tokens, no blocking, no wins). What changed is how they behave and move, in every dream:

- **They keep to the players.** Each has their own pace, nerve and liking for company: most of the time one heads for a spot a few steps from one of the players still in it (on the same level, never far), otherwise about near the start.
- **They run from what hunts you.** When one of the dream's hunters (the giant officers, the dead) comes close, they bolt away from it at a panicked run, with a look back over the shoulder; the brave let it come nearer. Never more than a short way from where they began.
- **Between runs they stand the way the dream calls for:** kneeling to the Administrator (or her figure) in a propaganda dream, arms held out or hands together, now and then cheering her; cowering or glancing over a shoulder in a nightmare; waving at a nearby player, cheering or swaying in a happy one; looking about in a neutral one.
- **They see what happens to you.** A player near them loses a life: they flinch towards it and one says so by name ("They got Ada!"). A player is out: they cower. A player wins: all of them cheer ("Ada did it!").
- **Their own bad moments:** losing a life they cower a moment before daring to get up; just before dropping out they stagger and may get a last word in.
- **In an ending** (where they used to stand as statues) they turn slowly to whoever is nearest and, by the mood, wave, cheer, sway, glance about or cower. They stay on their spot: the ending has its own crowd and dance.
- **What they say** is half the time about the dream they're in (the maze, the tower, the crumbling floor, Freeze Citizen, the cheese), and they turn smoothly instead of snapping round.
- **Custom animation** (`cl_livingcity_dreamacting.lua`): what each is doing is sent to the dreamers' screens (NW2 `lc_dream_act` on the figure), where arms, head and back are moved every frame on top of the model's sequence: hands over the head and trembling, arms thrown up in a flinch, fists pumping, a waving forearm, a glance over each shoulder, arms held out to her, hands together and rocking. Where the model has its own sequence for the act only the small things are added. Every move eases into the next.

No new settings or commands. The poses use the bone directions the endings' dances found in play, but nobody has looked at these in game yet: say which look wrong and they get tuned.

## The Midnight Rally (10 November; the owner: "a Mario kart esque buggy race dream")

`rally`, a Happy dream you play, up to 200 seconds (`sv_livingcity_dreamrally.lua`, driving in `sh_livingcity_kart.lua`, the view in `cl_livingcity_dreamrally.lua`). An original race in that spirit: nothing of that game's names, characters, tracks or items.

- **The road:** a winding loop round the dream point on a dark floor, cones down both sides, two posts at the line, sized to the dream zone. The number of laps is worked out so the race is about the same length in a small dream as in a big one (`LC.RallyDistance`, 3 laps at least).
- **Driving:** there is no real vehicle. Each dreamer is hidden and a small buggy rides under them; forward accelerates, back brakes and then reverses, and the buggy comes round to where you look only so fast (less sharply at speed), so a corner taken flat out runs wide. No strafing, jumping or crouching. Off the road (inside or outside the cones) the top speed drops to under half. The view is from behind and above the buggy.
- **The start:** held on the grid for four seconds ("3, 2, 1, GO!").
- **Laps:** the road is twelve stretches; a lap counts only when they are driven in order, on the road or just beside it (no cutting across the middle, no circling it). Fall off the floor and you're put back on the road where you were.
- **Pickups:** six glowing crates on the road (back five seconds after being taken), one pickup carried at a time, **Jump (or attack) uses it:** Boost (half as fast again for 2.5 seconds, and the grass doesn't slow you), Melon (dropped behind; the next buggy over it spins out for 1.3 seconds), Horn blast (everyone within 900 units is slowed for 2.5 seconds).
- **Rival drivers:** this dream brings its own stand-ins. When fewer than `lc_dream_min` are asleep the city's named people drive the other buggies (seven at most): they follow the road, change lanes, slow for the tight parts, sometimes use a pickup, spin out on melons, and ease off when far ahead of every player. They can finish ahead of players.
- **The finish:** the first three buggies over the line win (a player who wins wakes as from any dream they've won); later finishers are told their place and wake smiling; when time is up the race is called. Nobody loses anything.
- **On screen:** "Lap 2 of 4   3rd of 6   Carrying: BOOST (Jump to use)", and "off the road", "Spinning out!", "Slowed!".
- **Music:** the owner's `rally` file in `sound/livingcity/dreams/` if there is one, else Half-Life 2's own. Late sleepers can join it like any dream.

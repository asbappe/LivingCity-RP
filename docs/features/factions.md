# Factions and ranks

**Switching faction from the factions menu (F4) keeps you where you're standing**: the new uniform and kit go on right there, instead of sending you back to a spawn point. (A new character, another of your characters, or an admin setting your faction still starts you at a spawn point.)

**Joining Civil Protection needs good standing**: 100 or more Civil Standing (`lc_cp_min_standing`), so a new character has to earn their way in with jobs and compliance first. Admins can join regardless, to test, and `lcf_setfaction <name> <faction>` sets anyone's faction directly.

| Faction | Ladder |
|---|---|
| Citizen | Citizen, Union Worker, Loyalist, Model Citizen |
| Resistance | Sympathizer, Member (pass for citizens), Fighter, Lieutenant, Commander (hunted outside rebel zones) |
| Civil Protection | Recruit, Metrocop, Division Leader, then the Overwatch: Combine Prison Guard, Combine Soldier, Elite Soldier. One Elite can be the **Overwatch Commander** (F4). |
| City Administrator | whitelist only |
| Infested | Zombie, Fast Zombie, Zombine (claws only: no guns; the Zombie and Zombine are no faster than a citizen; joining from F4 puts you in a nest; what they kill isn't murder, so no wanted and no response) |

**One career, from the records office to the Overwatch.** Civil Protection is a single ladder: XP takes you from Recruit (a baton, a desk) to Metrocop (a pistol) to Division Leader (an SMG, and an officer: sociostability, power banks, city-wide broadcasts). Past that are the Overwatch ranks, and they need the **transhuman conditioning** first: the Overwatch Candidate story comes to a Division Leader (F3), and at its end a technician offers it. Accept, and the Overwatch ranks open at once (your XP already counts: enough of it and you go straight to Combine Soldier or Elite). Decline, and you keep your name, and stay a Division Leader for good (XP still counts, the bar says why it stops). Overwatch ranks don't eat, don't get stopped, have their own voice lines, and their own board of Protocols alongside Civil Protection's. **The Overwatch Commander** (one at a time, from the Elite: F4) calls in an Overwatch squad by dropship to the landing zone nearest them, once every fifteen minutes (F1 > Unit tools, "Call in a squad"). Admins promoting someone (`!setrank`, `lcf_promote`) skip the story. **The Scanner Operator** (two at a time, any rank: F4) flies a city scanner from a Combine console, a data terminal, or anywhere in the Nexus (a map with none of those: anywhere), from F1 > Unit tools. Their reports earn XP (up to `lc_scanner_snitch_cap` every ten minutes), and a rebel they photograph goes out to every unit as a **BOLO** for half an hour; anyone else they catch is reported as usual, without one.

**Half-Life: Alyx-style Combine.** Install a Combine model pack and the Overwatch wear it, players in the Overwatch ranks and the city's own soldiers alike; nothing to set up. The first choice is Workshop 3449180606 (soldier, shotgunner, prison guard, shotgunner prison guard and Elite), the fallback Workshop 2041528484 (a soldier and the red prison guard). Which model is which comes from its name and path, and players who join are sent whichever of the two the server has mounted. Metrocops stay Half-Life 2's. `lc_combine_models_list` (admin) shows what was found; `lc_pm_<kind>` and `lc_npcmodel_<kind>` (soldier, shotgunner, guard, guard_shotgun, elite) name a model by hand; `lc_combine_models 0` keeps Half-Life 2's.

**Squads.** Units hunting the same person move as one squad (`lc_squads`): the one nearest the target leads, the rest keep with him (running to catch up, walking once close) and, once with him, stand watch facing open ground instead of a wall. Whoever sees the target fights; one hurt while running stops and fights back. Soldiers call it on the radio (contact, lost contact), and they share Half-Life 2's own squad, so its tactics (taking turns to shoot, flanking) work too. Soldiers holding a landing zone face out the same way.

**The kick.** Every Civil Protection rank can kick: G (`lc_kick_key`), or F1 > Kick (3). A unit's F1 (the owner, 7 November): 1 Voice lines, 2 **Unit tools** (everything a unit has), 3 Kick, 4 backup, 5 a BOLO, 6 their 10-20; what's in front of you, then the usual ones, from 7. Facing a locked door a kick would open, close up, a unit is reminded on screen how: "Locked. To kick it in: press G (or F1 > 5)", with their own kick key and wherever Kick is in F1 at the time (the owner, 2 November). An unlocked door bangs open, fast, away from you; a locked one gives on the second kick (`lc_kick_door_kicks`, 2) and an apartment's tenants are told; a Combine door opens; a prison door or a "locked" door doesn't give. Someone kicked is knocked back and hurt a little (`lc_kick_damage`, 12); something loose goes flying, and a boot breaks crates, planks and anything breakable the way a crowbar does. **The Overwatch's boots are heavier:** a Combine Soldier kicks an ordinary hinged door off its hinges and it falls over where it hung; an Elite sends it flying into the room, five to ten feet (never the same twice), and whoever it hits is **knocked out** (15 seconds of black, then the room swims; never a death or a close call, so kicking doors in during a curfew can't kill a block); a door falling over only leaves you dizzy; the Overwatch Commander, eight to fourteen (`lc_kick_door_power`, 1, scales the flight; 0 turns it off). Brush doors (a sliding warehouse door, a Combine shutter, a gate) don't budge for anyone's boot. **Rehanging it:** a Maintenance Technician presses E on the fallen door to lift it, carries it back to its frame (marked on screen) and presses E there (8 seconds; `lc_rehang_pay`, `lc_combine_models`, `lc_outfits`, `lc_people_heights`, `lc_citizen_tint`, `lc_squads`, 10 credits); the city's own maintenance crews come and do it if no player has. Failing all that it's back by itself after `lc_kick_door_back` seconds (600). Everyone sees the leg come up, on any model (the thigh and knee are bent in code), and in first person you see your own leg, so it's always the right boot. `lc_kick 0` turns it off.

**The Smuggler** (Resistance, two at a time, Member and up: F4) moves the cell's cargo: smuggling runs on their board (F3), collected from the quartermaster and taken to three drops in the city against the clock. No questions, no sender: they know it's guns and ammunition, and so does any unit whose search finds it. Better paid than a courier's run (6 a drop, 10 more for getting it all through, and XP). A courier who knowingly carries a hot parcel is a one-off; smuggling is the Resistance's trade.

**Special roles** (Hacker, Smuggler, Father Grigori, Free Vortigaunt, Director of Propaganda) are held on top of your rank, not instead of it: a Fighter who takes up the Hacker's role is still a Fighter, keeps earning XP toward Lieutenant, and carries the role's kit (the multitool) instead of the rank's while holding it; Join the faction again from F4 to give it up and go back to the rank's kit. F4 says so under each role, and your HUD shows both ("Hacker (Fighter)"). They come back with you: leave and rejoin and you're still the Hacker, as long as there's still a place. Nobody holds a place by being offline, so if the places filled up while you were away you're back to your rank, and told so; take it again from F4 when one opens. Whitelisted factions come back too, while you're still whitelisted.

**Powered vests (what HL2 calls suit power).** Nobody in the city wears an HEV suit: the armour number is the charge in a **powered vest**, kevlar plates with charge cells, and you hold only as much as your vest takes (the VEST bar bottom left, in place of HL2's SUIT). Combine units have one built into the uniform (their rank's armour is its capacity, and the Civil Protection Commissary tops it up). The Resistance's ranks come with one (Fighter 25, Lieutenant 50, Commander 75). Anyone else holds no charge at all until they put one on: the **powered vest** is made in the Resistance lab (or sold on the black market), holds `lc_vest_capacity` (75) and comes with 30 in it; Put on, from your pockets. It's lost when you die. Only these charge it (sleeping doesn't): suit batteries and chargers, the Commissary for units, **charge cells** (Charge your vest, from your pockets: 25; the Resistance lab makes them from 2 scrap and 2 chemicals, and a dead Combine unit drops one `lc_vest_cell_drop` (25%) of the time, a downed scanner twice as often), and **vortigaunts**: E on a freed one in rebel territory and it charges you (25, once every `lc_vort_charge_minutes`, 10, not by itself while you stand or sleep beside it), and a vortigaunt player's right click charges whoever they look at.

**Father Grigori, the man himself.** While no player is Father Grigori, the NPC keeps to a rebel territory with Annabelle (the largest at first), muttering scripture; every `lc_grigori_move_minutes` (12) or so he moves on to another of the Resistance's camps, when nobody's near enough to see him go (0: he stays put). E on him: **Ask for his blessing** (25 health, and blessed for five minutes: you take 15% less harm; once every five minutes each), **Ask him to preach** (he rants, and every Resistance member within earshot is emboldened for ten minutes: their shots hit the Combine 10% harder, and a little XP; once every ten minutes), or **Talk to him** (anyone). Only the Resistance get his blessing and his sermons; a unit who walks up gets a promise of Annabelle. Killed, he's back after `lc_grigori_respawn` minutes (10); `lc_grigori 0` turns him off.

**Father Grigori** (the city's own, in rebel territory) says something once a minute at most (`lc_grigori_talk_gap`, 60), and none of the engine's idle chatter. As a player, Father Grigori is a special Resistance role: his shotgun is Annabelle, and holds **two shells** at a time (the rest stay in his pockets); The Blessing heals, preaching draws Civil Protection, and he's hunted on sight outside rebel territory. Edit `gamemodes/livingcity/gamemode/modules/shared/sh_livingcity_factions.lua` to rename ranks, change XP, models and loadouts.

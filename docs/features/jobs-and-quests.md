# Jobs and quests (F4), and story chains

**Finding the objective.** The tracker on the right shows a compass for whatever you're after: a pointer relative to where you're facing, the distance in meters, a heading (ahead, behind left, ...) and whether it's on your level, a floor or more **UP**, or **DOWN**. It works even when the target is far enough away that the game isn't drawing it. Quests never send you to the infestation or onto Combine ground. Deliveries are made to **people**: the mail comes from the postal clerk and goes to named residents, Mrs. Kleiner hands you her parcel herself, luggage goes back to its owner, uniforms go to the unit at the checkpoint. Ration crates come from the truck driver and go to the dispenser attendant, and the Resistance's supply runs, the weapons ring and the proof-of-nerve parcel are handed over by and to contacts. Each person waits at a marked spot; walk up and press E.

Each faction has a board of radiant jobs built from your actual map: Directives, Protocols, Agenda, Operations, Assignments, Urges. Many have **choices with d20 checks** (Authority, Empathy, Guile, Nerve), and your choices build three traits that stick: **Loyalty, Sympathy, Corruption**.

Built for crowded servers: no job ever targets a player, boards are finite and scale with faction size, violent jobs are capped city-wide, and people can squad up.

**Directives for what's happening now.** The city's events bring their own jobs, posted two minutes before an event starts (units are briefed early even for a surprise sweep or raid) and as it begins, shown only while it's on, and withdrawn when it ends (whoever was already working one is paid; whoever hadn't started isn't held to it). Units get **Ration line supervision** (rations), **Civil status inspections** (sweeps: pat-downs), **Housing block inspection** (block raids: breach the door, get everyone down, search the place), **Curfew enforcement** and **Lockdown: hold the perimeter**; the Resistance get **Warn the block** (raids) and **Run supplies under curfew** (curfew and lockdown). When the infestation spreads or the Resistance take ground, **Containment** or **Retake the block** goes up at once for that patch, and every unit is told. These, and the chases (Apprehend, Disturbance), are squad jobs: up to 4 to 8 people work one together (the rest of the board takes `lc_quest_squad`, 3). Take it from the board and the others join you from theirs.

**The night shift** (Civil Protection and Overwatch, the owner, 8 November): most nights (`lc_nightshift_chance`, 0.85) the board has a **Night shift** directive from dusk, always offered first while it's up: walk six patrol stops through the night. Taking it excuses the unit from lights out that night, and a CP Commissary issues them one stimulant shot (`civil-protection-tools.md`).

## Story chains

Personal stories that unlock from what you've done, appear at the top of your board marked **Your Story**, and branch on your choices:

| Story | Who it finds |
|---|---|
| Internal Affairs | Cops who take bribes |
| The Man Inside | Kind, clean cops. Defect, or stay in uniform as a double agent with secret **Inside Work**, until **Suspicion** brings a **Mole Hunt** (caught: wanted, no surrender, Resistance for good; see the Resistance Insider below) |
| The Missing Unit | Experienced cops. Three different truths |
| The Overwatch Candidate | Division Leaders. Accept the conditioning and the Overwatch ranks open, or keep your name and go no higher |
| The Pamphleteer | Kind citizens. Can end with you joining the Resistance |
| The Informant | Loyal citizens. Includes tailing a neighbor: a suspicion meter fills while he can see you (faster the closer you are and when you run, slower crouched) and drains when you get out of sight, so stepping round a corner into view gives you time to duck back |
| Father Grigori's Flock | The chaplain. A secret mass, and a traitor among the faithful |

Admins can start any chain directly: `lcq_startchain <ia|inside|missing|candidate|pamphleteer|informant|flock> [player]`. `lcq_story <name>` shows someone's traits, counters and progress; `lcq_resetstory <name>` starts them over.

**Changes from the 7 November playtest.** A story quest ("Your story") waits until the character has done 2 to 4 ordinary jobs (drawn once and saved; existing characters too, from now); Union shifts, rehabilitation, Insider operations, the stalker's lot, the collar and chains already started are never held back, and a tip says when stories open. In the missing unit's story, the infestation leaves headcrab blood smeared up the wall and across the floor (it said "headcrab shells").

**The quest trail.** While you have a quest, amber chevrons on the ground lead to its objective, flowing toward it. The server works out the way over the paths drawn in the editor first, then the map's own nodegraph, then its navmesh if it has one, and straight there if none of them can (`sv_livingcity_guidance.lua`). It sends it again when the objective moves (someone you're following walks off), when you wander more than about 6 m (300 units) off it, and every 10 seconds while you're on the move; no more than 4 ways are worked out a second, so a full server stays cheap. On your screen, the points you've walked past drop away at once, so the trail always starts at your feet, and only the next 1400 units (about 25 m) are drawn, fading at the far end. It goes when you reach the objective, when the quest ends, and while Breen's water clouds your memory. C > Settings hides it for you (`lc_trail_show`, 1: the server stops sending it too); `lc_trail 0` turns it off for everyone.

**Changes from the 7 November playtest (part 2).**
- **Retake the block** (Civil Protection, the Overwatch): the job is to eliminate every rebel on the ground, not three of them (the objective counts down who's left, and the marker points to the nearest). From the moment the team arrives, no new rebels turn up on that ground or in the camp it grew from (nor a lookout post there) until the job is over and two minutes after (`LC.QuietRebels`). Holding it for the last 30 seconds, a wave of fighters (two plus one per member, up to six) comes from outside to take it back. The time limit is 12 minutes (was 10).
- **A squad for every violent job:** take a violent job and `lc_quest_allies` (2) of your own side go with you: units with pistols and SMGs for Civil Protection, soldiers for the Overwatch, fighters for the Resistance. Free ones nearby join first; else they're brought in out of sight. They follow you and fight what you fight (Civil Protection's and the Overwatch's also turn on the infested near them) until the job ends, and one who falls is replaced now and then. Joining a sweep and backing up a rebel raid bring their own fighters, so no squad there. Not for the infested's jobs.
- **The Missing Unit, part 2 ("What's left of him"):** no choice window in the middle of the infestation. After three kills a zombine in a Civil Protection uniform comes at you, and a notice on screen says it must be 6-12: kill him to end his suffering (the "mercy" ending), or leave the infestation and let a cleanup crew deal with him (the "left" ending). The same endings, decided by what you do.
- **Contraband sweep:** after the three stashes, the last one has something on it (a ration coupon, a work card) with a citizen's number: ask a citizen near the stashes who that is (E), track the suspect down (marked), then detain them, make them say who it was for (authority), take a bribe, or let them go, and arrest them with the baton. +30 XP for the trail; 15 minutes in all. With no prison or nobody about, the sweep ends after the stashes as before.
- **Get the lab running** (the Resistance): when the Combine wreck a lab, this job goes up on the Resistance's board. Find spare parts (marked), bring them to the lab, and stay at the bench for 20 seconds while you and the scientist put it back together. Only players can do it.

## The trail on the ground (11 November)

The chevrons keep their place on the ground and flow on at the same speed however fast you move (they were spaced from your own feet, so they ran with you). The way leaves out path points you could walk straight past: on your own level (70 units up or down at most), nothing solid in the way, floor under the line, up to 2200 units, as far as the objective itself. `lc_trail_straight` (1; 0 follows every path point as before).

## 14 November (the owner's 13 November playtest)

- **Kills count sooner.** A kill made on the way to a job's fight counts toward the kills its next stage asks for, and with them all made, arriving is done too. A kill by an NPC of your own side within 1500 units of you counts for your job (`LC.QuestAssist`).
- **"Repel the attack" ends with the attack:** over while you were there, or after a kill of yours, it is complete; over before you arrived, it is called off ("the attack was over before you got there"). A job template can say this with `over`.
- **The trail goes round walls into the other side's ground.** The planners keep units off rebel ground and everyone else off the Combine's; a job there used to get one straight line through every wall. Now a way is looked for again with no ground kept out of.
- **No distance in a job's title** ("to the north-east", not "240 metres north-east of you"): the marker shows it.
- **Headcrab hunt:** any of the Resistance's supply crates takes the meat.
- **The lab:** walk off while the scientist is making something and he works on, keeps it for you alone, and hands it over when you next talk to him; if a unit takes him, it is lost with him. Back from the cells, or the lab fixed, the blood is cleaned off him, the bench and the kit.

## 14 November (the owner's answers)

- **Repel the attack tracks every attacker:** "Put down the attackers (3 of 5 down)", counting all of the raid's fighters whoever brings them down; done when none is left standing, or when the attack is over.
- **The trail never draws a line through walls.** When either end isn't near a path node on its own floor (an objective on a crate or a balcony, you on a ledge), the way is planned between the path nodes nearest each end; with no way at all, no trail is drawn (the marker still shows where). `LC.TrailNoWay` counts those.

**The owner's 14 November notes.** The Union shift, Civil rehabilitation and Inside Work are marked **YOUR JOB** on the board (stories keep YOUR STORY). The board holds one job fewer (`1 + 0.5 per player`, 2 to 8) and an untaken job is up for 150 seconds (`LC.BoardPostSecs`, was 240). **Doctor the records** is the Hacker's job only. **Extraction**: the fugitive hides in the Combine's city, never on rebel ground, and is brought back to rebel territory. **Planned operations** are on the Resistance's F4 board as a card (join, back out, call it off; or each target with In 2, 5 or 10 minutes), F2 > Resistance > Operations still has them. **Get the lab running**: its parts are searched for by hand (hold E) in the reclamation yard's scrap and among the radio repair shop's sets (seen by a unit taking it, it's theft), and freed from a loudspeaker's utility box with the wiring game (seen, sabotage); a spare part hidden nearby stands in for any the map lacks; the bench is put back together with the wiring game too.

**Job boards in the world (settled 14 November).** The editor's "Fixture: job board" (`lc_jobboard`): whose it is follows where it stands (rebel ground: the Resistance's Operations; Combine ground: Civil Protection's Directives; else the Union's Assignments). Its glass lists up to five jobs up on that board; E opens your Jobs (F4) if it's your side's, and says whose it is if not. F4's Jobs stay for everyone, wherever they are. sv_livingcity_jobboards.lua.

**Hold E works whichever code hears it first (14 November, the robot players).** A thing a job puts down to be searched or worked on by hand (a flyer, the valve, the ration crates, the bucket, a bin) is answered by the search, never swallowed by the older hook for job items: before, five such steps started or not by the order two hooks ran in.

**No "stand here" steps (the owner, 14 November).** No job step is standing, staying or crouching in a spot for so many seconds: a minigame, a search by hand (`LC.QuestSearchStage`), work by hand (`LC.QuestHandsStage`: hold E on the thing), an interrogation, a chase, or something scripted instead (`CLAUDE.md`, house rule 9). **The exception (the owner, 14 November):** holding ground against attacks or waves of a counterattack, a unit's checkpoint or post, and holding a zone or territory you attacked or raided stay as they are. **What replaced the older ones:** the bin round (hold E on each bin), graffiti (hold E on the bucket), The count (hold E on each crate), letters (delivered as you reach the door), a streetlight (the wiring game), a leak (hold E on the valve), Break them out (the needle game on the cell lock), Free a vortigaunt (the wiring game on its collar), Address the citizens (speak to 3 citizens), Paper the walls (hold E on each flyer), Sabotage (wire the charge), Dead drop (the note is handed over as you arrive), Collect your ration (reach the front), Something you saw and Eyes and ears (no watching step), Curfew enforcement (the three stops only), The secret mass (bless each of the faithful, E on each), Leak the schedule (the dead drop game), A whisper in the vortessence and Read the Combine (the needle game), a stalker's Processing (done as you arrive). With `lc_jobgames 0` the old Union shifts still stand at their work, as that setting always did.
**Every kill counter can be finished (the owner, 14 November: "if there is a counter ... the gamemode needs to check and see if THREE SOLDIERS EXIST OR NOT. If they don't, then SPAWN THEM").** Every three seconds a kill counter counts the targets still standing that it would take (`WatchTargets`, sv_livingcity_quests.lua). Short for ten seconds, it brings the rest: the stage's `refill` (the landing ambush: off the dropship, or at the landing zone) or `Q.Spawns(stage, { role, pos, r, arg })` (round the place, out of sight if it can; with no `r`, from out of sight a walk away). Every kill counter has one. With nothing to bring and nothing to fight for 45 seconds, what was done is paid, or the job is called off. **New kill counters must have a `refill` or `Q.Spawns`.**

## 15 November (the owner's playtest)

**Address the citizens goes to her speech stand** (the owner: "I placed an administrator speech point and a crowd point, but the quest 'Address the citizens' is pointing me to a random room in a habitation block"). The job took any spot where people idle; it now marks the speech stand ("Point: Administrator's speech stand", the one nearest the crowd point when there is one, else the nearest), and as she gets there up to six citizens near by walk to the crowd point ("Administrator's speech crowd") and stand facing her while the job lasts, so there is someone to speak to. With no stand on the map it is as before (a plaza).

**Review the Civil Protection units is a line she walks down** (the owner: "this quest makes sense but is boring, nothing happens and there is no feedback, change in anything, it's just slop"). Taking it calls three units to fall in, a line a few steps in front of where she stands, facing her: the nearest free ones, fresh ones from out of sight for the rest. She walks down it, E on each (he is marked), sees how he looks in a line ("Boots polished, baton clean, eyes front", "Scuffed boots, fogged mask, slouching", "Split knuckles. Three complaints this week") and decides: **commend** him (he salutes, "Affirmative", and goes back to his beat; Civil Protection's strength +1, her loyalty), **reprimand** him (he hangs his head, "Roger that", back to his beat; authority +1), or **reassign** him (he is marched off out of sight and gone; authority +1, Civil Protection's strength -1). The answer that fits how he looks earns 5 XP more. One called away before she gets to him is skipped; the review is what she saw to. Those who came from out of sight go back out of it when it is over.

## 19 November: what each job pays, on the board

- **Every job says what it pays** (the owner: "every job should list the credits a player will receive if successful, or at least the range if dependent on success/metrics, and similarly the range of credits that will/can go into the resistance fund. this means all players feel responsible for helping grow the resistance fund"). The posting's own line, beside its experience: "30 XP, 10 credits", "35 XP, 13-25 credits, fund +15-18", or only "40 XP" for a job that pays no credits (never "0 credits"); the story parts and Union shifts too. Nothing else on the screen changed (the tracker never showed the experience, so it shows no pay either). A range is the least and the most it can pay this player: a job game pays by how well it went (each game says its own range beside its pay, `payRange`; the time bonuses of the parcel run and the radio bench are counted as if done at once, so their top is generous), a choice can pay or not (the Block Warden's report that holds up, 0 or 10), and a share of the fund's take depends on how many are on the job and, for someone saved, on the random take. Read from where the pay is given, so a change of pay changes the board: `LC.PostingPay(ply, posting)` (sv_livingcity_quests.lua) adds the economy's pay for any job (`LC.QuestCredits`, sv_livingcity_economy.lua, which also pays it), what a job pays of its own (`extraCredits` on its build or template: the gun run's sixty, the Administrator's job's 100 in all), its games (`LC.JobGamePay`: each step that starts one says which, `st.game`; games a choice may add are listed in `posting.games`), with Realistic survival's and the loyal's multipliers for the player the board is for, and the fund's part (`LC.ResistanceFundShares`). Not counted: what the world pays whether or not you are on a job (a unit's ration coupons, the convoy's pay for guarding a supply drop, the containment pay for the infested put down, a bounty).
- **The Resistance's jobs pay credits only when they bring something in or deliver something** (the owner: "Only jobs that bring in money: Raids, convoy hits, supply runs and rescues pay credits as now. Resistance jobs that bring in nothing (headcrab hunts, deliveries, scouting and the like) pay experience only. Citizen and Civil Protection jobs keep paying, since the Union pays them", and then: "please be logical: a delivery would pay credits. Delivery is literally an economic business sector in the real world; why would anyone deliver things for free"). Each job on the Resistance's board says which with `brings = true` or `brings = false` (no word: experience only). Paying: the supply runs (`res_supply`, `res_supplyrun`, `res_curfewrun`), the raids (`res_raidsupply`, `res_backupraid`, `res_dataheist`, `res_motherlode`, and since 19 November `res_paymaster`, robbing the Union paymaster: `docs/features/resistance-money.md`), the deliveries (`res_smuggle`, `res_deaddrop`, `cit_gunrun`), the rescues (`res_jailbreak`, `res_rescue`, `res_breakout`, `res_freevort`, `res_vort_kin`) and the job to kill the Administrator (its pay is the owner's own). Experience only: taking ground, flyers, sabotage, sounding out neighbours, doctoring the records, the ambushes, going to ground, the ministry and Father Grigori's flock, warning a block, the headcrab hunt, reading the Combine, fixing the lab. Experience only means none of the board's pay and nothing of its own, its job games included. The fund's takes and the shares of them are unchanged. Union shifts and community service are the Union's, whoever works them.
- **An admin's copy** (the owner: "can you please add a 'copy to clipboard' button that shows only for admins?"): on the board, beside its name, **Copy to clipboard**, drawn only when the server says the player is an admin. It puts on the clipboard, with a click and no other word, the pay of every job the server knows: the map and the date, then a line a job ("Resistance | Supply run | +35 XP | 10 credits", "| fund +5-15" where it feeds the fund), by side, then every story part and every kind of shift of each Union job. A job that can't be built just then says so, with what its template alone says. The server sends it only to an admin who asks, once in five seconds (`lcq_paylist`, `LC.JobPayList`). Tests: `Nov19_JobPayShownTests`; the client smoke test; the robot players compare what each job paid with what its posting said (`PAY` in their summary).

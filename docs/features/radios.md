# Radios

**Talking over your radio.** A radio only listens. There are two frequencies people transmit on: **the CP net** (every unit, issued) and **Freq 7** (a Freq 7 radio plus a **radio transmitter**, contraband: the black market, 70, or the Resistance lab, from a Union radio and 2 scrap). Nobody transmits on the Union band: it's the city's, for listening. Anyone who can transmit has a **Radio transmitter** next to their Hands: switch to it like any weapon (your hands are full: no gun at the same time). While it's up, everything you type in chat and everything you say on voice goes out on the frequency it's tuned to, and only someone standing right beside you (`lc_radio_overhear`, 110 units, about two metres) hears you directly; nobody else nearby hears a thing. Commands, `/me` and out of character chat work as usual. While it's up you hear a soft hiss of static (the radio's static recording, `radio/static...`), just you, as a reminder that you're on the air (`lc_radio_static_volume`, your own setting, 0.08; 0 turns it off). R, or switching to your hands or a gun, puts it away. **Right click switches frequency** for someone who can send on both, a unit carrying a Freq 7 radio and a transmitter (a Resistance Insider): the screen says which one you're on, so mind it. `!r <message>` sends one line without raising it.

**Your own radio.** A Freq 7 radio (every radio that isn't Civil Protection's) has **Listen** in your pockets: a small window with the same dial, Station and Static meters, and the same sound (static between stations, the station fading in through it), and anyone within about twenty feet hears it too. The Union band always sits at 96.4; **Freq 7 moves** (below), and a retuned radio starts on wherever it is now and follows it when it hops (a Union radio has to find it). Close the window and it keeps playing (Listen again, or `lc_radio`, brings it back); **Switch it off** stops it. It goes off if you lose the radio, die or are taken into custody. Playing Freq 7 out loud where a unit can see you (about 300 units) makes you wanted for listening to an illegal broadcast.

**Freq 7, the Resistance's pirate broadcast.** "Frequency 7" is a rule, not a number: the Resistance only broadcast where the digits add up to seven, and there are exactly seven such frequencies on the dial (**100.6, 101.5, 102.4, 103.3, 104.2, 105.1, 106.0**). It's one station: every radio on it hears the same thing at the same moment, a **voice line, then a quiet spell** (just static, about `lc_freq7_quiet` seconds, 120) so the band stays clear for people talking on it, then another voice line. No music by default: `lc_freq7_songs 1` puts songs back in the gaps. When something's coming for the Resistance, a **warning cuts in** at once, even mid-line (two minutes before a sweep, a block raid, curfew or a supply convoy; when Civil Protection sweep into rebel territory, a lockdown falls or an Overwatch dropship comes in), and a quiet spell follows it. **The Combine hunt it:** after a while on one frequency it's located and jammed (a harsh whine there, units told on their net), the broadcast goes dark for a minute or so, then comes back on another of the seven; a jammed frequency stays dead for `lc_freq7_jammed` minutes (8). How long it lasts depends on the city's mood: about `lc_freq7_hold` minutes (18) on green, 60% of that on yellow, 30% on red. **Jamming runs on city power:** in a blackout the hunt stops and Freq 7 holds where it is (a jam in progress lifts). A retuned (Resistance) radio follows each hop by itself; a Union radio has to be retuned by hand. Recordings go in the sound addon's `radio/` folder: `freq7_1.wav`... (voice lines), `freq7_song_<anything>.ogg` (songs: `.ogg` or `.wav`, so the server can tell how long they run; public domain recordings, such as anything published before 1926), `freq7_sweep1.wav`, `freq7_raid1.wav`, `freq7_curfew1.wav`, `freq7_supply1.wav`, `freq7_incursion1.wav`, `freq7_lockdown1.wav`, `freq7_dropship1.wav` (the warnings) and `jam1.wav` (the whine; a stock sound otherwise). `lc_freq7_status` (admin) says where it is and what's on.

The Resistance and Civil Protection have their own (on their boards):

- **Doctor the records** (Resistance Operations): get into a civil records terminal (or a Combine console). Stop the needle in the band four times; every hit makes it faster and the band narrower, and a miss loses one. A scanner comes over every few seconds (the panel warns you): touch the panel while it's overhead and it sees you, twice and the alarm goes and you're wanted. "Step away from it" closes the panel; E brings it back. Done clean, it wipes a wanted comrade's record.
- **Dead drop** (Resistance Operations): after the contact, three hiding places near the drop. E on one, then crouch beside it and keep still. Anyone who sees you at it (a unit from further away, a citizen up close) burns that place; try another.
- **Civil status inspections** (Civil Protection, during sweeps): a **pat-down**. E on a citizen stops them; pat them down a place at a time (coat, pockets, waistband, boots, bag). Watch their hands: what they do usually says where. Each empty place costs them nerve, and with none left they bolt. Then release, confiscate (once you've found it) or detain: right calls pay, detaining someone clean is docked, and letting contraband walk is a miss.
- **Housing block inspection** (Civil Protection, during block raids): a **breach**. E on the block's door sets a charge; it blows the door open. Then everyone on the floor (E on each) before they run out the back or burn the evidence, and search the furniture (E) for contraband.

Whatever a game wants you to look at is outlined: hiding places, residents, furniture to search, the body on the slab. If you close a game's panel, E (on whatever you're working on) brings it back.

`lc_jobgames 0` (editor panel, Survival and economy) puts the old shifts back.

Place each job's workplace from the editor's "Workplaces" section. Shifts pay Credits and Civil Standing, and many carry small choices: the mother with one ration card, the beaten man who begs you to write "fell down the stairs". The newer jobs are also where contraband comes from, if you want it: a dead man's papers from the morgue, a suitcase that's heavier than clothes, a pistol under the rubble. (Blank forms, uniforms and Freq 7 radios come from the black market, the dead, scavenging and the Resistance lab.)

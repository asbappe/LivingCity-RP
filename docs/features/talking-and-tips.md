# Talking, knowing people, going down, and tips

**Chat is in character and spoken out loud.** Ordinary chat only reaches people within `lc_chat_range` (350 units).
- `/y` yells further; `/w` whispers to whoever is right next to you.
- `/me` describes an action (`/me leans against the wall.`); `/it` describes the scene (`/it A siren starts up somewhere.`).
- `/roll` rolls 1-100 for everyone near.
- `//` or `/ooc` is out of character, server-wide, with a cooldown (`lc_ooc_cooldown`, admins exempt; `lc_ooc 0` turns it off). `/looc` is out of character, nearby only.
- `!` commands still go to the game as before. A mistyped `/` command is swallowed, not said out loud.
- **Voice** is proximity too: `lc_voice_range` (900 units, 0 for everyone hears everyone).
- `lc_ic_chat 0` turns all of this off.

**Recognition.** Strangers don't know your name: they hear and see your description instead ("A man in Union blue").
- `/chardesc <text>` sets your description (8 to 120 characters).
- `/recognize` introduces you to the person you're looking at; `/recognize all` to everyone in earshot. They hear you by name from then on, and nametags follow the same rules.
- Who knows whom is remembered across sessions. A new character (after a permanent death) starts with nobody known.
- Units are known by their designation while masked.

**Acts.** Poses you hold: `/sit`, `/kneel`, `/lie`, `/lean`, `/cower`, `/pose 1-4` (`/stand`, or just move, to get up), and `!handsup`. Gestures: `/wave`, `/salute`, `/bow`, `/nod`, `/shake`, `/point`, `/beckon`, `/halt`, `/cheer`, `/laugh`. All work with `!` too. While you hold a pose the camera pulls back so you can see yourself (`lc_act_thirdperson 0` keeps it first person). Each pose tries a list of animations, the GMod player ones first, then Half-Life 2's; a model with none of them still holds you still and says so. `lc_acts 0` turns them off.

**Going down instead of dying.** A killing blow puts a player on the ground, bleeding out, for `lc_downed_secs` (90 seconds).
- Downed players can only whisper.
- Press E on a downed body to help them up: 8 seconds bare-handed (back at 10 health), 4 with a bandage (back at 30).
- A unit pressing E on a wanted person cuffs them where they lie.
- Shooting or hitting a downed body finishes them. Damage of 300 or more (explosions and the like) kills outright.
- `!drag` while looking at a downed body drags it along; `!drag` again lets go.
- `!giveup` lets go if nobody is coming: your body stays where it lies (no second ragdoll), and the screen goes to the death view.
- Zombies and build mode skip all this. `lc_downed 0` turns it off.

## Tips as you play

So nobody has to read all this first, the game explains itself as it happens. The first time you run into something, a small card slides in on the right of the screen saying what it is and what you can do with it, using your own key bindings. Each stays up 14 to 28 seconds, depending on its length. The getting-started tips come half a minute apart; tips about what's around you come as you meet things. Each tip shows once per player and is remembered across sessions; a few (hungry, thirsty, hurt) come back now and then. One shows at a time, and anything urgent (you've gone down, you're wanted, a unit is stopping you) goes straight to the front.

- **What happens to you:** going down, getting back up, wanted, the escalation levels, stop and search, being told to stop running, custody, release, escape, curfew warnings, putting on a disguise, a blown cover, a conduct strike, a new character, promotions, your first ration, making a gun.
- **What happens around you:** rations, inspection sweeps, block raids, curfew, lockdown, rebel raids and CP sweeps, an outbreak, the city tipping into unrest or full compliance. Units and citizens are told different things.
- **What's near you:** someone down (if you can see them), a body, bins, water, sinks, stoves, vending machines, ration dispensers, the Commissary, the black market dealer, the quartermaster and his fighters, rebel supply crates and pots, power banks and turrets, graffiti, citizens, units, someone wanted (for units), strangers, birds, headcrabs, rebel ground, the infestation, your own block, a Combine-only zone, a gun out while in plain clothes, the Grenades weapon in your hand.
- **What's in your pockets:** seeds, a fishing line, a spray can, a lockpick, a bypass charge, a keycard, raw meat, empty or untreated water, clothes, a uniform, Resistance gear, papers, a blank form, a forged ration card, a radio, a permit, materials, a bandage, guns and ammo, a smuggled crate, grenades, tablets, a wedding ring.
- **How you're doing:** hungry, thirsty, parasites, hurt, standing below zero, or high enough to join Civil Protection.

`!hints` shows the last few again in chat, `!hints off` and `!hints on` turn them off and on for you, and `!hints reset` starts them over. `lc_hints 0` turns them off for the whole server. The tips themselves are a table at the top of `gamemodes/livingcity/gamemode/modules/server/sv_livingcity_hints.lua`, easy to reword or add to.

**Learning the city before the cards (the owner, 10 November: "integrate info to be more inferred and learned naturally through the environment and gamemode and city rather than just being told via text popups"; all of it the owner's go-ahead: "keep going with the other changes").** The tip cards stay as the fallback; the city teaches first (`sv_livingcity_learning.lua`):
- **Cards wait until you seem stuck.** A tip about something near you, something in your pockets, or how you're doing doesn't show the first time: the clock starts, and the card only comes if you meet it again `lc_hints_wait` seconds (240) later and still haven't learned it. What happens to you (going down, wanted, custody, curfew, events) is never held back. `lc_hints_wait 0` is cards straight away, as before.
- **Learning it any other way means no card:** doing it (searching a bin, collecting a ration, fishing, picking a lock, crafting at the lab, getting a room), asking the Citizen Information clerk or the old-timer (`city-people.md`), reading the orientation pamphlet (`civil-intake.md`) or a civil notice poster.
- **Your body tells you first** (`cl_livingcity_bodysigns.lua`): hungry, the colour drains from the world with waves of weakness (and your stomach, if there's a recording in `sound/livingcity/body/stomach*`); thirsty, a warm haze and hard breathing; badly hurt (below 35), red at the screen's edges and a pounding heart. Each player can turn it off in the full menu's Settings (`lc_body_signs`).
- **Machines answer in their own voice** (`sv_livingcity_machines.lua`): the ration dispenser and the vending machines say what's wrong out loud and low on the screen like anyone talking to you ("No ration card detected. Report for labor assignment."), instead of a card at the top; the full explanation stays in chat. Recordings in `sound/livingcity/machines/` (`VOICE_LINES.md` section 8). `lc_machine_voice 0` is cards as before.
- **Old hands who help newcomers:** someone new to the city (registered this session) gets a room, collects a first ration or finishes a first shift with someone nearby they know by name: that person earns `lc_mentor_xp` (25) XP, once per newcomer for each.
- **Civil notice posters** (editor: "Fixture: Civil notice poster", on a wall): their words fit whatever official thing is nearest (beside the dispenser, that labor earns rations; at the Commissary, that credits come from work; at the desks, that arrivals must register; by a sink or a water machine, that untreated water carries parasites; by the law board, to know the law; anywhere else, curfew). E reads it closely, which is learning it. **Your own artwork:** `garrysmod/materials/livingcity/posters/<kind>.png` (or `.jpg`; kinds `dispenser`, `commissary`, `intake`, `water`, `law`, `curfew`), 3:4 portrait, 768 x 1024 is plenty: the poster shows it instead of the drawn notice, and it's sent to players when the map starts. The brief for each picture is `POSTER_BRIEF.md` (the owner, 10 November: "describe them so I can have a stronger model make them").
- **The poster roll** (`weapon_lc_posters`, in the Director of Propaganda's loadout; anyone holding one can use it): left click hangs the chosen poster on the wall in front of you (within reach, on a wall, not over another poster; at most 24 hand-hung posters, `LC.PosterHangMax`), right click takes any poster down (a hand-hung one is removed; one the editor placed is only hidden, so the saved layout is untouched, and it is back when the map next starts), reload opens the roll to choose among all seventeen. Eleven posters are hung by hand only and are never picked by what is nearby: work, loyalty, informants, contraband, radio, illness, infestation, housing, vortigaunt, recruit, administrator. A hand-hung poster keeps the poster it was hung as, teaches no tip card, and stays up until the map changes (it is not saved). Pictures for all seventeen are in `materials/livingcity/posters/` (drawn by `_dev/posters/gen.js`). (`LC.HangPoster`, `LC.TakeDownPoster`, `LC.HungPosters`.)
- **The Resistance answers** (the owner, 10 November): a **spray can** used while looking at a poster paints over it instead of spraying the lambda on the wall (2.5 seconds, one can; seen by a unit or scanner it is Vandalism, unseen a Resistance member earns 3 XP, as for a tag). A painted poster shows its `<kind>_x` picture (all seventeen are drawn: the city's poster with words struck out and painted over, and a lambda) and E reads what was painted. It stays that way until someone with a roll left clicks on it (a fresh sheet goes over it) or takes it down. **Four paste-ups of their own** (`rebel_watch`, `rebel_leash`, `rebel_water`, `rebel_home`: sprayed and painted by hand on scrap paper, with a typed line under) are on the roll only in a Resistance member's hands, so they go up when the Resistance gets hold of a roll; nobody paints over those. (`LC.DefacePoster`, `LC.PosterAt`, hook `LivingCityPosterDefaced`.)
- **Paste-ups from the black market** (the owner, 10 November): the item `pasteup` ("Resistance paste-up", contraband; the dealer sells 3 for 20). Use it from your pockets while looking at a bare wall, up close: after 2.5 seconds one of the four Resistance paste-ups is on the wall (not the same one twice running). Anyone holding one can paste it. Seen by a unit or scanner it is Vandalism; unseen, a Resistance member earns 3 XP. **They are limited:** at most `lc_pasteup_max` (8) in the city, and one more takes the oldest down; never within 500 units of another (`LC.PasteUpGap`); not where a poster already hangs. They do not count against the Director's 24, last until the map changes, and the Director's roll takes them down. (`LC.PasteUp`, `LC.HangPoster(..., pasted)`.)
- The PA explains itself only the first few times (`city-events.md`).

**Changes from the 7 November playtest.** The cards at the top of the screen come one at a time, with a short gap and a "+N more" count (a warning goes first), taking turns with the tips on the right. A new character no longer gets the last one's wedding ring (rings still turn up in bins, the canal and on the dead).

**What you can do here (the panel on the right).** A small, faint panel on the right of the screen, under the quest tracker and above the tip cards, lists the things this city has built that the game never tells you: what the tool in your hands does (the Arrest Baton: E on a citizen stops them for a search, R searches, right click arrests, cuff and drag from F2 > Unit tools; the hoover, the sprayer, the radio, the multitool, the grenades, your hands, the Blessing, vortessence, the microphone, the Administrator's tablet, a crowbar, the Living City Editor), where you stand (rebel ground, the infestation, your own block, a Combine-only zone, the prison while you serve time), who you are (a unit's kick and F2 tools, the Resistance's gear, a citizen's hands up) and what this step of your quest wants (crouch in the spot, keep out of sight, walk them home, take the marked item, follow the trail). Nothing the game already teaches. Keys are your own bindings, looked up live. The tool in your hands comes first; when there's no room (a tip card showing, a long quest), the least useful part goes, and it never sits on top of the tracker or a tip. Bright for a few seconds when it changes, then faint. C > Settings turns it off for you (`lc_context_show`, 1). Other modules add lines with `LC_CL.AddContextHint` (`cl_livingcity_context.lua`); where you stand comes from the server as `lc_here` (`sv_livingcity_guidance.lua`).

**Team chat (the owner, 9 November: "a U chat that is team only", as in TF2 or Counter-Strike).** U, the game's own team chat key, reaches only your own side, wherever they are: Civil Protection, Overwatch and the Administrator together; the Resistance and free vortigaunts together; citizens; the infested; stalkers on their own. It shows as "(Team)" in chat. Ordinary chat (Y) is unchanged.

**Out of character from F1 (the owner, 10 November: "F1 > 6 > Out of character > 1. Nearby or 2. Server-wide").** F1's sixth item, right after My pockets, is the "Out of character" page: 1 Nearby (/looc), 2 Server-wide (//), each asking what to say. The two Talk entries are still there too.

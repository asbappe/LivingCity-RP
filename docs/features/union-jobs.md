# Union jobs, and escorts through Combine doors

**Everyone works.** A registered citizen without a job is assigned one by the Union, the post with the fewest hands (a notice says which, and where to report); ask for another on F3 > Jobs. The Resistance aren't assigned (one can still take a job, passing as a citizen). `lc_assign_work 0` leaves it to people to choose. Changing jobs ends the old job's shift, so the new job's can be taken. Citizens (and Resistance members who still pass for citizens) choose a job on their board. Promotions never take your tools away. Each job posts a steady stream of **Union shifts** at its workplaces, or in the streets if none are marked:

| # | Job | Shifts |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sanitation Worker | Litter sweeps, bin rounds (at the map's own bins), graffiti removal |
| 2 | Ration Production | The sealing line (split packs to the reject bin), supply runs, the count against the register, the counter (forged ration cards). Rations are made, not cooked |
| 3 | Clinic Medic (gets a medkit) | Clinic rounds, house calls |
| 4 | Factory Worker | Assembly quotas, the loading dock, a foreman who skims |
| 5 | Courier | Mail rounds, sealed envelopes, parcel runs |
| 6 | Maintenance Technician | Streetlights (or fuse boxes on a map with none), faulty terminals |
| 7 | Intake Processor | New arrivals to walk home, luggage, ticket checks |
| 8 | Records Clerk | Filing (*a game; open while `lc_jobgames` is on*) |
| 9 | Uniform Laundry | Sorting (*a game; open while `lc_jobgames` is on*) |
| 10 | Mortician | Hauling the dead to the incinerator, clearing the streets after trouble, searching the dead before the fire, bagging dead headcrabs |
| 11 | Reclamation Crew | Scrap runs, rubble that sometimes hides a weapons cache |
| 12 | Radio Repair | Repairs (*a game; open while `lc_jobgames` is on*) |

**Games only:** every Records Clerk, Uniform Laundry and Radio Repair shift is one of the games below (the city's own people still work there too). With `lc_jobgames 0` those three are closed: anyone who had one is told the post has closed, and picks another.

**Work you actually do.** Most jobs' shifts are small games played with your hands in the world, and pay on top of the shift for how well you do them:

- **Street sweep** (Sanitation): a wheeled cart waits at the depot. E grabs its handle and you push it; E again lets go. Rubbish lies about as real things (cans, bottles, scrap metal, chemical jugs, newspaper), outlined as you get near: pick a piece up (E) and throw it in (left click). A credit a piece, another for a long throw, a bonus for a quick round. Some pieces hide something (tokens, a ration, a blank form, ammunition, a note). Push the full cart back to the depot.
- **On the line** (Factory): a conveyor carries parts past you. The defective ones are rust-red and spark: grab them and throw them in the scrap bin before they reach the end, and let the good ones through. Twenty parts, faster as it goes; pay for every right call, docked for every wrong one, a bonus for a clean shift. One part rattles: R while you hold it slips what's inside into your pocket (a unit who sees calls it theft). Place the factory's conveyor with the editor ("Fixture: factory conveyor"); without one, a line is set up where you report for the shift. Every part is one you can grab, set square on the belt and kept from spinning; the city's own workers at a point beside the belt face it.

**A ration break.** Rations come out while you're on a shift or a job: F1 (or the C menu) > Take a ration break (`!rationbreak`) and the job waits, its clock stopped, till you've had your ration (or five minutes, or `!back`).
- **Papers check** (Intake Processor, the job once called Station Porter): travelers walk up from the platform (or from out of sight) one at a time, and walk off again out of sight. E on one opens their papers beside today's rules: their photo (look at them: is it them?), name, district, expiry, stamps, and the seal (it must read OCS). Approve, deny, or call a unit. Mistakes earn a citation and are docked; a forgery handed to a unit pays well, and one waved through is between you and the Resistance (unless there's an audit).

- **Rewiring** (Maintenance): every streetlight, fuse box and faulty terminal opens a board of scrambled circuit tiles (bends, straights and tees; 5 by 4, a terminal 6 by 5). Click a tile to turn it a quarter (right-click turns it back) until the current runs from the feed on the left to the lamp on the right; every tile the current reaches lights up, so you can see how far it gets. Only one winding way through works, and the odd tiles round it lead nowhere. **Live sparks** jump about the board (two, three on a terminal): turn a tile while it's sparking and it bites (a shock, and 4 seconds off the clock), so time your clicks. 55 seconds (75 for a terminal); too slow and the breaker trips and the board scrambles again, for less pay. Done in no more turns than it needs plus two ("par", on the board) is a bonus of 2 credits; during a blackout it pays double.
- **Triage** (Clinic Medic): three or four patients come in at once, bleeding out at different rates (a bad bleed, a stab wound, a stunstick beating, burns, a broken arm). A bar over each head shows who's worst; the medkit (primary fire) brings them up, and the wound takes them back down. Get them on their feet before they're gone. Paid for every one who walks out, docked for every one lost, and a bonus when nobody is. The beaten man asks you not to write down what did it.
- **Rubble** (Reclamation Crew): each rubble pile is a heap of concrete blocks to break up with a crowbar (lent for the shift if you haven't one, and no unit books you for carrying it). Each block takes a few blows and leaves scrap where it broke; now and then something else. The weapons cache under the second pile is still there.
- **Parcel run** (Courier): three parcels against the clock, each door marked on your screen. They aren't letters. **First, the sender hands it over**, in a small window, and the clock doesn't start till you decide. Most say what's in it (and it's harmless); some won't ("Don't ask. Just get it there."); some own up to contraband and offer 8 credits extra when it gets there. You can **take it**, **hand it back** (no parcel, no pay), **report the sender** to Civil Protection (only one who wouldn't say or owned up: Civil Standing, more if there really was contraband in it), or **open it** to see for yourself (Civil Standing lost, and 1 credit a delivery instead of 3 if you deliver it; after that you can still take it, hand it back or report it). Close the window and E brings it back. A unit who gets a good look at you (a meter on the panel fills while one can see you; crouching keeps it low) calls you over: stand still for five seconds while he searches, or run. Run, and you're wanted; stand, and most of the time he hands it back, but if there's something in it, it's seized and so are you. Paid per parcel, more for time to spare, a bonus for never being stopped.
- **Searching the dead** (Mortician): three bodies for the incinerator, one at a time on the slab. E on one shows its pockets: search each, then **log** what you find (paid, and contraband logged earns standing), **keep** it (it's yours: dead men's papers, a ring, ration coupons, rounds, a data chip sewn into a hem), or set it aside **for the family**. The overseer looks in now and then (the panel says when): keep something while he's watching and it goes in the tray, and in his notebook. Then into the fire.
- **Sorting** (Uniform Laundry): Civil Protection uniforms, one at a time, by their unit tag into the bins for its division (each bin takes two); bloodstained ones to the incident wash. Twelve, against the clock. Units leave things in their pockets: pocket it (the supervisor sometimes counts) or turn it in.
- **Filing** (Records Clerk): ten forms, each beside what the register says. Name, number and status all match: into the drawer for the first two digits of the citizen number (37-412 in 25-49). Anything different (a misspelt name, two digits swapped, another status) goes in Discrepancy. The window says so at the top, and after each form it says whether it was right and, if not, why and where it should have gone. Stealing a blank identity form from the cabinet (one a shift) is a red button marked as the crime it is: a unit who sees it makes you wanted and costs you standing.
- **Repairs** (Radio Repair): four sets off the shelf, two minutes each (`lc_radio_set_secs`, 120; the time left ticks down on the bench, red near the end). Finishing a set (or running out of time on one) shows it a moment before the next comes off the shelf: clean, or back on the shelf unfixed. A set is fixed when it's on its station **and** it comes through clean, and that's the repair: three **trimmer screws** inside it, left somewhere wrong, that you turn with the screwdriver (hold the mouse on one and go round it, clockwise to turn it up, anticlockwise to turn it down, like a real screw; or the mouse wheel for the last little bit) and set by ear. The **volume trimmer** sets how loud the station comes through, the **squelch** how much hiss is left under it, and the **alignment** how sharp it is (off, and the station is weak and smeared across the dial, with more static bleeding in). Find the station on the dial first, then work the screws until the hiss drops away and the station is full; two meters, Station and Static, show what you're hearing. **Test it** when it sounds right: on the station but still off, the bench tells you which screw it sounds like (and costs you a couple of seconds). Now and then the clearest thing on the band is **Freq 7**: report it (standing), write it down where nobody will look, or tune past it (the dial stays where you left it). **Written down**, it's a **Freq 7 sheet** in your pockets: Read it for the rule and the seven frequencies (and where you heard it). It's contraband, so pass it on to someone who wants it, or burn it before a search. The city's own repairers (NPCs on the job) work on a radio sitting on each bench point: put the Radio Repair work points on a table or a bench, and the radio sits on top, with the repairer standing at it. **It sounds like a radio:** static plays the whole time, and as the dial nears a station the station fades in through it (they overlap; the hiss never quite goes until you're on it). Anyone standing near the set hears it as you tune it, Freq 7 included: full volume beside it, quieter as they step back, and nothing past about twenty feet (240 units). The recordings are yours to make, in the same folders as the announcements (below): `radio/static/` (the hiss), `radio/union/` (an ordinary station; with none, the Administrator's `ambient/` recordings) and `radio/freq7/` (the pirate broadcast; its `.txt` is the line shown on the dial). Long files that loop cleanly work best.

### Escorts through Combine doors

A Union worker whose job is behind Combine doors (a generator in the nexus, a fuse box in the precinct) asks for a **Civil Protection escort**: the actions menu (Getting by > Ask for a Civil Protection escort), or `!escort`. You have to be employed and on a job (a shift or assignment you've accepted). The nearest free unit comes for you, or a player unit takes it on from their own menu (Civil Protection > Escort requests, or "Escort them" when they look at you).

With your escort beside you, **Combine doors open for you** (E: the unit buzzes you through), forcefields let you by, and you aren't trespassing in Combine-only areas. The unit follows you about and opens the doors it walks through. Your HUD shows who's escorting you.

The unit watches the doors it opens. If **someone slips through behind you** (a rebel, anyone with no business in there), it breaks off and goes after them, and they're wanted for unauthorized access. If **a prisoner gets out** through a door it opened, it goes and marches them back to their cell. Either way it radios that you're unattended, and the nearest free unit comes to take over (you're still covered while it comes, `lc_escort_handover` seconds). It goes on your file and costs you standing; **twice on one job and dispatch won't send anyone else with you** for `lc_escort_ban_minutes` (10). A unit called away by anything else (a crime nearby, being shot at) hands over the same way. Walk away from your escort for fifteen seconds and you're on your own (trespassing, if you're inside).

**When your job's done**, your escort walks you out to the **release point** (the way out of the prison, outside the Combine area) and leaves you there. With no release point on the map, they see you out of the Combine area; if you never went through a Combine door, they just go back to their beat. Player units can hand an escort back to dispatch from their menu.

(Half-Life 2's own "follow me" squads only work for rebels, not Civil Protection, so units follow the city's own way, the same as a Resistance fighter does.) `lc_escorts 0` turns escorts off; `lc_escort_range` (6000) is how far a unit is sent from.

**Changes from the 7 November playtest.**
- **One job change every 15 minutes** (the owner: "can we make it so a person can't switch union jobs more than once every 15 minutes?"): `lc_job_cooldown` is 900 (was 120); a server still on 120 is moved to 900. The refusal says how many minutes are left.
- **Tools come back** after a rejoin or prison for anyone still in the job; becoming a unit or an anticitizen revokes the job (see Infestation Cleanup for what is kept).
- **Work clothes stay on for the whole shift:** a sanitation worker (and the mortuary and scrap crews) only changes back at bedtime or on leaving, not after every bin.
- **The hoover** is drawn about 2 feet further from your face, and the airboat's fan motor runs under its own sounds while vacuuming.
- **Weeds** sit on the floor under their spot (some floated 2 to 3 feet up).
- **The incinerator's fire** shows from the start (each has its own flames; before, it only lit after being moved).
- **Both Commissaries** have a floating sign in the Civil Intake style: "UNION COMMISSARY / Goods for citizens, by Civil Standing. Press E" and "CP COMMISSARY / Units: your issue is here. Press E".
